7 Hidden Gems Most Tourists Miss

Millions visit London each year, but almost none of them find these extraordinary spots. Here are seven places that will make your trip unforgettable.

1

God's Own Junkyard

A dazzling warehouse in Walthamstow filled floor to ceiling with vintage neon signs, movie props, and light installations. It was the life's work of neon artist Chris Bracey and is now maintained by his family. Walking through feels like stepping into a Blade Runner fever dream — every wall, floor, and ceiling pulsates with colour.

Walthamstow, E17 Free entry (donations welcome) Open Fri–Sun only

How to get there: Take the Victoria Line to Walthamstow Central, then a 15-minute walk or short bus ride to Unit 12, Ravenswood Industrial Estate.

Best time: Saturday afternoon. The on-site Rolling Scones cafe serves excellent coffee and cake. Arrive by 1 PM to avoid crowds.

2

Leadenhall Market

A stunning Victorian covered market in the heart of the City of London, dating back to the 14th century. Its ornate cobalt-blue and cream ironwork was designed by Horace Jones in 1881. Harry Potter fans will recognise it as the entrance to Diagon Alley. During the week it buzzes with City workers; on weekends it is blissfully quiet.

City of London, EC3 Free to visit Best Mon–Fri for atmosphere

How to get there: Bank or Monument station, 3-minute walk. Look for the entrance on Gracechurch Street.

Best time: Weekday lunchtime for the full bustling experience. Thursday or Friday after 5 PM for a drink in one of the market pubs.

3

Postman's Park

A small, peaceful garden near St Paul's Cathedral with one of London's most moving memorials: the Watts Memorial to Heroic Self-Sacrifice. A long wall of hand-painted ceramic tiles commemorates ordinary people who died saving the lives of others. Each tile tells a brief, heartbreaking story. It is quiet, beautiful, and profoundly human.

Near St Paul's, EC1 Free Open daily dawn to dusk

How to get there: St Paul's or Barbican station, 5-minute walk. Enter from King Edward Street.

Best time: Morning or early afternoon for solitude. Bring a coffee and sit on one of the benches beneath the memorial.

4

Little Venice

Where the Grand Union and Regent's Canals meet, a tree-lined waterway neighbourhood of colourful narrowboats, waterside cafes, and peaceful towpaths. It feels impossibly serene for a spot just minutes from Paddington station. In summer, you can take a canal boat from here all the way to Camden Lock, passing through Regent's Park and London Zoo.

Maida Vale, W9 Free to walk Canal boat £12–15

How to get there: Warwick Avenue station (Bakerloo Line), 2-minute walk to the canal.

Best time: Spring or summer mornings. The Waterside Cafe is lovely for brunch. The canal boat to Camden takes about 45 minutes and is a unique way to see the city.

5

Neal's Yard

A tiny, hidden courtyard in Covent Garden exploding with colour. Every wall is painted in bright blues, yellows, greens, and pinks, draped with hanging plants and fairy lights. It houses independent restaurants, a cheese shop, and the original Neal's Yard Remedies store. It is one of the most photographed spots in London among those who know it exists.

Covent Garden, WC2 Free to visit Open daily

How to get there: Covent Garden station, 3-minute walk. Enter through the narrow passage on Shorts Gardens — easy to miss if you are not looking.

Best time: Late morning before the lunch rush. Try Homeslice for pizza or Jacob the Angel for coffee.

6

Kyoto Garden, Holland Park

A tranquil Japanese garden tucked inside Holland Park, complete with a waterfall, koi pond, stone lanterns, and immaculate landscaping. It was donated by the Chamber of Commerce of Kyoto in 1991 and is maintained to exacting standards. Peacocks roam the surrounding park, occasionally wandering through the garden itself.

Holland Park, W8 Free Open daily 7:30 AM to dusk

How to get there: Holland Park station (Central Line), 5-minute walk. Enter via the main park entrance on Abbotsbury Road.

Best time: Early morning or late afternoon for the best light on the waterfall. Autumn is spectacular when the maples turn red and gold.

7

Crossbones Graveyard

A medieval unconsecrated burial ground in Southwark, once used for "single women" (a euphemism for sex workers who were denied church burial) and later for London's poorest. The gates are now covered in ribbons, flowers, and mementos left by visitors. A small community garden has been created on part of the site. It is atmospheric, moving, and completely unlike anything else in London.

Borough, SE1 Free (garden open Wed 12–3 PM) Gates viewable anytime

How to get there: Borough station (Northern Line), 2-minute walk. Located on Redcross Way, just south of the Shard.

Best time: Wednesday lunchtime when the garden is open and volunteers share the history. The decorated gates can be viewed any time of day.

Planning Your Hidden Gems Day

You can combine several of these spots into a single day trip. Here are two suggested routes:

Route A: East London Hidden Gems (Half Day)

Start at Leadenhall Market mid-morning for coffee in the Victorian arcade. Walk 15 minutes south to Postman's Park for a quiet moment at the memorial. Cross the river via Millennium Bridge to Southwark, then walk east along the Thames Path to Crossbones Graveyard near Borough station. End with lunch at Borough Market, just 2 minutes away.

Route B: West London Hidden Gems (Half Day)

Start at Little Venice for a canal-side brunch. Take the canal boat to Camden (45 min) or walk to Paddington and catch the Tube to Holland Park for the Kyoto Garden. Then take the Central Line to Tottenham Court Road and walk 5 minutes to Neal's Yard in Covent Garden for lunch and photos.

Best days: Saturday is ideal for Route A (Crossbones garden is open Wednesdays, but gates are viewable daily). Route B works any day of the week. God's Own Junkyard is only open Friday to Sunday, so plan that as a standalone trip to Walthamstow.

More Hidden Gems Worth Knowing

If these seven spots have whetted your appetite, here are a few more under-the-radar recommendations:

  • Daunt Books, Marylebone — An Edwardian bookshop with a stunning stained-glass skylight. Travel books organised by country.
  • The Barbican Conservatory — A tropical greenhouse hidden inside London's brutalist arts centre. Free, open Sundays and some weekdays.
  • Dennis Severs' House, Spitalfields — An immersive 18th-century time capsule. Silent candlelit visits on Monday evenings are unforgettable.
  • The Hunterian Museum, Lincoln's Inn Fields — A beautifully weird medical collection inside the Royal College of Surgeons. Free entry.
  • Pergola on the Roof, Television Centre — A rooftop bar and kitchen in White City with panoramic west London views.
