30 Most Instagrammable Spots in London

From pastel Notting Hill houses to ancient ruined churches, these are London's most photogenic locations — with practical tips to help you get the perfect shot.

Photography Tips for London

Best light: Golden hour (the hour after sunrise and before sunset) gives the warmest, most flattering light. In summer, sunrise is around 5 am and sunset around 9 pm. In winter, sunrise is around 8 am and sunset around 4 pm — the low winter sun creates dramatic shadows.

Avoiding crowds: Early morning (before 8 am) is the secret to crowd-free photos at popular spots. Sunday mornings are quieter than Saturday. Rainy days clear crowds from outdoor spots.

Weather: Overcast skies actually produce soft, even light that is excellent for portraits and street photography. Do not wait for sunshine — London is photogenic in all weather.

Iconic Landmarks

1. Tower Bridge

Best angle: From the south bank, slightly east of the bridge (near St Katharine Docks) for a classic side view. Or from the north bank near the Tower of London for a wider composition.

Best time: Sunrise for golden light and no crowds. Blue hour (just before sunrise) for dramatic city-lit shots. Check the Tower Bridge website for bridge-lift times — photographing the bridge raised is spectacular.

2. Big Ben & Houses of Parliament

Best angle: From Westminster Bridge for the classic postcard shot. From the south bank near St Thomas' Hospital for a wider composition including the river. From the Victoria Embankment for a three-quarter view.

Best time: Early morning from Westminster Bridge when it is empty. Evening for the golden-lit clock face. The scaffolding-free view after the recent restoration is stunning.

3. Millennium Bridge with St Paul's

Best angle: From the Tate Modern end of the bridge, looking north toward St Paul's Cathedral. The bridge frames the cathedral dome perfectly.

Best time: Sunrise when the bridge is nearly empty. Midweek early morning. The long, straight perspective of the bridge needs minimal people for the best shot.

4. The Shard from Bermondsey

Best angle: From the junction of Bermondsey Street and Leathermarket Street, where The Shard looms dramatically above the Victorian rooftops. From More London near City Hall for the full riverside composition.

Best time: Blue hour or golden hour. The glass facade reflects sunset colours beautifully.

5. Buckingham Palace

Best angle: From the Queen Victoria Memorial for the straight-on classic view. From The Mall with the tree-lined avenue leading to the palace.

Best time: Very early morning (before 7 am) when the forecourt is empty. During Changing of the Guard (11 am, check schedule) for ceremonial shots — but it is very crowded.

Colourful Streets & Hidden Gems

6. Neal's Yard, Covent Garden

Location: Off Shorts Gardens, WC2H • Nearest Tube: Covent Garden

A tiny, hidden courtyard painted in brilliant blues, purples, yellows, and greens. Every surface is colourful, with hanging flower baskets and fairy lights adding to the whimsy. The best photo spot is from the narrow entrance looking into the courtyard.

Best time: Before 9 am on a weekday for empty shots. It is tiny, so even a few people fill the frame. Overcast days make the colours pop without harsh shadows.

7. Notting Hill Pastel Houses

Location: Lancaster Road, Westbourne Park Road, and surrounding streets, W11 • Nearest Tube: Ladbroke Grove

The pastel-painted Victorian terraces of Notting Hill are some of London's most photographed houses. The most concentrated rows are along Lancaster Road (pale pinks, blues, and yellows) and Westbourne Park Road. Chepstow Villas and Denbigh Terrace also have beautiful examples.

Best time: Early morning (before 8 am) on a sunny day when the light is soft and the streets are empty. These are residential streets — be respectful of homeowners and avoid blocking driveways.

8. Peggy Porschen, Belgravia

Location: 116 Ebury Street, SW1W 9QQ • Nearest Tube: Victoria

London's most photographed cafe — the pink facade covered in seasonal flowers (cherry blossoms in spring, roses in summer, autumn foliage) is a constant Instagram favourite. The cakes inside are beautiful and genuinely delicious.

Best time: Before opening time (around 8 am) for a crowd-free exterior shot. The flower display changes seasonally — spring cherry blossoms are the most popular.

9. Columbia Road, Hackney

Location: Columbia Road, E2 7RG • Nearest Tube: Hoxton (Overground)

On Sundays during the flower market, this East London street is a riot of colour and life. Buckets of flowers, barrow boys shouting prices, and the prettiest independent shop fronts in London. The colourful doors and windows of the shops are photogenic any day.

Best time: Sunday 8–9 am for the market in full swing without the worst crowds. Midweek for the shopfronts without the market.

10. St Dunstan in the East

Location: St Dunstan's Hill, EC3R 5DD • Nearest Tube: Monument

A bombed-out medieval church ruin reclaimed by nature — ivy and climbing plants cover the Gothic arches and window frames, creating a secret garden in the heart of the City. It is one of London's most magical and unexpected spots.

Best time: Early morning for soft light filtering through the arches. Midweek to avoid the Instagram crowds who have discovered it. Spring and summer when the greenery is lush.

11. Leadenhall Market

Location: Gracechurch Street, EC3V 1LT • Nearest Tube: Bank

The ornate painted Victorian ironwork — cobalt blue, deep red, and cream — under the glass-roofed arcade is stunning in photographs. Used as Diagon Alley in Harry Potter. The long perspective shots down the arcade are the most striking.

Best time: Early morning on a weekday (before 8 am) for empty shots. Lunchtime for atmospheric shots with City workers.

Skyline Views

12. Sky Garden

Location: 20 Fenchurch Street, EC3M 3BY • Nearest Tube: Monument

Free entry (must pre-book) to the 35th-floor garden with 360-degree views. The tropical plants framing the cityscape make for unique compositions. The glass walls give clear, unobstructed views.

Best time: Sunset slots are the most coveted. Book the moment tickets become available (three weeks ahead).

13. Primrose Hill

Location: Primrose Hill, NW1 • Nearest Tube: Chalk Farm

A gentle hill in Regent's Park with one of the best panoramic views of the London skyline. You can see from the BT Tower to The Shard to Canary Wharf. The viewpoint bench at the summit is iconic.

Best time: Sunrise for golden light across the skyline. New Year's Day is traditional but packed. Any clear evening works beautifully.

14. Greenwich Observatory

Location: Blackheath Avenue, SE10 8QJ • Nearest: Cutty Sark DLR

The view from the observatory terrace across the Maritime Greenwich UNESCO site, with the Queen's House, Old Royal Naval College, and Canary Wharf beyond, is one of London's grandest compositions. Stand on the Prime Meridian line for a unique photo.

Best time: Late afternoon for warm light on the white buildings below. The climb up the hill filters out casual visitors.

15. Parliament Hill, Hampstead Heath

Location: Hampstead Heath, NW5 • Nearest Tube: Gospel Oak (Overground)

A protected viewpoint offering a sweeping panorama across the entire London skyline. You can pick out every major landmark. Kite-flying and dog-walking add foreground interest.

Best time: Sunset or sunrise for the best light. Bonfire Night (5 November) for fireworks over the skyline.

Gardens & Natural Beauty

16. Kyoto Garden, Holland Park

Location: Holland Park, W8 6LU • Nearest Tube: Holland Park

A tranquil Japanese garden with a waterfall, koi pond, stone lanterns, and perfectly pruned maples. The autumn colours (October–November) are spectacular — reds, oranges, and golds reflected in the water.

Best time: Early morning for still water reflections and no crowds. Autumn for colour. Spring for the wisteria and azaleas.

17. Kew Gardens

Location: Royal Botanic Gardens, TW9 3AE • Nearest Tube: Kew Gardens • Entry: £15

A photographer's paradise in every season. The Palm House (Victorian glasshouse with tropical plants), the Temperate House (the world's largest surviving Victorian glass structure), the Treetop Walkway, and the seasonal gardens offer endless compositions. The lily pads in the Waterlily House are particularly photogenic.

Best time: Spring for bluebells and cherry blossoms. Autumn for the arboretum colours. The Temperate House interior works in any weather.

18. Little Venice

Location: Blomfield Road, W9 • Nearest Tube: Warwick Avenue

Where the Regent's Canal meets the Grand Union Canal, creating a peaceful basin lined with colourful narrowboats, weeping willows, and waterside cafes. The reflections on still mornings are beautiful.

Best time: Early morning for mirror-like water reflections. Spring when the trees are in blossom. Take the canal boat to Camden for a scenic ride.

19. Hampstead Pergola & Hill Garden

Location: Inverforth Close, NW3 7EX • Nearest Tube: Hampstead

A hidden Edwardian pergola draped in wisteria, climbing roses, and vines. The raised walkway overlooks the West Heath and feels like a secret garden from a novel. One of London's least-known beautiful spots.

Best time: Late May to early June when the wisteria is in bloom. Early morning for dappled light through the vines.

Architecture & Interiors

20. Platform 9¾, King's Cross

Location: King's Cross Station, N1C 4AH • Nearest Tube: King's Cross

The Harry Potter trolley-in-the-wall photo spot. A professional photographer is on hand to take your photo (free, prints available to buy). The queue can be 30–60 minutes at peak times. The adjacent Harry Potter shop is also photogenic.

Best time: Weekday mornings (before 10 am) for the shortest queue. Early January and February are the quietest months.

21. The Natural History Museum Entrance Hall

Location: Cromwell Road, SW7 5BD • Nearest Tube: South Kensington

Hintze Hall with its soaring Romanesque arches, intricate ceiling paintings, and the suspended blue whale skeleton is breathtaking. The grand staircase is the classic shot. The exterior terracotta facade is equally impressive.

Best time: When the museum opens at 10 am — head straight to the main hall before it fills. The exterior is best shot from across Cromwell Road in morning light.

22. The British Museum Great Court

Location: Great Russell Street, WC1B 3DG • Nearest Tube: Holborn

The Norman Foster-designed glass roof over the Great Court is a masterpiece of modern architecture. The tessellated glass panels create beautiful patterns of light. Wide-angle lenses show the full sweep of the curved glass ceiling around the circular Reading Room.

Best time: Midday when the sun is directly overhead and light streams through the glass. Weekday mornings for fewer people.

23. St Pancras Renaissance Hotel

Location: Euston Road, NW1 2AR • Nearest Tube: King's Cross

The grand staircase of the St Pancras hotel is one of the most photographed interiors in London — soaring Gothic Revival architecture with painted walls, iron railings, and dramatic curves. The Eurostar concourse with its blue ironwork and clock is also stunning.

Best time: The hotel lobby is publicly accessible. Visit midweek for fewer people on the staircase.

24. Liberty London

Location: Regent Street, W1B 5AH • Nearest Tube: Oxford Circus

The Tudor Revival timber-frame exterior is unique on Regent Street. Inside, the galleried atrium with its dark wood beams and stained glass is stunning. The haberdashery department with its rolls of Liberty print fabric is a visual feast.

Best time: The exterior is best on a bright day. The interior works in any weather. Christmas decorations (November–December) are particularly photogenic.

More Hidden Gems

25. Daunt Books, Marylebone

Location: 83 Marylebone High Street, W1U 4QW • Nearest Tube: Baker Street

An Edwardian bookshop with a long oak-galleried room, stained-glass window, and William Morris-style skylights. Often called the most beautiful bookshop in London. The back room with its double-height gallery is the shot everyone wants.

Best time: When the shop opens (9 am weekdays) for the emptiest shelves and softest light through the skylights.

26. The Barbican Conservatory

Location: Silk Street, EC2Y 8DS • Nearest Tube: Barbican • Entry: Free (limited hours)

London's second-largest conservatory, hidden inside the brutalist Barbican complex. Tropical plants, cacti, koi ponds, and exotic birds amid the concrete architecture create a surreal contrast. Open Sundays and some weekday afternoons only — check the website.

Best time: Sunday afternoons when it opens. The combination of tropical green against raw concrete is unlike anything else in London.

27. Covent Garden Market

Location: The Piazza, WC2E 8RF • Nearest Tube: Covent Garden

The iron and glass market building, the flower stalls, and the street performers create a photogenic scene. The Apple Market interior with its iron pillars is particularly atmospheric. The Christmas decorations here (from November) are spectacular.

Best time: Early morning (before 9 am) for the architecture without crowds. Evenings when the market is lit with fairy lights.

28. Battersea Power Station

Location: Circus Road West, SW11 8DD • Nearest Tube: Battersea Power Station

The iconic Art Deco power station, now a retail and residential complex, is stunning from the riverside. The four white chimneys against the sky are unmistakable. The interior atrium is also impressive.

Best time: Sunset from the river walk for warm light on the brick facade. The illuminated chimneys at night are dramatic.

29. The Walkie Talkie & Cheesegrater Reflections

Location: Fenchurch Street / Leadenhall Street, EC3 • Nearest Tube: Monument

The cluster of modern skyscrapers in the City — the Walkie Talkie, Cheesegrater, Scalpel, and Gherkin — create dramatic reflections and angular compositions when photographed from street level looking up. The contrasts with older buildings (medieval churches, Victorian pubs) make for striking juxtapositions.

Best time: Midday when the sun creates reflections on the glass facades. Blue hour for illuminated towers against a deep blue sky.

30. Richmond Park Deer

Location: Richmond Park, TW10 5HS • Nearest Tube: Richmond

Over 600 free-roaming red and fallow deer in a 2,500-acre royal park. The deer are wild but habituated to people, allowing relatively close photography (keep at least 50 metres distance). The Isabella Plantation within the park is a woodland garden with azaleas reflected in streams.

Best time: Autumn rutting season (September–November) for dramatic stag photography with misty mornings. Early morning year-round for golden light and active deer. Use a zoom lens — 200mm minimum.

Photography Gear Tips

  • Smartphone: Modern smartphone cameras are excellent for London street photography. Use portrait mode for cafe shots and wide-angle for architecture. Clean your lens — it makes more difference than any filter.
  • Wide-angle lens: Essential for interiors (museums, markets, churches) and for capturing the full scale of buildings.
  • Zoom lens: Useful for Richmond Park deer, rooftop details, and candid street shots.
  • Tripod: Needed for blue hour and night shots. Small travel tripods work for most locations. Note: tripods are not allowed inside most museums.
  • Rain cover: A plastic bag with a hole for the lens works in a pinch. London rain is usually light enough that a few minutes of shooting is fine.
  • Editing: London's grey skies can make photos look flat. Slight increases to contrast, warmth, and vibrance bring London photos to life without looking over-edited.
