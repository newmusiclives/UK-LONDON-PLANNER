Christmas in London 2026

From dazzling light displays to festive markets and world-famous ice rinks, London transforms into a winter wonderland every December. Here is everything you need to plan your Christmas trip.

Plan My Christmas Trip

Christmas Lights and Displays

London's Christmas lights are switched on from mid-November and stay illuminated through early January. Most displays are free to enjoy on foot.

Oxford Street

The most famous Christmas lights in London stretch over a mile along one of the world's busiest shopping streets. Typically switched on in early November, the display features hundreds of thousands of LED lights in festive designs. Walk the full length from Marble Arch to Tottenham Court Road for the best experience.

Best time to visit: Weekday evenings after 6pm to avoid the heaviest shopping crowds.

Regent Street

The elegant curve of Regent Street hosts some of London's most spectacular lights, often featuring angel-wing designs that arch across the road. The switch-on event in November draws large crowds and sometimes features live performances. The lights complement the grand architecture beautifully.

Tip: Continue south to Piccadilly Circus and the side streets for more displays.

Carnaby Street

Carnaby Street takes a creative, themed approach each year, often collaborating with charities or artists for unique installations. Past themes have included ocean conservation, neon rock-and-roll, and bohemian-inspired designs. The pedestrianised street makes for a relaxed stroll through independent shops and restaurants.

Don't miss: The surrounding Kingly Court for food and cocktails after your walk.

More Lights Worth Seeing

Covent Garden: Giant Christmas tree, mistletoe chandeliers, and a reindeer installation in the Piazza. Bond Street: Luxury boutiques with elaborate window displays and elegant overhead lights. Kew Gardens: Christmas at Kew is a ticketed after-dark trail through illuminated botanical gardens (book early, it sells out). South Molton Street: A quieter, charming alternative near Bond Street station.

Winter Wonderland and Ice Skating

Hyde Park Winter Wonderland

Europe's largest free-entry Christmas event runs from mid-November to early January. The sprawling park features fairground rides, a giant observation wheel, an ice rink, the Magical Ice Kingdom (walk-through ice sculpture experience), circus shows, and dozens of food and drink stalls. Entry is free but must be booked in advance (timed slots). Individual attractions are ticketed.

Dates: Typically 17 November - 1 January

Budget tip: Entry is free. Bring cash and card as food stalls vary. Rides typically cost between 5-10 pounds each. Visit on weekdays to avoid the worst queues.

Somerset House Ice Rink

The most iconic ice rink in London, set in the stunning neoclassical courtyard of Somerset House on the Strand. Skating here feels genuinely magical, especially in the evening when the building is lit up. Sessions last around 40 minutes. Book well in advance as this sells out fast, particularly weekend evening slots.

Price: From around 12-20 pounds depending on time slot. Premium experiences with champagne available.

Natural History Museum Ice Rink

Skate in front of one of London's most beautiful buildings. The rink sits right outside the museum's grand Romanesque entrance in South Kensington, with a large Christmas tree as the centrepiece. Perfect for families. Combine with a visit to the free museum galleries before or after your session.

Tip: Morning sessions are usually quieter and cheaper. The surrounding museums (V&A, Science Museum) are all free.

Tower of London Ice Rink

Skate in the shadow of the historic Tower of London with views of Tower Bridge. This dramatic setting on the moat of a 1,000-year-old fortress is hard to beat. Evening sessions offer the most atmospheric experience as the Tower is beautifully illuminated. A café and bar are available rinkside.

Combine with: A visit to the Tower's Crown Jewels (separate ticket required, book online).

Christmas Markets

London hosts dozens of Christmas markets from late November through December. Here are the best ones to visit for food, gifts, and festive atmosphere.

Southbank Centre Winter Market

Stretching along the Thames from the Southbank Centre to the London Eye, this market features over 100 wooden chalets selling handmade crafts, street food from around the world, and mulled wine. The riverside setting with views of the Houses of Parliament across the water is stunning. Free to walk through, with regular free live music and performances inside the Southbank Centre.

Must-try: Raclette cheese stalls, Dutch pancakes, and hot chocolate with churros.

Leicester Square Christmas Market

A traditional Bavarian-style market right in the heart of the West End. The wooden chalets sell ornaments, handcrafted gifts, and German sausages. The small but charming Santa's grotto is popular with families. Its central location makes it easy to combine with a theatre visit, dinner in Chinatown, or shopping on nearby streets.

Open: Usually early November through early January. Free entry.

Greenwich Christmas Market

Set within the beautiful grounds of the Old Royal Naval College, this artisan market focuses on handmade and locally-produced goods. It feels less commercial than central London markets and offers a more relaxed, community-oriented atmosphere. The covered market hall is open year-round, but the outdoor Christmas extension adds dozens more stalls.

Getting there: Take the DLR to Cutty Sark station, or ride the Thames Clipper from central London for a scenic approach. Combine with a visit to the Cutty Sark, the Royal Observatory, and Greenwich Park.

More Markets Worth Visiting

Kingston Christmas Market: Charming Surrey market modelled on German Christmas traditions. Covent Garden: The piazza hosts artisan stalls alongside its permanent market. Borough Market: London's premier food market adds seasonal specials including mince pies, game meats, and artisan cheeses. Columbia Road: The Sunday flower market sells beautiful wreaths and Christmas trees.

Pantomimes, Shows, and Carol Services

Pantomime Season

Pantomime is a uniquely British Christmas tradition that runs from late November through January. These raucous, family-friendly theatrical shows feature audience participation ("He's behind you!"), slapstick comedy, cross-dressing actors, and celebrity casts. Major venues include the London Palladium, Hackney Empire, and Richmond Theatre. Pantos are perfect for families and anyone wanting to experience a truly British holiday tradition.

Price: Tickets range from 15-75 pounds depending on venue and seating. Book early for popular casts.

Carol Services and Concerts

Westminster Abbey: The famous Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols is one of the most prestigious carol services in the world. Free but ticketed — apply well in advance as demand far exceeds capacity. St Paul's Cathedral: Multiple carol services throughout December in Sir Christopher Wren's masterpiece. The acoustics are extraordinary. King's College Chapel: A day trip to Cambridge for the world-famous Christmas Eve broadcast service (ballot for tickets in autumn).

Free options: Many parish churches across London hold free carol services. Check local listings for your area.

West End Christmas Shows

The West End sparkles during the Christmas season. Classic productions of The Nutcracker at the Royal Opera House, A Christmas Carol adaptations, and seasonal musicals are complemented by the regular long-running shows. Many productions run special Christmas performances and limited-run holiday shows. TKTS booth in Leicester Square offers same-day discounted tickets.

Festive Food, Afternoon Teas, and Dining

Harrods Christmas Department

Harrods opens its legendary Christmas department on the fourth floor as early as August, but visiting in December is a truly special experience. Browse elaborate ornaments, luxury crackers, handmade stockings, and exquisitely designed Christmas trees. The ground-floor food halls offer premium hampers, Christmas puddings, and festive treats that make wonderful gifts. The building's exterior light display is a destination in itself.

Festive Afternoon Teas

London's top hotels and tea rooms offer special Christmas-themed afternoon teas throughout December. Expect mince pie variations, Christmas cake, gingerbread, and champagne pairings. Top picks include:

The Ritz: The most famous (and most expensive) at around 75 pounds per person. Book months ahead. Sketch: Instagram-worthy pink room with creative festive pastries. Fortnum & Mason: The classic London choice in their elegant Diamond Jubilee Tea Salon. Claridge's: Glamorous Art Deco setting with their famous annual designer Christmas tree in the lobby.

Christmas Day Dining

Most restaurants close on Christmas Day, so advance booking is essential if you want to eat out. Many hotels serve elaborate Christmas Day lunches (expect 100-250 pounds per person at top venues). For budget options, some gastropubs stay open and offer set menus at more reasonable prices. Chinatown restaurants are typically open on Christmas Day and can be a fun alternative. Self-catering visitors should stock up by Christmas Eve as most shops close on the 25th.

Key tip: Book Christmas Day dining by early November at the latest. Popular venues sell out months in advance.

Boxing Day Sales and Practical Tips

Boxing Day Sales (26 December)

The Boxing Day sales are a major British tradition. Queues form outside department stores like Selfridges, Harrods, and Liberty from the early hours of the morning. Oxford Street, Regent Street, and Westfield shopping centres see massive footfall. Discounts typically range from 30-70% off. Many retailers now start their sales online on Christmas Eve, so check websites if you prefer to avoid the crowds.

Strategy: Have a hit list of items and stores. Arrive early (before 9am) for the best selection. Consider Bicester Village (1 hour from London) for designer outlet shopping.

Practical Tips for Christmas in London

Transport: There is NO public transport on Christmas Day. Plan taxis (surge pricing applies) or walk. Limited services run on Boxing Day. Weather: Expect 3-8 degrees Celsius, rain is common, snow is rare. Dress in warm, waterproof layers. Accommodation: Book early. Hotel prices peak in the two weeks before Christmas. Consider Airbnb or apart-hotels for longer stays and self-catering flexibility.

Money-saving tips: Many museums remain free year-round. Christmas lights walks are free. Book ice skating and Winter Wonderland slots for off-peak times. Eat at markets instead of restaurants for better value.

Suggested 3-Day Christmas Itinerary

Day 1: Christmas lights walking tour (Oxford Street to Regent Street to Carnaby Street), dinner in Covent Garden, evening at a West End show.

Day 2: Ice skating at Somerset House, Southbank Winter Market, afternoon tea, carol service at St Paul's Cathedral.

Day 3: Harrods food halls, Hyde Park Winter Wonderland, Greenwich Market, evening pantomime.
