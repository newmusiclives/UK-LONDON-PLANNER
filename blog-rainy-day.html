20 Brilliant Things to Do in London When It Rains

London averages 106 rainy days per year. Do not let grey skies ruin your trip — some of the city's best experiences happen indoors.

World-Class Museums (Free)

1. The British Museum

Location: Great Russell Street, WC1B 3DG • Nearest Tube: Holborn / Tottenham Court Road • Cost: Free

You could spend an entire rainy week here and still not see everything. The Rosetta Stone, Egyptian mummies, Parthenon sculptures, and Sutton Hoo treasure are the highlights, but the real joy is wandering and stumbling on something unexpected. Start in the Great Court (the largest covered public square in Europe) and work outward. The less-visited rooms on the upper floors — Japanese art, Islamic tiles, Enlightenment gallery — are where you escape the crowds.

Rainy day tip: Everyone has the same idea on rainy days, so arrive when doors open at 10 am or after 3 pm when coach groups leave.

2. The V&A (Victoria and Albert Museum)

Location: Cromwell Road, SW7 2RL • Nearest Tube: South Kensington • Cost: Free

The world's largest museum of decorative arts and design. The Fashion Gallery, the Cast Courts (full-size plaster copies of Trajan's Column and Michelangelo's David), and the Jewellery Gallery are particular highlights. The Medieval and Renaissance galleries on the ground floor are stunning. Allow at least 3 hours.

Rainy day tip: There is a subway tunnel from South Kensington station directly into the museum — you will not get wet at all.

3. The Natural History Museum

Location: Cromwell Road, SW7 5BD • Nearest Tube: South Kensington • Cost: Free

The dinosaur gallery, the blue whale skeleton in Hintze Hall, the earthquake simulator, and the Wildlife Photographer of the Year exhibition (ticketed) make this one of London's most engaging museums. The building itself — Romanesque terracotta cathedral designed by Alfred Waterhouse — is as impressive as the exhibits.

4. The Science Museum

Location: Exhibition Road, SW7 2DD • Nearest Tube: South Kensington • Cost: Free (some exhibitions ticketed)

Genuinely interactive and fun for all ages. The flight gallery, space exploration section, and the Wonderlab (ticketed, £10) are standouts. The IMAX cinema shows spectacular science and nature documentaries on a screen five storeys high.

South Kensington triple: All three museums above are within a 5-minute walk of each other. You could easily fill an entire rainy day hopping between them via the covered Exhibition Road underpass.

Covered Markets

5. Borough Market

Location: 8 Southwark Street, SE1 1TL • Nearest Tube: London Bridge • Open: Mon–Sat (full market Wed–Sat)

London's most famous food market is largely covered, making it a perfect rainy day destination. Graze your way through artisan cheese, fresh oysters, gourmet sandwiches, Ethiopian injera, Turkish gozleme, and some of the best sourdough bread in the country. Get there before 11 am on a Saturday or the crowds become overwhelming.

6. Old Spitalfields Market

Location: 16 Horner Square, E1 6EW • Nearest Tube: Liverpool Street • Open: Daily

A beautiful Victorian covered market with a rotating mix of independent fashion, art, vintage, and food stalls. Thursdays are antiques day, Fridays bring an independent fashion market, and weekends have the largest selection. The surrounding streets — Brick Lane, Hanbury Street, Fournier Street — are worth exploring between showers.

7. Leadenhall Market

Location: Gracechurch Street, EC3V 1LT • Nearest Tube: Bank / Monument • Open: Mon–Fri

A stunning Victorian covered market in the heart of the City. The ornate painted roof and cobblestone lanes are gorgeous (and were used as Diagon Alley in the Harry Potter films). The market houses wine bars, cheese shops, and boutiques. It is primarily a weekday destination — most shops close at weekends.

Afternoon Tea

8. The Ritz

Location: 150 Piccadilly, W1J 9BR • Price: From £75 per person • Dress code: Smart (jacket for gentlemen)

The gold standard of London afternoon teas, served in the opulent Palm Court with live harp music. Finger sandwiches, warm scones with clotted cream and jam, and a tiered selection of pastries. Book at least 6–8 weeks ahead. It is expensive but genuinely special.

9. Sketch (The Gallery)

Location: 9 Conduit Street, W1S 2XG • Price: From £60 per person

Afternoon tea in a pink-velvet David Shrigley art gallery. The egg-shaped toilets alone are worth the visit. The food is excellent — the pastries are works of art — and the Instagram potential is off the charts. Book 2–3 weeks ahead.

10. The Orangery at Kensington Palace

Location: Kensington Gardens, W8 4PX • Price: From £40 per person

A more affordable option in a beautiful 18th-century orangery overlooking Kensington Palace gardens. The setting is serene and the scones are generous. Less formal than The Ritz, making it better for families or those who prefer a relaxed atmosphere.

Special Cinema Experiences

11. The Electric Cinema, Notting Hill

Location: 191 Portobello Road, W11 2ED • Nearest Tube: Ladbroke Grove • Tickets: From £16

One of the oldest working cinemas in the UK (opened 1910), with luxurious leather armchairs, velvet sofas, front-row beds, and footstools. They serve wine and cocktails to your seat. Watching a film here in the rain is about as cosy as London gets.

12. BFI IMAX

Location: 1 Charlie Chaplin Walk, SE1 8XR • Nearest Tube: Waterloo • Tickets: From £15

The UK's largest cinema screen — 20 metres high and 26 metres wide. For blockbusters, nature documentaries, and special event screenings, there is nothing like it. The screen is inside the iconic circular building on the Waterloo roundabout.

13. Curzon Mayfair

Location: 38 Curzon Street, W1J 7TY • Nearest Tube: Green Park • Tickets: From £14

A beautiful Art Deco cinema showing independent and arthouse films alongside mainstream releases. The bar is excellent, and the plush seating makes this a civilised alternative to multiplex chains.

Indoor Activities

14. Escape Rooms

London has some of the best escape rooms in the world. Handmade Mysteries (various locations, from £28pp) are brilliantly themed with a sense of humour. AIM Escape (Aldgate, from £25pp) has high-tech puzzles and impressive set design. clueQuest (King's Cross, from £30pp) is great for teams and has multiple difficulty levels. Book online in advance as popular time slots sell out.

15. All Star Lanes (Bowling)

Locations: Brick Lane, Holborn, Stratford • Price: From £9 per game (off-peak)

Retro-American bowling alleys with a cocktail bar, diner food, and private karaoke rooms. The 1950s Americana decor is fun, the burgers are solid, and two games plus food makes a proper rainy afternoon. Off-peak (weekday afternoons) is significantly cheaper and quieter.

16. Indoor Climbing at The Castle

Location: Green Lanes, N4 2HA • Nearest Tube: Manor House • Price: From £14 (day pass)

London's largest indoor climbing centre, housed in a converted Victorian pumping station. Bouldering walls, roped climbing, and courses for all levels. No experience needed — they run beginner sessions. A brilliant way to burn energy on a rainy day.

Historic Shopping Arcades

17. Burlington Arcade

Location: 51 Piccadilly, W1J 0QJ • Nearest Tube: Green Park

Britain's first shopping arcade (1819), a covered Regency promenade lined with luxury boutiques selling cashmere, jewellery, perfume, and leather goods. The beadles (top-hatted security) still patrol. Even if you cannot afford anything, the architecture and atmosphere are worth a stroll. It connects Piccadilly to Burlington Gardens — useful as a rain shortcut too.

18. Royal Arcade

Location: 28 Old Bond Street, W1S 4SL • Nearest Tube: Green Park

A beautiful Victorian arcade (1879) with ornate arched windows and a painted glass roof. Smaller and quieter than Burlington Arcade but equally atmospheric. It connects Old Bond Street to Albemarle Street.

19. Leadenhall Market (revisited as architecture)

Already mentioned for its market stalls, but worth emphasising as pure architectural spectacle. The ornate cobalt, maroon, and cream painted ironwork under the Victorian glass roof is one of London's most photogenic interiors. Visit at lunchtime when the City workers fill the wine bars.

Spa & Wellness

20. Ironmonger Row Baths

Location: 1–11 Ironmonger Row, EC1V 3QN • Nearest Tube: Old Street • Price: Turkish bath from £20

A council-run Turkish bath and spa that has been operating since 1931, recently renovated but retaining its Art Deco character. The three-hour Turkish bath session (hot room, steam room, plunge pool, relaxation area) is one of London's best wellness bargains. The swimming pool is beautiful — 30 metres in a light-filled hall. Book Turkish bath sessions online as they have limited capacity.

Honourable mentions

  • AIRE Ancient Baths (Covent Garden): Premium Roman-style thermal baths in a beautifully converted warehouse. From £50 for a 90-minute session. Stunning candlelit spaces.
  • Porchester Spa (Bayswater): Another council-run Turkish bath, even older than Ironmonger Row. Art Deco interiors, seriously hot steam rooms, and cold plunge pools. From £18.
  • Thermae Bath Spa (Bath): Technically a day trip, but if the rain drives you out of London entirely, the rooftop thermal pool overlooking Bath Abbey is extraordinary. See our day trips guide for train info.

Rainy Day Survival Tips

  • Carry a compact umbrella: London rain is usually light drizzle rather than torrential downpour. A small fold-up umbrella is more useful than a heavy raincoat.
  • Use the Tube strategically: Many stations connect to shopping centres and attractions via underground passages. The South Kensington subway to the museums and the Canary Wharf connections to the shopping mall are two of the best.
  • Book museums with timed entry: The most popular free museums now offer free timed-entry tickets. Pre-booking avoids queuing in the rain and guarantees entry.
  • Embrace the pub: A rainy afternoon in a London pub with a good book, a pint of ale, and a packet of crisps is not a consolation prize — it is one of the great pleasures of visiting the city.
  • Check last-minute theatre: Rainy days often mean returned tickets for West End shows. The TodayTix app and the TKTS booth in Leicester Square sell discounted same-day tickets.
  • Waterproof shoes matter: London puddles are deeper than they look and the pavements are uneven. Waterproof shoes or boots make a bigger difference to comfort than any coat.
