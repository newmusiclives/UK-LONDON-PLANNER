Neighbourhood Spotlight: Bermondsey

A walking route through one of South London's most exciting neighbourhoods — from world-class food markets to contemporary art and craft beer.

Your Bermondsey Walking Route

Bermondsey has quietly become one of London's most compelling neighbourhoods. Once a centre for leather tanning and warehousing, it is now home to independent food producers, cutting-edge galleries, and some of the best craft beer in the country. This walking route takes you through the highlights at a relaxed pace.

2.5 km Total walking distance
3–5 hours Recommended time
Saturday Best day to go
10 AM Ideal start time

Getting there: Take the Jubilee Line, Northern Line, or bus to London Bridge station. Exit onto Tooley Street and walk south-east along Bermondsey Street. Alternatively, take the bus to Bermondsey station (Jubilee Line) and walk west.

1

Maltby Street Market

Maltby Street, SE1 3PA Sat 10 AM–5 PM, Sun 11 AM–4 PM Free entry

Start your walk at Maltby Street Market, Bermondsey's answer to Borough Market but without the tourist crowds. Housed under Victorian railway arches, this intimate market is where serious London food lovers come. The stalls change seasonally but reliably excellent options include The Cheese Truck (grilled cheese sandwiches with truffle honey), Bad Brownie (dense, fudgy brownies), and Wazen (Lebanese flatbreads).

Wander through slowly, taste as you go, and grab a flat white from one of the coffee stalls. Allow 45 minutes to an hour to explore properly. Saturday is the best day — more stalls open and the atmosphere is buzzing.

2

White Cube Bermondsey

144–152 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3TQ Tue–Sat 10 AM–6 PM, Sun 12–6 PM Free entry

White Cube is one of the world's most influential contemporary art galleries. The Bermondsey space — a converted 1970s warehouse — is their largest, with over 5,500 square metres of exhibition space. Shows rotate every 2–3 months and feature major international artists: think Damien Hirst, Tracey Emin, Antony Gormley, and Georg Baselitz.

The gallery's scale is dramatic. The main hall can accommodate enormous installations that would not fit in most museums. Even if contemporary art is not usually your thing, the sheer ambition of the space makes it worth a visit. Allow 30–45 minutes.

↓ 3-minute walk south on Bermondsey Street ↓
3

Fashion and Textile Museum

83 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3XF Tue–Sat 11 AM–6 PM £12 adults

Founded by designer Zandra Rhodes, this vivid orange and pink building houses rotating exhibitions on fashion, textiles, and design. Past shows have covered everything from Swinging Sixties fashion to Japanese textile art. The exhibitions are beautifully curated and surprisingly engaging even if fashion is not your primary interest.

The building itself is a Bermondsey landmark — designed by Mexican architect Ricardo Legorreta, it is one of the most colourful structures in London. Check their website for current exhibitions as they change quarterly. Allow 45 minutes to an hour.

↓ 2-minute walk, then left on Tanner Street ↓
4

Jose Tapas Bar

104 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3UB Daily 12–10:30 PM £6–14 per dish

Jose is a tiny, standing-room-mostly tapas bar that serves some of the best Spanish food in London. Run by Jose Pizarro (one of London's most respected Spanish chefs), it is modelled on the txikiteo bars of San Sebastian. The space is narrow and always packed, with legs of Ibérico ham hanging from the ceiling and a short, perfect menu chalked on the blackboard.

Order the croquetas (impossibly creamy), the grilled Padron peppers, and whatever ham or cheese they recommend that day. The sherry selection is outstanding. If Jose is too full, Pizarro — his larger sit-down restaurant next door — serves equally excellent food with table service and bookings.

↓ 8-minute walk east along Druid Street ↓
5

Bermondsey Beer Mile

Along Druid Street & Enid Street, SE1 Most open Sat 11 AM–6 PM £3–7 per half pint

End your walk at the famous Bermondsey Beer Mile — a stretch of railway arches housing some of London's best independent breweries and taprooms. Within a few hundred metres you can visit Fourpure, Partizan, Brew by Numbers, Anspach & Hobday, and The Kernel (widely considered one of the UK's finest craft breweries).

Each taproom has its own character. The Kernel is minimal and serious, pouring rotating pale ales and stouts from battered kegs. Partizan is arty and experimental. Brew by Numbers is polished and accessible, great for newcomers to craft beer. Most offer small pours (thirds and halves) so you can taste widely without overdoing it.

Top tip: Saturday is the only day all the taprooms are open. Start at Fourpure or Partizan at the east end and work your way west towards Maltby Street. Some taprooms only accept cash, so bring a mix of payment options.
