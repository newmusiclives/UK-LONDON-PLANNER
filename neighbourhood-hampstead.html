Hampstead Neighbourhood Guide

Ancient heathland, village pubs, literary history, Kenwood House treasures, and London's greatest panoramic views from Parliament Hill

Overview

Hampstead is London's most beloved village neighbourhood, perched on a hill in north London and surrounded by 790 acres of wild, ancient heathland. Its narrow lanes of Georgian and Victorian cottages, independent bookshops, and cosy pubs give it a character entirely distinct from the rest of the city. For centuries it has attracted writers, artists, and intellectuals — Keats, Constable, Freud, and many more made their homes here.

The vibe: Leafy, literary, and wonderfully unhurried. Hampstead feels like an English village that happens to be four miles from central London. The Heath provides a genuine wilderness experience, the high street has retained its independent character, and the pubs are among the most atmospheric in the city.

Best for: Walkers, nature lovers, literary pilgrims, pub enthusiasts, and anyone who wants to escape the city without leaving it. A day combining a Heath walk with a pub lunch and a visit to Kenwood House is one of London's great pleasures.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Hampstead Heath

    Hampstead Heath, NW3

    One of London's greatest green spaces — 790 acres of rolling meadows, ancient woodland, ponds, and hills. Unlike London's manicured Royal Parks, the Heath is gloriously wild and feels like genuine countryside. The swimming ponds (separate men's, women's, and mixed bathing) are a unique London experience, open year-round including winter.

  • 2. Parliament Hill

    Hampstead Heath, NW5 1QR

    The kite-flying hill on the southern edge of the Heath offers one of the most iconic panoramic views in London, stretching from the Shard and St Paul's to the towers of Canary Wharf. A protected view that is spectacular at sunset and on clear winter days. Londoners come here to fly kites, run, and simply take in the skyline.

  • 3. Kenwood House

    Hampstead Lane, NW3 7JR

    A magnificent neoclassical mansion on the northern edge of the Heath housing the Iveagh Bequest — an exceptional art collection including Vermeer's The Guitar Player, a Rembrandt self-portrait, and works by Gainsborough and Turner. The house and grounds are free to visit, and the cafe terrace overlooks a sweeping lawn down to a lake.

  • 4. Keats House

    10 Keats Grove, NW3 2RR

    The Regency house where John Keats lived from 1818 to 1820 and wrote some of his greatest poems, including Ode to a Nightingale, inspired by a nightingale singing in the garden. A beautifully preserved literary shrine with original manuscripts and personal belongings.

  • 5. Freud Museum

    20 Maresfield Gardens, NW3 5SX

    The final home of Sigmund Freud after he fled Nazi-occupied Vienna in 1938. The study is preserved exactly as he left it, complete with the famous psychoanalytic couch, his collection of antiquities, and his library. A fascinating insight into one of the most influential minds of the 20th century.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Jin Kichi

    73 Heath Street, NW3 6UG • Japanese • $$

    A beloved Hampstead institution serving exceptional Japanese grilled skewers (yakitori), sushi, and izakaya dishes. The charcoal grill behind the counter fills the tiny restaurant with incredible aromas. Book ahead — it is small and immensely popular.

  • 2. The Wells Tavern

    30 Well Walk, NW3 1BX • Modern British • $$$

    A sophisticated gastropub on one of Hampstead's most charming streets. The upstairs dining room serves refined Modern British food, while the ground-floor bar offers a more relaxed pub experience. Sunday roasts are exceptional. The terrace is perfect for post-walk drinks.

  • 3. Villa Bianca

    1 Perrins Court, NW3 1QS • Italian • $$

    A long-established Italian restaurant on a pretty lane off Hampstead High Street. Classic Italian dishes done well — handmade pasta, wood-fired pizza, and excellent veal Milanese. The outdoor courtyard is a hidden gem in summer.

  • 4. Gaucho Hampstead

    64 Heath Street, NW3 1DN • Argentine steak • $$$

    Premium Argentine steaks in a stylish setting on Hampstead's main street. The ribeye and chorizo cuts are excellent, and the Argentine wine list is extensive. A treat after a long Heath walk when you have earned a serious meal.

  • 5. Arancini Brothers

    115 Fleet Road, NW3 2QG • Italian street food • $

    Handmade Sicilian arancini (fried risotto balls) with creative fillings from classic ragu to truffle and mushroom. Great for a quick, affordable, and delicious bite before or after a Heath walk. Takeaway or eat at the small counter.

Best Pubs & Bars

  • 1. The Holly Bush

    22 Holly Mount, NW3 6SG

    One of London's most beautiful and atmospheric pubs, tucked away up a flight of steps on Holly Mount. The gas-lit interior, open fires, dark wood, and intimate snugs have barely changed in two centuries. Real ales, good wine, and a timeless ambiance. A strong contender for London's best pub.

  • 2. The Spaniards Inn

    Spaniards Road, NW3 7JJ

    A legendary 16th-century coaching inn on the edge of the Heath, mentioned by Dickens in The Pickwick Papers and by Keats in poetry. The huge beer garden is one of London's finest. Supposedly haunted and once a haunt of the highwayman Dick Turpin. Outstanding Sunday roasts.

  • 3. The Flask

    14 Flask Walk, NW3 1HE

    A charming 18th-century pub on the picturesque Flask Walk, with a traditional front bar, a more formal dining room at the back, and outside tables on the pedestrianised lane. Well-kept Young's ales and good pub food.

  • 4. The Garden Gate

    14 South End Road, NW3 2QE

    A wonderful neighbourhood pub at the bottom of the Heath near the Overground station. Excellent craft beer selection, a warm welcome, a beautiful beer garden, and a proper Sunday roast. Popular with walkers returning from the Heath.

  • 5. The Stag

    67 Fleet Road, NW3 2QU

    A smart gastropub on Fleet Road with an impressive craft beer selection, natural wines, and refined but unfussy food. The terrace gets afternoon sun, and the weekend brunch is very popular with locals.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Ginger & White

    4A-5A Perrins Court, NW3 1QS

    A much-loved specialty coffee shop on a pretty lane off the high street. Excellent espresso, outstanding sausage rolls, and the best bacon sandwich in Hampstead. A neighbourhood institution and the perfect pre-walk fuel stop.

  • 2. Kenwood House Brew House Cafe

    Kenwood House, Hampstead Lane, NW3 7JR

    The cafe in the old service wing of Kenwood House with a terrace overlooking the sweeping lawn and lake. Good coffee, cakes, and light lunches in one of London's most beautiful settings. Combine with a visit to the free art collection.

  • 3. Louis Patisserie

    32 Heath Street, NW3 6TE

    A traditional Hungarian patisserie that has been serving cakes and pastries since 1963. The display of elaborate Central European cakes — Dobos torte, chestnut puree, and cream-filled slices — is irresistible. A Hampstead legend and a window into old-world cafe culture.

Shopping Highlights

  • Daunt Books — The beautiful Hampstead branch of the beloved independent bookshop at 51 South End Road, with an outstanding travel and fiction selection.
  • Flask Walk shops — A charming pedestrianised lane with antique shops, an excellent cheese shop (La Fromagerie), and independent boutiques.
  • Hampstead Antique & Craft Emporium — A treasure trove at 12 Heath Street with multiple dealers selling vintage jewellery, ceramics, prints, and decorative objects.
  • Keith Fawkes Books — A delightfully old-fashioned second-hand bookshop at 1 Flask Walk, crammed floor to ceiling with books on every subject.
  • Hampstead High Street — A mix of independent shops and high-end chains that has maintained its village character, including excellent clothing boutiques, homewares, and gift shops.

Getting There

Hampstead is easily reached from central London:

  • Hampstead (Northern line) — the main station, the deepest on the Tube network at 58.5 metres. Emerge onto Heath Street in the village centre
  • Hampstead Heath (London Overground) — for the southern end of the Heath, near Parliament Hill and the swimming ponds
  • Gospel Oak (London Overground) — for the southeast corner of the Heath and the Parliament Hill lido

Insider Tips

  • The best Heath walk route: start at Hampstead Tube, walk to the top of Parliament Hill for views, continue across to Kenwood House for art and coffee, then loop back via the Spaniards Inn for a well-earned pint. Allow 2-3 hours.
  • Swimming in the Hampstead Ponds is a quintessential London experience. The mixed bathing pond is open year-round. Bring a towel and be prepared for cold water — even in summer the ponds are bracing.
  • Visit the Holly Bush at lunchtime on a weekday for the best chance of a seat by the fire. Weekend evenings are atmospheric but very busy.
  • Hampstead is one of the best areas in London for autumn colour. The Heath is spectacular in October and November, especially the avenue of trees leading to Kenwood House.
  • Explore the narrow streets and steps around Holly Mount, Mount Vernon, and Hampstead Grove for some of London's prettiest and most atmospheric residential streets.
