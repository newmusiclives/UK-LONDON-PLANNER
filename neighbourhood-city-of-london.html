City of London Neighbourhood Guide

2,000 years of London history in one square mile — Roman walls, Wren churches, the medieval Guildhall, and Europe's most ambitious skyline

Overview

The City of London (not to be confused with Greater London) is the original Roman Londinium — a one-square-mile local authority that has governed itself since the 12th century. By day it's the UK's financial capital; by night and weekend, an eerily quiet medieval and Wren-era museum.

Highlights compress into tight walks: St Paul's, the Tower, Leadenhall Market, Guildhall, the Barbican, plus the glass-and-steel skyline of the Gherkin, Cheesegrater, Walkie-Talkie, and Scalpel.

The vibe: Weekday: busy, monied, suited. Weekend: almost abandoned, atmospheric, perfect for photography and slow exploration.

Best for: History buffs, architecture nerds, photographers, weekend wanderers who like empty streets.

Top Attractions

  • 1. St Paul's Cathedral

    St Paul's Churchyard, EC4M 8AD

    Sir Christopher Wren's 1710 masterpiece, built after the Great Fire. Climb 528 steps to the Golden Gallery (highest public viewpoint in any British cathedral).

  • 2. Tower of London

    Tower of London, EC3N 4AB

    Nearly 1,000 years of royal palace, fortress, and prison. Home to the Crown Jewels and the famous Beefeaters. Arrive 9am to see the Jewels before crowds.

  • 3. Leadenhall Market

    Gracechurch St, EC3V 1LT

    19th-century Victorian covered market — the entrance to Diagon Alley in the Harry Potter films. Deserted on weekends; best for photos.

  • 4. Sky Garden

    20 Fenchurch St, EC3M 3BY

    The free 35th-floor tropical garden atop the Walkie-Talkie. Book online 3 weeks ahead. Sunset slots go fastest.

  • 5. Guildhall & Roman Amphitheatre

    Gresham St, EC2V 7HH

    The medieval seat of City government, with a 2,000-year-old Roman amphitheatre in the basement (free). Combine with the free Clockmakers' Museum next door.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Sweetings

    39 Queen Victoria St, EC4N 4SA · British · $$$

    The City's Victorian fish restaurant (1889) — lunchtime-only, cash-preferred, potted shrimps and Dover sole for generations.

  • 2. City Social

    Tower 42, 25 Old Broad St, EC2N 1HQ · British · $$$$

    Jason Atherton's 24th-floor Michelin-starred Art Deco restaurant with spectacular skyline views. Set lunch £45.

  • 3. The Ivy Asia St Paul's

    20 New Change, EC4M 9AG · Asian · $$$

    Glitzy pan-Asian Ivy group outpost with a cathedral-facing terrace and emerald-green illuminated floor.

  • 4. Duck & Waffle

    110 Bishopsgate, EC2N 4AY · European · $$$$

    Britain's highest 24-hour restaurant on the 40th floor of the Heron Tower. The namesake duck & waffle dish £20.

  • 5. Hawksmoor Guildhall

    10 Basinghall St, EC2V 5BQ · British · $$$$

    Longhorn beef and lamb chops in a City chop house. Express menu £30 weekdays 5-6:30pm.

Best Bars & Pubs

  • 1. Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese

    145 Fleet St, EC4A 2BU

    Rebuilt in 1667 after the Great Fire; Dickens, Dr Johnson, and Mark Twain drank here. Low ceilings, dark wood, medieval cellar bar.

  • 2. The Jerusalem Tavern

    55 Britton St, EC1M 5UQ

    A 1720 Clerkenwell-border gem — St Peter's Brewery's tiny tap, feels like a time capsule. Squeeze onto a bench.

  • 3. Oriole

    East Poultry Ave, Smithfield, EC1A 9LH

    Smithfield's underground cocktail bar from the Nightjar team. Live jazz nightly, global cocktail menu.

  • 4. The Viaduct Tavern

    126 Newgate St, EC1A 7AA

    An 1869 Victorian gin palace opposite the Old Bailey — gilded mirrors, etched glass, cells from Old Newgate Prison in the basement.

  • 5. Coq d'Argent Rooftop

    1 Poultry, EC2R 8EJ

    Rooftop garden cocktail bar beside Bank Tube — views over the Lloyd's Building and City skyscrapers.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Host Café (St Mary Aldermary)

    Watling St, EC4M 9BW

    A specialty coffee shop set inside a 17th-century Wren church — the most dramatic café setting in London.

  • 2. Workshop Coffee Clerkenwell

    27 Clerkenwell Rd, EC1M 5RN

    Flagship roastery of one of London's most influential specialty coffee companies.

  • 3. Café Below (St Mary-le-Bow)

    Cheapside, EC2V 6AU

    Beneath the Bow bells in the Norman crypt — proper hot lunches, cash-only. City-worker classic.

Shopping Highlights

  • Leadenhall Market — independent jewellers, wine merchants, the Lamb Tavern. Closed weekends.
  • One New Change — shopping centre directly beside St Paul's with a free public rooftop viewing terrace (6th floor).
  • Daunt Books Cheapside — City branch of the Edwardian travel bookshop.
  • London Silver Vaults (53-64 Chancery Lane) — the world's largest retail antique silver collection, 30+ dealers in underground vaults.
  • Royal Exchange — luxury shopping arcade inside the 19th-century building opposite Bank.

Getting There

Main stations serving this neighbourhood:

  • Bank / Monument (Central, Northern, Waterloo & City, District, Circle, DLR) — the Square Mile's central crossroads
  • St Paul's (Central) — cathedral side
  • Liverpool Street (Central, Metropolitan, Circle, Hammersmith & City, Elizabeth) — north-east corner
  • Tower Hill (District, Circle) — Tower of London; connects to Tower Gateway DLR

Insider Tips

  • The City is half-empty on weekends — perfect for photography of Leadenhall Market and Lloyd's of London without crowds.
  • The Horizon 22 viewing gallery (58th floor, 22 Bishopsgate) is free — book 2 weeks ahead.
  • Free lunchtime concerts at St Martin-in-the-Fields, St Lawrence Jewry, and St Mary-le-Bow most weekdays.
  • Sunday morning Evensong at St Paul's (17:00 actually) is free — no cathedral entry fee required.
  • Open House London (September) opens City skyscrapers to the public for free — the Gherkin, Cheesegrater, and Lloyd's are the standouts.
