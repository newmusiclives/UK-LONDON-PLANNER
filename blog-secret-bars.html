London's Best Secret Bars & Speakeasies

Hidden behind bookshelves, inside fridges, and down unmarked staircases — the cocktail bars you were never meant to find

London has always loved a secret. From medieval plague tunnels to wartime bunkers, the city is built on hidden spaces. Today, that tradition continues in the form of secret bars — concealed cocktail dens that test your ability to find them before you can enjoy what is inside. Some hide behind unmarked doors, others are accessed through working refrigerators or vintage wardrobes, and a few require a password to enter. The cocktails in these bars tend to be exceptional, the atmosphere electric, and the experience unforgettable. Here are 15 of the best secret bars in London, along with exactly how to find them and what to order when you get there.

1. Cahoots

Location: 13 Kingly Court, Carnaby Street, W1B 5PW

How to find it: Walk down the steps at Kingly Court near Carnaby Street. The entrance is designed to look like a disused 1940s Tube station. You descend into what feels like a wartime underground shelter, complete with vintage carriages, sandbag walls, and swing music.

What to order: Cocktails are themed around the 1940s and served in enamel mugs, flasks, and vintage teacups. Try the Blitz Spirit (gin, elderflower, prosecco) or the Ration Book (rum, lime, ginger). Cocktails run 12 to 15 pounds.

Price range: Moderate. Expect to spend 30 to 50 pounds per person for an evening.

2. Evans & Peel Detective Agency

Location: 310C Earls Court Road, SW5 9BA

How to find it: Ring the doorbell of what appears to be a private detective's office. A detective will interview you through the intercom — you need to present a "case" (a reason to drink, essentially). Be creative. Once admitted, you descend into a prohibition-era speakeasy in the basement. Booking is essential.

What to order: The cocktail menu changes regularly, but the bartenders are skilled at making classics. Ask for an Old Fashioned or a Whiskey Sour. Cocktails are around 13 to 16 pounds.

Price range: Moderate to high. Budget 40 to 60 pounds per person.

3. The Mayor of Scaredy Cat Town

Location: Inside The Breakfast Club, 12-16 Artillery Lane, E1 7LS

How to find it: Enter The Breakfast Club in Spitalfields and tell the hostess you are here to see the Mayor. You will be led to what looks like a Smeg fridge in the back of the restaurant. Open the fridge door, step through, and descend into a dimly lit basement bar. It is genuinely surreal the first time.

What to order: Their espresso martini is one of the best in London. The bar also does excellent whiskey cocktails. Drinks are 11 to 14 pounds.

Price range: Moderate. Around 30 to 45 pounds per person.

4. Nightjar

Location: 129 City Road, EC1V 1JB

How to find it: Look for a small, unremarkable door on City Road near Old Street station. There is no sign, just a small bird logo. Descend the stairs into a dimly lit jazz bar with live music every night. Booking is absolutely essential — this is one of London's most acclaimed cocktail bars.

What to order: The menu is divided into Pre-Prohibition, Prohibition, and Post-Prohibition eras. Each cocktail is a production — served with theatrical garnishes and in unusual vessels. Try the Coaltit (mezcal, lime, charcoal) or ask the bartender for a recommendation. Cocktails are 14 to 18 pounds.

Price range: High. Budget 50 to 70 pounds per person for cocktails and music.

5. Opium

Location: 15-16 Gerrard Street, W1D 6JE (Chinatown)

How to find it: In the heart of Chinatown, look for a jade-green door between the restaurants on Gerrard Street. There is no sign — just a small buzzer. Ring it, and if there is space you will be admitted to a multi-floor cocktail den themed around a 1920s oriental tea house, with dim sum and Asian-inspired cocktails across three floors.

What to order: The Peony cocktail (jasmine-infused gin, lychee, rose) is their signature. The dim sum is surprisingly good for a cocktail bar. Cocktails are 13 to 16 pounds.

Price range: Moderate to high. Around 40 to 60 pounds per person with food and drinks.

6. Discount Suit Company

Location: 29A Wentworth Street, E1 7TB

How to find it: On Wentworth Street near Petticoat Lane Market, descend a flight of stairs below what was once a discount suit shop (hence the name). The small basement bar has an industrial feel, exposed brick walls, and a cocktail list that punches well above its weight for the price.

What to order: Cocktails are excellent and notably cheaper than most secret bars — around 9 to 12 pounds. Try their Negroni or any of the house specials. The happy hour offers even better value.

Price range: Budget-friendly for a cocktail bar. 25 to 40 pounds per person.

7. Oriole

Location: Smithfield Market, East Poultry Avenue, EC1A 9LH

How to find it: From the team behind Nightjar, Oriole is hidden in the basement of Smithfield Market. Look for a small sign at street level and descend into a lavish, jungle-themed bar with live music ranging from jazz to gypsy swing. The setting is opulent and transporting.

What to order: The menu is inspired by global exploration, divided into Old World, New World, and Orient sections. Each cocktail is elaborately presented. Try something from the Orient section for the most theatrical experience. Cocktails are 15 to 19 pounds.

Price range: High. Budget 55 to 75 pounds per person.

8. Experimental Cocktail Club (ECC)

Location: 13A Gerrard Street, W1D 5PS

How to find it: Another Chinatown gem. Look for an unmarked black door on Gerrard Street — you might walk past it several times. Ring the bell and head upstairs to a beautifully designed Parisian-style cocktail bar across two floors, with exposed brick, vintage furniture, and exceptional drinks.

What to order: The rotating seasonal menu always impresses. Their twists on classics are inventive without being gimmicky. Try the house Gimlet or whatever the bartender recommends. Cocktails are 13 to 16 pounds.

Price range: Moderate to high. Around 40 to 55 pounds per person.

9. Blind Pig

Location: Above Social Eating House, 58 Poland Street, W1F 7NR

How to find it: Above Jason Atherton's Social Eating House in Soho, look for a small brass pig on the door at street level. Head upstairs to a speakeasy-style bar with low lighting, leather banquettes, and inventive cocktails inspired by British desserts and comfort food.

What to order: The cocktails take inspiration from unexpected sources — think banoffee pie or Eton mess translated into drinks. Creative and delicious. Cocktails are 14 to 17 pounds.

Price range: High. Budget 45 to 65 pounds per person.

10. The Vault at Milroy's

Location: 3 Greek Street, W1D 4NX

How to find it: Enter Milroy's whisky shop on Greek Street in Soho. Browse the shelves of single malts, then look for a hidden door disguised as a bookshelf at the back. Push it open and descend into a candle-lit basement cocktail bar specialising in whisky-based drinks.

What to order: This is a whisky bar at heart, so go with a whisky cocktail. Their Rob Roy and Penicillin are excellent. They also have an impressive selection of rare single malts if you prefer to sip neat. Cocktails are 12 to 16 pounds.

Price range: Moderate to high. 35 to 55 pounds per person depending on whisky choices.

11. Bar Termini

Location: 7 Old Compton Street, W1D 5JE

How to find it: Not strictly hidden, but this tiny Soho bar is so small and unassuming that most people walk straight past. Modelled on an Italian train station bar, it seats only about 20 people and serves exceptional Negronis, espresso, and Italian aperitivi. The atmosphere is intimate and classy.

What to order: Their Negroni is regularly cited as the best in London. They make it with their own house vermouth. The espresso martini is also superb. Cocktails are 11 to 14 pounds.

Price range: Moderate. 25 to 40 pounds per person.

12. Purl

Location: 50-54 Blandford Street, W1U 7HX

How to find it: On a quiet Marylebone side street, look for an unremarkable door with a small sign. Descend into a candlelit basement bar where the cocktails involve molecular mixology techniques — think smoked cocktails, edible bubbles, and drinks served in teapots. The bartenders here are among the most skilled in London.

What to order: Ask for the bartender's recommendation based on your spirit preference. The theatrical presentations are half the fun. Cocktails are 14 to 18 pounds.

Price range: High. Budget 50 to 70 pounds per person.

13. Swift

Location: 12 Old Compton Street, W1D 4TQ

How to find it: The upstairs bar is open and visible, but the real gem is the downstairs bar, accessed via a staircase at the back. The basement is a sophisticated whisky and cocktail lounge with leather seating, candlelight, and an entirely different menu from the floor above. It feels like discovering a different world beneath the Soho streets.

What to order: Downstairs specialises in whisky and aged spirits. Try their Irish Coffee — widely considered the best in London — or a classic Manhattan. Cocktails are 12 to 16 pounds.

Price range: Moderate. 30 to 50 pounds per person.

14. Gordon's Wine Bar

Location: 47 Villiers Street, WC2N 6NE

How to find it: London's oldest wine bar, established in 1890, is not truly hidden but feels like a secret. Descend the stairs near Embankment station into a candlelit cellar with vaulted ceilings, bare brick walls, and centuries of character. There are no electric lights in the cellar — only candles. It is atmospheric beyond description.

What to order: Wine by the glass from the extensive list, or share a bottle. The cheese and charcuterie boards are excellent accompaniments. Wine starts from around 6 pounds a glass.

Price range: Budget-friendly. 20 to 35 pounds per person.

15. Ladies and Gentlemen

Location: 2 Highgate Road, NW5 1NR

How to find it: This bar is literally a converted Victorian public toilet in Kentish Town. Descend the original steps that once led to underground lavatories, and you emerge into a surprisingly stylish cocktail bar that retains some of the original Victorian tiling. It is one of the most unusual bar conversions in London.

What to order: The cocktail list is well-crafted and reasonably priced for London. Classic cocktails are done well. Try a Gimlet or a French 75. Cocktails are 10 to 13 pounds.

Price range: Budget-friendly. 25 to 40 pounds per person.

Tips for Visiting London's Secret Bars

  • Book ahead. Most of these bars are small and popular. Nightjar, Oriole, and Evans and Peel require reservations. Others operate on a first-come basis but can have queues.
  • Dress smart casual. While none have strict dress codes, turning up in sportswear or flip-flops may get you turned away. Smart casual is always safe.
  • Go midweek. Fridays and Saturdays are packed. Tuesday to Thursday evenings offer a better experience with shorter waits.
  • Trust the bartenders. At bars like Nightjar and Purl, telling the bartender what flavours you enjoy and letting them choose will often result in a better drink than picking from the menu.
  • Budget wisely. Cocktails at these bars typically cost 12 to 18 pounds each. Plan for three to four drinks over an evening, plus any food.
  • Combine with dinner. Many of these bars are in Soho or Shoreditch, surrounded by excellent restaurants. Have dinner first, then explore a secret bar as the evening's highlight.
