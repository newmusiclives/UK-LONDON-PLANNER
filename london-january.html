London in January

Bargain hotel rates, legendary sales, and blissfully crowd-free museums make January one of the smartest months to visit.

🌡️ Weather Overview

January is London's coldest month, but it rarely drops to extremes. Here's what to expect:

  • Average temperature: 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F)
  • Rainfall: Around 55mm across 15 rainy days — carry an umbrella but don't expect downpours
  • Daylight hours: Roughly 8 hours, with sunrise around 8:05am and sunset by 4:10pm
  • Snow: Possible but uncommon — perhaps one or two light dustings

Days are short and grey, but the city compensates with warm pubs, cosy cafes, and beautifully lit interiors. The low sun can produce stunning golden-hour light along the Thames in the early afternoon — perfect for photography.

🎭 What's On in January

January may feel quiet after the festive season, but there's plenty happening:

  • London's New Year's Day Parade (1 Jan): Over 10,000 performers march from Piccadilly to Parliament Square. Arrive early for a spot near Trafalgar Square.
  • January Sales (all month): Oxford Street, Regent Street, Selfridges, Harrods, and Westfield all slash prices — some by 70% or more. Boxing Day sales spill well into January.
  • Lumiere London (mid-Jan, biennial): A spectacular free light festival that transforms landmarks across the West End, King's Cross, and South Bank with large-scale illuminated installations.
  • Twelfth Night Celebrations (5-6 Jan): The Shakespeare's Globe and Bankside host traditional celebrations marking the end of Christmas, including a Holly Man procession and wassailing.
  • London Short Film Festival (mid-Jan): Screenings at venues across the city including the ICA, Rich Mix, and BFI Southbank showcase emerging filmmakers.
  • Pantomime Season (until mid-Jan): Catch the tail end of panto season at the Hackney Empire, Palladium, or Theatre Royal Stratford East — a uniquely British tradition.
  • London Art Fair (late Jan): The Business Design Centre in Islington hosts one of the UK's biggest art fairs, featuring over 100 galleries showing modern and contemporary work.
  • Burns Night (25 Jan): Scottish restaurants and pubs across London celebrate Robert Burns with haggis suppers, whisky tastings, and poetry. Try Boisdale of Belgravia or The Ship Tavern in Holborn.

✨ Top Things to Do in January

January rewards visitors with shorter queues, lower prices, and a more local feel. Here are the best ways to spend your time:

  • Explore the British Museum without crowds: January is arguably the best month to visit. You can linger over the Rosetta Stone and Parthenon Marbles without being jostled. Free entry.
  • Warm up in the Barbican Conservatory: London's second-largest conservatory is a hidden tropical oasis. Free to visit on Sundays and select days — check ahead as slots fill up.
  • Walk the South Bank at dusk: The early sunset means you can enjoy the illuminated skyline from Waterloo Bridge or the Thames Path by mid-afternoon.
  • Take afternoon tea: January is ideal for a long, cosy afternoon tea. The Ritz, Claridge's, Sketch, and The Lanesborough all offer memorable experiences — book well ahead.
  • Catch a West End show: January offers the best theatre ticket prices of the year. The TKTS booth in Leicester Square sells same-day discounted seats.
  • Visit the V&A for free: The Victoria and Albert Museum is stunning in winter. The Medieval & Renaissance galleries and the jewellery collection are world-class.
  • Go ice skating: Some rinks, like the Natural History Museum's, stay open into early January. Check dates before you go.
  • Explore Borough Market: London's premier food market is open year-round. In January it's far less hectic — perfect for sampling raclette, fresh oysters, and hot cider.
  • Day trip to Kew Gardens: The glasshouses at Kew are warm and lush in January. The Princess of Wales Conservatory is especially welcome on a cold day.
  • Visit the Churchill War Rooms: This underground museum is one of London's best — and January means shorter queues. Allow at least 2 hours.

🍽️ Where to Eat & Drink

January calls for hearty, warming food. These spots deliver:

  • Rules, Covent Garden: London's oldest restaurant (est. 1798) serves classic British game — venison, grouse, and rabbit — perfect for cold January evenings. Rich interiors and impeccable service.
  • The Lamb & Flag, Covent Garden: One of London's oldest pubs, tucked down an alley. Order a pint of real ale and a pie by the fire.
  • Dishoom, King's Cross: The bacon naan roll at breakfast is legendary, but the full menu of Bombay-inspired comfort food — black daal, chicken ruby — is ideal for a January warm-up. Expect queues; go early.
  • St. John, Smithfield: Fergus Henderson's nose-to-tail restaurant is at its best in winter. Try the roast bone marrow with parsley salad, a signature dish since 1994.
  • Gordon's Wine Bar, Embankment: London's oldest wine bar is candlelit, cave-like, and gloriously atmospheric. Pair a glass of sherry with a cheese board on a drizzly January night.
  • Padella, Borough Market: Fresh handmade pasta at remarkably fair prices. The pici cacio e pepe is a masterclass. No reservations — queue from 11:45am for lunch.

💡 Insider Tips

  • Book hotels for a bargain: January is London's cheapest month for hotels. Rates at 4-star properties can drop 40-50% compared to summer. Book Sunday-Thursday for the best deals.
  • Use Oyster off-peak: Tube fares are cheaper after 9:30am on weekdays. A daily cap on contactless means you'll never overpay.
  • Visit museums on Friday evenings: Many museums host "Late" events — the V&A Friday Late, Science Museum Lates, and Natural History Museum's after-hours events offer a completely different atmosphere, often with DJs and bars.
  • Layer up, don't bulk up: London buildings are well-heated. Wear layers you can peel off indoors. A waterproof outer layer matters more than a heavy coat.
  • Check for January restaurant deals: Many top restaurants run special menus and promotions in January to attract diners during the quiet period. Check OpenTable and SquareMeal for offers.

🎒 What to Pack

  • Warm waterproof coat with a hood
  • Scarf, gloves, and a warm hat
  • Layering pieces — thermal base layers, jumpers, cardigans
  • Waterproof boots or shoes with good grip
  • Compact umbrella
  • Portable phone charger (short days mean more phone use for maps and light)
  • Comfortable walking shoes for long museum visits
