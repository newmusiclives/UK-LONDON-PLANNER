Soho Neighbourhood Guide

Theatreland, global dining, legendary nightlife, and the beating heart of London's West End

Overview

Soho is the epicentre of London's nightlife, dining, and entertainment scene. Bounded by Oxford Street to the north, Regent Street to the west, Shaftesbury Avenue to the south, and Charing Cross Road to the east, this compact district packs an extraordinary density of restaurants, bars, theatres, and independent shops into its grid of narrow streets.

The vibe: Electric and diverse. By day, Soho hums with media workers grabbing coffee, shoppers browsing Carnaby Street, and tourists exploring its colourful lanes. By night, it transforms into London's most vibrant going-out destination with everything from cosy wine bars to legendary live music venues.

Best for: Foodies, theatre lovers, nightlife seekers, LGBTQ+ travellers, and anyone who wants to be at the centre of the action. Soho rewards the curious explorer willing to duck down side streets and discover hidden gems.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Carnaby Street

    Carnaby Street, W1F 9PS

    The iconic pedestrianised shopping street that defined Swinging Sixties London. Today it is home to independent boutiques, flagship stores, and a network of side streets including Kingly Court, a three-storey food and shopping courtyard.

  • 2. Soho Square Gardens

    Soho Square, W1D 3QA

    A charming green oasis in the middle of Soho with a half-timbered Tudor-style gardener's hut at its centre. On sunny days the grass fills with workers and visitors enjoying a rare patch of West End green space.

  • 3. Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club

    47 Frith Street, W1D 4HT

    One of the world's most famous jazz clubs, open since 1959. Legends from Miles Davis to Ella Fitzgerald have graced its intimate stage. Book ahead for evening shows or try for late-night walk-in sets.

  • 4. The Photographers' Gallery

    16-18 Ramillies Street, W1F 7LW

    The UK's leading gallery dedicated to photography, hosting cutting-edge exhibitions across multiple floors. Free before noon, with a well-stocked bookshop and a great cafe.

  • 5. Old Compton Street

    Old Compton Street, W1D

    The beating heart of London's LGBTQ+ scene and one of Soho's most vibrant streets. Lined with cafes, bars, and restaurants, it is a fantastic place to people-watch and soak up the neighbourhood's energy at any hour.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Barrafina

    26-27 Dean Street, W1D 3LL • Spanish tapas • $$

    Michelin-starred Spanish tapas served at a counter. No reservations, so join the queue early for some of the best tortilla, croquetas, and grilled prawns in London.

  • 2. Bao Soho

    53 Lexington Street, W1F 9AS • Taiwanese • $

    Pillowy steamed bao buns with fillings like braised pork and fried chicken. The queue is legendary but moves fast. Their 40-day aged milk bao dessert is unmissable.

  • 3. Kiln

    58 Brewer Street, W1F 9TL • Thai • $$

    Fiery Northern Thai and Burmese cooking over charcoal and wood. The Tamworth pig larb and hand-pulled noodles with smoked chilli relish are extraordinary. Walk-ins only.

  • 4. Kricket Soho

    12 Denman Street, W1D 7HH • Indian • $$

    Modern Indian small plates that are inventive yet deeply flavourful. The samphire pakoras and keralan fried chicken are standouts. Compact basement space adds to the buzz.

  • 5. Wong Kei

    41-43 Wardour Street, W1D 6PY • Cantonese • $

    A Chinatown institution famous for its brusque service (now softened) and enormous portions of excellent Cantonese classics at bargain prices. Spread across four floors. Cash preferred.

Best Bars & Pubs

  • 1. The French House

    49 Dean Street, W1D 5BG

    Soho's most storied pub, where the Free French planned resistance in WWII and Dylan Thomas and Francis Bacon drank. They only serve half pints of beer and have an excellent wine list. No phones allowed at the bar.

  • 2. Bar Termini

    7 Old Compton Street, W1D 5JE

    A tiny Italian-inspired cocktail bar with just 24 seats. Impeccable Negronis, outstanding espresso martinis, and a short menu of amaro-forward drinks. Walk in and grab a stool.

  • 3. Swift

    12 Old Compton Street, W1D 4TQ

    A two-floor cocktail bar. Upstairs is a bright space for quick pre-theatre drinks and aperitifs. Downstairs is a moody, intimate lounge for serious cocktail connoisseurs. Consistently ranked among London's best bars.

  • 4. The Dog and Duck

    18 Bateman Street, W1D 3AJ

    A beautifully preserved Victorian pub with ornate tiling and etched mirrors. George Orwell and John Constable were regulars. Small and gets packed, but full of character. Great cask ales.

  • 5. Experimental Cocktail Club

    13A Gerrard Street, W1D 5PS

    Hidden above a Chinatown street behind an unmarked door, this speakeasy-style bar serves creative, beautifully presented cocktails in a low-lit space. Ring the buzzer to enter.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Bar Italia

    22 Frith Street, W1D 4RF

    Open since 1949 and serving strong Italian espresso nearly 24 hours a day. A Soho institution with a retro interior, pavement seating, and an unbeatable people-watching spot.

  • 2. Flat White

    17 Berwick Street, W1F 0PT

    The cafe that helped launch London's specialty coffee revolution. New Zealand-inspired with excellent flat whites (of course) and a relaxed atmosphere on the edge of Berwick Street Market.

  • 3. Maison Bertaux

    28 Greek Street, W1D 5DQ

    London's oldest French patisserie, open since 1871. Charmingly eccentric with wobbly tables, art exhibitions upstairs, and outstanding croissants and cakes. Pure old Soho character.

Shopping Highlights

  • Carnaby Street & Kingly Court — Independent fashion, streetwear, and a three-storey food courtyard tucked behind the main drag.
  • Berwick Street Market — One of London's oldest street markets (since the 1770s), now mixing vinyl record shops, fabric stores, and fresh produce stalls.
  • Liberty London — The Tudor-revival department store just off Carnaby Street, famous for its floral print fabrics and beautifully curated homeware.
  • Sounds of the Universe — A Broadwick Street record shop specialising in rare soul, reggae, funk, and electronic music from around the world.
  • Foyles — The flagship Charing Cross Road bookshop spanning five floors with an excellent events programme and a top-floor cafe.

Getting There

Soho is one of the best-connected areas in London, with multiple Tube stations at its edges:

  • Tottenham Court Road (Central, Northern, Elizabeth lines) — east side of Soho
  • Leicester Square (Northern, Piccadilly lines) — south side
  • Piccadilly Circus (Bakerloo, Piccadilly lines) — southwest corner
  • Oxford Circus (Victoria, Central, Bakerloo lines) — north side

Insider Tips

  • Visit Berwick Street Market on a weekday lunchtime for the best street food with fewer crowds.
  • For West End shows, check the TKTS booth in Leicester Square for same-day half-price tickets rather than buying online at full price.
  • Soho's side streets and alleys hide the best gems. Wander down Meard Street, Bateman Street, and the passage from Kingly Street to Carnaby Street.
  • Pre-theatre dining deals (typically 5-7pm) at Soho restaurants can save you up to 50% compared to the a la carte menu.
  • The area between Old Compton Street and Shaftesbury Avenue is packed with excellent dim sum restaurants offering affordable lunch sets.
