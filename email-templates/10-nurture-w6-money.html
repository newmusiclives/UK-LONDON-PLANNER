Tipping, taxes & money: the American's complete guide

Tipping — much less than home

  • Restaurants: 12.5% service is usually on the bill already. Add nothing. If it's not included, 10% is generous.
  • Pubs: £0. Nobody tips at the bar. Food served at a table? Same — unless service is added.
  • Taxis: Round up to the nearest pound.
  • Hotels: £1–£2 for a porter. Housekeeping rarely tipped.
  • Cafés/coffee shops: There's a jar sometimes. Don't worry about it.

Taxes — already baked in

20% VAT is included in every price tag. The number on the menu is the number you pay. No mental math at checkout. (Note: the UK scrapped tourist VAT refunds in 2021 — you don't get sales tax back at the airport any more.)

Card vs cash — go card

London is ~99% contactless. Black cabs, markets, pubs, Tube, even street buskers take contactless. Cash is fine but never necessary. Carry £20–£40 for emergencies.

Best cards for Americans

  • Wise or Revolut — zero FX fee, mid-market rate, contactless. Open online before you fly.
  • Chase Sapphire Preferred/Reserve — zero foreign transaction fee, good points
  • Capital One Venture — zero foreign fee, simple points

Avoid: using your regular debit card at ATMs — 3% fees plus a flat £5 charge is common.

Dynamic Currency Conversion — say no

At card machines in restaurants/shops, you'll be asked "Pay in GBP or USD?" Always choose GBP. USD uses a garbage exchange rate that costs you 4–7%.
