Hidden London: the spots only locals know (and we're sharing them)

Every Londoner has a list of places they secretly resent telling tourists about. Here are seven from ours.

1. Leighton House (Kensington)

A 19th-century artist's house with a Moorish tiled hall that looks like it belongs in Marrakech. Ten minutes from the V&A. Barely busy. £13 well spent.

2. Gordon's Wine Bar (Embankment)

London's oldest wine bar. Candlelit vaults under a tunnel. You descend into it. Arrive by 5pm or queue. Cheese boards and port, that's the move.

3. Daunt Books (Marylebone)

An Edwardian bookshop with a stained-glass skylight and a whole wall of travel writing. Better than Shakespeare & Co without the queue.

4. Kyoto Garden (Holland Park)

A tiny Japanese garden inside a West London park. Koi, peacocks, a waterfall. Get there before 10am and you'll have it to yourself.

5. Sir John Soane's Museum

A 19th-century architect's house, left exactly as he died in it. Rooms stuffed with antiquities, hidden panels, an Egyptian sarcophagus in the basement. Free. Booking helps.

6. The Eagle (Farringdon)

The pub that invented the gastropub in 1991. Still great. Steak sandwich at the bar, pint of Bombardier. Pure London.

7. Little Venice walk

Walk the canal from Warwick Avenue to Camden Lock. 45 min. Narrowboats, bridges, barely any tourists, cafés along the way. London's best free activity.
