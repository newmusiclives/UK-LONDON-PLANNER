Dog-Friendly London: Where to Go With Your Four-Legged Friend

London is one of Europe's most dog-friendly cities. Here is everything you need to know about exploring the capital with your canine companion.

London Loves Dogs

Londoners are famously devoted to their dogs, and the city reflects it. Most pubs welcome well-behaved dogs, many restaurants have outdoor (and sometimes indoor) dog-friendly seating, and the parks are some of the best urban green spaces in the world. Whether you are visiting with your own dog or just want to know where the good boys and girls hang out, this guide covers every angle.

Dog-Friendly Pubs

The great British pub and dogs go together like real ale and crisps. Most London pubs welcome dogs in the bar area (not always the dining room), but these are particularly well set up for four-legged visitors.

The Lamb & Flag, Covent Garden

Address: 33 Rose Street, WC2E 9EB • Nearest Tube: Covent Garden

One of London's oldest pubs (established 1623) and genuinely dog-friendly throughout the ground floor bar. They keep water bowls behind the bar and staff are known to hand out the occasional treat. The cosy interior with its dark wood panelling and low ceilings makes it a wonderful place to shelter with a pint on a rainy day. Dogs must be kept on leads inside.

The Churchill Arms, Kensington

Address: 119 Kensington Church Street, W8 7LN • Nearest Tube: Notting Hill Gate

Famous for its extraordinary flower-covered exterior and Churchill memorabilia inside, this Fuller's pub welcomes dogs in the front bar area. The main bar is a wonderful spot to sit with your dog while enjoying a pint of London Pride. Note that the Thai kitchen at the rear does not allow dogs. Water bowls are provided on request.

The Flask, Hampstead

Address: 14 Flask Walk, NW3 1HE • Nearest Tube: Hampstead

Perfectly positioned at the foot of Hampstead Heath, this is the quintessential post-walk pub. Dogs are welcome in the main bar and the beer garden. On weekends you will find more dogs than people in the garden. It is a lovely spot after a long walk across the Heath — grab a table outside in summer and let your dog rest under the bench.

The Spaniards Inn, Hampstead

Address: Spaniards Road, NW3 7JJ • Nearest Tube: Hampstead (then walk)

A legendary 16th-century pub right on the edge of Hampstead Heath with a huge beer garden that is pure dog paradise. Mentioned by Dickens and Keats, it has a genuinely warm welcome for dogs — water bowls, treats at the bar, and plenty of muddy-pawed regulars. The garden is spacious enough that dogs can relax off-lead if well-behaved.

The Narrowboat, Islington

Address: 119 St Peter's Street, N1 8PZ • Nearest Tube: Angel

A canal-side pub with a terrace overlooking Regent's Canal. Dogs are welcome inside and on the terrace. It is a great stop on a canal-side walk. The towpath walk from here to Camden or King's Cross is one of London's best dog walks.

The Windmill, Clapham Common

Address: Clapham Common South Side, SW4 9DE • Nearest Tube: Clapham Common

Right on the edge of Clapham Common, this Young's pub has a large beer garden that is a haven for dog walkers. Dogs welcome inside the bar area and throughout the garden. Sunday afternoons here with a roast and a Labrador at your feet is peak London living.

Dog-Friendly Parks & Walks

Hyde Park & Kensington Gardens

Size: 350 acres combined • Off-lead: Yes, in most areas (check signage)

Dogs are welcome throughout both parks and can go off-lead in most areas. There are designated dog-free zones near the Diana Memorial Playground and certain flower gardens — look for signs. The Serpentine provides a swimming spot for water-loving dogs. The best circuit is from Hyde Park Corner along Rotten Row to the Italian Gardens and back via the Serpentine — about 3 miles.

Hampstead Heath

Size: 790 acres • Off-lead: Yes, in most areas

The ultimate London dog walk. Wild, hilly, and enormous, the Heath gives dogs the closest thing to countryside within the city. Parliament Hill offers panoramic city views. The Hampstead Ponds are available for dog swimming (not the designated bathing ponds). A full circuit takes about 2 hours and is magnificently varied — woodland, meadows, hills, and ponds.

Richmond Park

Size: 2,500 acres • Off-lead: Yes, but dogs MUST be under close control near deer

London's largest park and home to 630 free-roaming deer. Dogs are welcome but must be kept under close control at all times — this means immediate recall, not just a long lead. During deer birthing season (May–July) and rutting season (September–November), it is strongly recommended to keep dogs on leads. The Isabella Plantation is a beautiful enclosed woodland garden where dogs must be on leads.

Victoria Park, Hackney

Size: 218 acres • Off-lead: Yes, in designated areas

East London's most beloved green space, known as "the People's Park." There is a dedicated dog-walking area and dogs are welcome off-lead in most of the park. The canal towpath walk connecting to the park is a great warm-up route. On weekends, the park has a popular dog-walking community around the lake area. The farmers' market by the Crown Gate entrance (Sundays) is dog-friendly.

Regent's Canal Towpath Walk

Distance: 8.6 miles (Camden to Limehouse) • Surface: Flat, paved

Not a park but one of London's best urban dog walks. The towpath runs from Little Venice through Camden Lock, past King's Cross, through Islington, Hackney, and down to Limehouse Basin. Dogs should be on leads (the path is shared with cyclists) but it is a fantastic linear walk with plenty of pub stops along the way.

Dog-Friendly Restaurants & Cafes

Counter-Service & Cafes

  • Dishoom (King's Cross): Dogs are welcome in the outdoor courtyard area. The chai is famously good, and your dog gets a water bowl.
  • Gail's Bakery (various locations): Most branches welcome dogs inside or at outdoor tables. A favourite of London's dog-walking community.
  • The Gentlemen Baristas (various): Excellent coffee and dogs welcome at all locations. They provide water bowls and dog treats at the counter.
  • Caravan (King's Cross): The outdoor terrace overlooking Granary Square is perfect for dogs. Great brunch and all-day dining.

Sit-Down Restaurants

  • The Ivy Chelsea Garden: Dogs welcome in the garden terrace area. Book specifically for the terrace when making reservations.
  • Le Pont de la Tour (Tower Bridge): Dogs welcome on the riverside terrace. Stunning setting and the staff are charming with animals.
  • Rochelle Canteen (Shoreditch): The outdoor courtyard is dog-friendly. Simple, excellent British cooking in a converted school bike shed.
  • The Wolseley (Piccadilly): Typically allows small dogs in the main dining room — call ahead to confirm. One of the most elegant dog-friendly options in London.

Dog-Friendly Shops & Department Stores

  • Liberty (Regent Street): Well-behaved dogs on leads are welcome throughout this beautiful Tudor-revival department store. It is one of the most elegant places to shop with a dog in London.
  • Selfridges (Oxford Street): Dogs are welcome on leads. They even have a dog accessories section and occasionally host dog events.
  • Harrods (Knightsbridge): Small dogs are permitted if carried or in a bag. Larger dogs are not allowed.
  • Anthropologie (Regent Street and King's Road): Dogs on leads welcome in all London stores.
  • Most independent bookshops: Small shops like Daunt Books, John Sandoe, and Persephone Books generally welcome dogs — just ask at the door.
  • Borough Market: Dogs on leads are welcome throughout the market. Busy at weekends so keep your dog close.
  • Columbia Road Flower Market (Sundays): Dogs allowed but it gets extremely crowded. Best for confident, calm dogs only. Early morning (8 am) is quietest.

Dogs on the Tube, Buses & Trains

London Underground (the Tube)

Dogs are welcome on the Tube free of charge. There is no size restriction — all dogs are allowed provided they are on a lead or carried. Most Londoners keep their dogs close on a short lead. Avoid rush hours (7.30–9.30 am and 5–7 pm) as the crush is stressful for dogs and humans alike. The quietest times to travel are mid-morning and mid-afternoon.

Escalator tip: Carry small dogs on escalators. Larger dogs should use the lift — their paws can get caught in escalator mechanisms. Most major stations have lifts, but not all — check the TfL step-free Tube guide before travelling.

London Buses

Dogs are allowed on London buses at the driver's discretion. Small dogs should be carried; larger dogs should sit in the lower deck. Guide dogs are always permitted. In practice, most drivers are fine with well-behaved dogs, but do not rely on the bus if you have a tight schedule.

Overground, Elizabeth Line & National Rail

Dogs travel free on the Overground and Elizabeth Line. On National Rail services, up to two dogs per passenger travel free. Dogs must be on leads and kept off seats. First-class carriages vary by operator — check before travelling.

Thames Clippers (River Bus)

Dogs are welcome on Thames Clippers river buses free of charge. This is actually one of the most enjoyable ways to travel with a dog — sit on the open rear deck and enjoy the river views together.

Dog-Friendly Hotels

An increasing number of London hotels welcome dogs, though most charge a supplement (typically £25–£50 per night).

  • The Egerton House Hotel (Knightsbridge): Famously dog-obsessed. Dogs get their own bed, bowl set, and treats. Walking services available. From £350/night, no dog supplement.
  • The Hoxton (Shoreditch, Southwark, Holborn): Dogs stay free. Each property has a relaxed, urban vibe and the staff love dogs. From £150/night.
  • Kimpton Fitzroy (Russell Square): No size or breed restrictions. Dogs get a welcome kit with bed, bowls, and treats. No supplement. From £200/night.
  • The Zetter Townhouse (Clerkenwell): Boutique hotel that welcomes dogs with character. Quirky, eccentric decor and genuinely warm service. From £250/night, small dog supplement.
  • Premier Inn (various): Budget-friendly option. Dogs allowed at most (not all) London locations for £25/night supplement. Book a dog-friendly room when reserving.
  • The Laslett (Notting Hill): Stylish boutique hotel with a dedicated dog-friendly policy. Near Kensington Gardens and Hyde Park. From £200/night.

Practical Tips

  • Water: Always carry a collapsible water bowl. London has public water fountains in most large parks but they are less common on streets.
  • Waste bags: Carry plenty. London has strict fines for not cleaning up after your dog (up to £1,000 in some boroughs). Most parks have free bag dispensers but do not count on them.
  • Pavements in summer: London pavements get extremely hot in heatwaves. If it is too hot for your hand, it is too hot for paws. Walk early morning or after 7 pm in hot weather.
  • Lead laws: There is no universal lead law in London, but many boroughs have Public Space Protection Orders requiring leads in certain areas. Follow signage.
  • Emergency vet: The PDSA Pet Hospital (Victoria) and Vets Now (various locations) provide 24-hour emergency care. Save the number before your trip.
  • Dog-sitting: If you need a dog-free afternoon for a museum or show, services like Tailster and Rover connect you with vetted local dog sitters, often available same-day.
