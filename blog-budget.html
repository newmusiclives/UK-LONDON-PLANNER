London on a Budget: 50 Free Things to Do

London is one of the world's most expensive cities — but it is also one of the most generous with free attractions

London has a reputation as an expensive city, and it can be. A pint in Zone 1 costs 7 pounds, a Tube ride can set you back nearly 3 pounds, and hotel rooms rarely dip below 100 pounds a night. But here is a secret that seasoned London visitors know: the city offers an extraordinary number of completely free experiences. World-class museums, stunning parks, historic markets, panoramic viewpoints, and cultural events — all without spending a penny. Here are 50 genuinely free things to do in London, organized by category, plus our best tips for saving money on everything else.

Free Museums and Galleries (1-12)

London's free museum policy is one of the best things about the city. These are not second-rate institutions — they are among the finest museums on the planet.

  1. British Museum — Home to the Rosetta Stone, Egyptian mummies, and the Elgin Marbles. One of the most visited museums in the world.
  2. National Gallery — Masterpieces by Van Gogh, Monet, Rembrandt, and da Vinci in Trafalgar Square.
  3. Tate Modern — Contemporary art in a converted power station on the South Bank. The Turbine Hall installations are always spectacular.
  4. Tate Britain — The nation's collection of British art from 1500 to the present, including the Turner Prize exhibition.
  5. Victoria and Albert Museum — The world's largest museum of decorative arts and design, from medieval armour to fashion.
  6. Natural History Museum — Dinosaur skeletons, the blue whale model, and stunning architecture. The building itself is a masterpiece.
  7. Science Museum — Interactive exhibits, the space gallery, and the information age exhibition. Brilliant for all ages.
  8. Imperial War Museum — Moving exhibits on World War I, World War II, and the Holocaust. Deeply powerful and educational.
  9. Museum of London — The complete history of London from prehistoric times to the present day.
  10. National Maritime Museum — Britain's seafaring history in beautiful Greenwich.
  11. Sir John Soane's Museum — A hidden gem in Lincoln's Inn Fields. An architect's eccentric personal collection crammed into a townhouse.
  12. Wellcome Collection — Fascinating exhibitions exploring the connections between medicine, life, and art.

Free Parks and Gardens (13-22)

London is one of the greenest capital cities in the world. Nearly half its area is green space, and the royal parks are spectacular.

  1. Hyde Park — 350 acres of green space in central London. Visit Speakers' Corner on Sundays, row on the Serpentine, or just lie on the grass.
  2. Regent's Park — Beautiful rose gardens (free), open-air theatre in summer, and views of the Nash terraces.
  3. St James's Park — The prettiest of the royal parks, with views of Buckingham Palace and pelicans on the lake.
  4. Greenwich Park — Panoramic views of Canary Wharf and the Thames from the hilltop by the Royal Observatory.
  5. Hampstead Heath — Wild, hilly parkland in north London. The view from Parliament Hill is one of the best in the city.
  6. Richmond Park — Over 2,000 acres of wild deer, ancient oak trees, and the Isabella Plantation with its azaleas and rhododendrons.
  7. Kensington Gardens — The Italian Gardens, the Peter Pan statue, and the Serpentine Gallery (also free).
  8. Victoria Park — East London's favourite park with canals, a Chinese pagoda, and a weekend farmers' market.
  9. Kyoto Garden in Holland Park — A serene Japanese garden with waterfalls and koi carp, hidden in one of London's poshest neighbourhoods.
  10. Barbican Conservatory — A tropical greenhouse hidden inside the brutalist Barbican Centre. Free but book a time slot online.

Free Markets and Neighbourhoods (23-32)

Wandering London's markets costs nothing (unless you succumb to the incredible food). The atmosphere and people-watching are the real attraction.

  1. Borough Market — London's oldest food market. Free to browse, and many stalls offer free samples.
  2. Camden Market — Eclectic stalls, street art, and canalside atmosphere in one of London's most vibrant neighbourhoods.
  3. Columbia Road Flower Market — Every Sunday morning, this East London street transforms into a riot of colour and flowers.
  4. Portobello Road Market — Antiques, vintage clothing, and the colourful houses of Notting Hill.
  5. Brick Lane — Street art, vintage shops, curry houses, and a Sunday market. Walk the length for free murals by world-famous artists.
  6. Covent Garden — Free street performers, the Apple Market, and beautiful architecture in the old flower market.
  7. Leadenhall Market — A stunning Victorian covered market in the City. Appeared as Diagon Alley in the Harry Potter films.
  8. Bermondsey Street — Independent shops, galleries, and the White Cube gallery, all free to explore.
  9. Neal's Yard — A tiny, impossibly colourful courtyard hidden in Covent Garden. Perfect for photos.
  10. Maltby Street Market — A smaller, less touristy alternative to Borough Market under the railway arches in Bermondsey.

Free Viewpoints (33-38)

Skip the paid observation decks. These free viewpoints offer equally impressive (sometimes better) panoramas of the London skyline.

  1. Sky Garden — The highest public garden in London at 155 metres. Panoramic views in every direction. Free but must book online.
  2. Tate Modern Viewing Level — Level 10 of the Blavatnik Building offers sweeping views of St Paul's and the Thames.
  3. One New Change Rooftop — A shopping centre roof terrace directly opposite St Paul's Cathedral. Extraordinary close-up view.
  4. Parliament Hill — The classic panoramic view of the London skyline from Hampstead Heath.
  5. Primrose Hill — Another stunning viewpoint in north London, popular at sunset.
  6. Greenwich Observatory Hill — Views of the Queen's House, the Old Royal Naval College, and Canary Wharf.

Free Walks and Experiences (39-50)

  1. South Bank Walk — Walk from Westminster Bridge to Tower Bridge along the Thames. Pass the London Eye, Tate Modern, Shakespeare's Globe, and Borough Market.
  2. Changing of the Guard — The iconic ceremony at Buckingham Palace. Check dates as it does not happen every day.
  3. Ceremony of the Keys — The 700-year-old nightly locking of the Tower of London. Free but must apply for tickets months in advance.
  4. Lunchtime Concerts at St Martin-in-the-Fields — Free classical music concerts every Monday, Tuesday, and Friday at this Trafalgar Square church.
  5. Evensong at St Paul's Cathedral — Attend a choral evensong service to experience St Paul's for free. The music and acoustics are sublime.
  6. Walk the Regent's Canal — From Little Venice to Camden Lock through Regent's Park. A peaceful, traffic-free walk through hidden London.
  7. Explore the Inns of Court — The historic legal quarter of London. Beautiful courtyards, gardens, and medieval buildings tucked behind Fleet Street.
  8. Watch Skateboarding at the Southbank Undercroft — This graffiti-covered skate spot under the Southbank Centre is a London institution.
  9. Cross the Millennium Bridge — The wobbly bridge from Tate Modern to St Paul's Cathedral. One of the best views in London.
  10. Attend a Debate at Speakers' Corner — Every Sunday in Hyde Park, anyone can stand on a soapbox and speak. A London tradition since 1872.
  11. Walk through Little Venice — Colourful canal boats, waterside cafes, and a surprisingly tranquil spot in west London.
  12. Visit the Platform 9 and Three Quarters Trolley — At King's Cross station, a free photo opportunity with the half-disappeared luggage trolley from Harry Potter.

Money-Saving Tips Beyond Free Attractions

Transport Savings

  • Use contactless or Oyster — never buy single paper tickets, which cost roughly double.
  • Travel off-peak — journeys before 6:30 AM or after 9:30 AM on weekdays are significantly cheaper.
  • Walk between central zones — Westminster to Covent Garden is 15 minutes on foot but 2 Tube stops.
  • Take the bus — buses are cheaper than the Tube and you see more of the city. The number 11 route passes Big Ben, Trafalgar Square, and St Paul's.
  • Use Santander Cycles — the bike-share scheme costs just 1.65 pounds to unlock, then the first 30 minutes of every ride are free.

Food Savings

  • Eat at markets — street food at Borough, Camden, or Maltby Street costs 6 to 10 pounds for a full meal.
  • Use the Too Good To Go app — grab unsold food from cafes and restaurants at a steep discount.
  • Lunch sets and pre-theatre menus — many upscale restaurants offer 2-course lunches for 15 to 20 pounds.
  • Shop at Tesco, Sainsbury's, or Marks and Spencer — supermarket meal deals (sandwich, drink, and snack) cost around 3 to 5 pounds.
  • Eat early — pre-theatre dinner menus typically run from 5 to 6:30 PM and offer significant savings.

Entertainment Savings

  • TKTS booth in Leicester Square — same-day West End tickets at up to 50 percent off.
  • Rush tickets and day seats — many theatres release cheap seats on the morning of performance.
  • Free comedy nights — Angel Comedy Club and several pubs offer free stand-up comedy nights throughout the week.
  • Lunchtime concerts — free classical music at churches across the city, including St Martin-in-the-Fields and St James's Piccadilly.
  • Visit the cinema on Tuesdays — most London cinemas offer discounted tickets midweek.
