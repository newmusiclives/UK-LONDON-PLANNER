London in September

The crowds thin, the cultural season roars back to life, and early autumn brings a golden glow to the city.

Weather Overview

Average High: 19 °C (66 °F)  |  Average Low: 12 °C (54 °F)

Rainfall: Around 50 mm across 8–10 rain days. Showers become more frequent as the month progresses.

Daylight: 12–14 hours. Days shorten noticeably, with sunset moving from around 7:45 pm in early September to 6:45 pm by month's end.

What to expect: September often delivers some of London's loveliest weather. The first half can feel like an extension of summer, with warm, sunny days perfect for walking. By late September, there's a distinct crispness in the air and the first hints of autumn colour appear in the parks.

What's On in September

  • London Design Festival — A city-wide celebration of design with installations, exhibitions, and talks across hundreds of venues. The V&A serves as the festival hub with major commissions in its courtyard and galleries.
  • Open House London (mid-September weekend) — Over 800 buildings normally closed to the public open their doors for free. Explore private homes, government buildings, skyscrapers, and architectural gems. Book popular buildings early; many are first-come, first-served.
  • Totally Thames — A month-long festival celebrating the River Thames with art installations, river races, guided walks, and community events along its banks.
  • BBC Proms — Last Night (mid-September) — The iconic finale at the Royal Albert Hall with flag-waving, sing-alongs, and fireworks. Thousands gather in Hyde Park to watch on big screens for free.
  • London Fashion Week — While the main shows are industry-only, there are public events, pop-up shops, and a general buzz of style across Soho and the West End.
  • Thames Festival Trust events — The Great River Race, a 21-mile rowing race from Millwall to Ham, is a spectacular sight with hundreds of traditional boats.
  • Back-to-culture season — West End theatres launch new shows, galleries open major autumn exhibitions, and the Royal Opera House, English National Opera, and London orchestras begin new seasons.

Top Things to Do

  • Walk through early autumn colours — Kew Gardens, Richmond Park, and Hampstead Heath begin their golden transformation. The light is softer and the parks less crowded than summer.
  • Explore Open House buildings — See inside the Gherkin, the Barbican's hidden conservatory, Battersea Power Station, private members' clubs, and modernist homes. It's one of London's best free weekends.
  • Catch a new West End show — September marks the start of the theatrical season with premieres and previews. Tickets for previews are often cheaper than once reviews are published.
  • Visit the V&A for London Design Festival — The museum hosts large-scale installations in its grand entrance hall and across its galleries. The surrounding streets of South Kensington buzz with design events.
  • Cycle along the Thames — Mild temperatures make September ideal for cycling. The Thames Path from Richmond to Hampton Court is scenic and mostly flat, or try the Santander Cycles for a central London loop.
  • Borough Market's autumn produce — September brings British apples, pears, plums, wild mushrooms, and game. Stallholders offer tastings and the atmosphere is wonderful on crisp autumn mornings.
  • Evening walk across Millennium Bridge — September sunsets over St Paul's Cathedral are stunning from this pedestrian bridge. Continue to the Tate Modern or along the South Bank.
  • Day trip to the Cotswolds — The rolling hills start to turn golden. Trains to Moreton-in-Marsh take 90 minutes; from there, explore honey-stone villages like Bourton-on-the-Water and Stow-on-the-Wold.
  • Discover hidden London on foot — The comfortable temperatures are perfect for guided walking tours. Try a street art tour in Shoreditch, a Jack the Ripper walk in Whitechapel, or a Harry Potter filming locations tour.
  • Afternoon tea — As the weather cools, this becomes even more appealing. The Savoy, Claridge's, and Sketch are iconic; for something different, try the themed teas at The Berkeley or Mr Fogg's.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • The Palomar (Soho) — Modern Jerusalem cuisine. Sit at the zinc counter bar for the best experience and watch the chefs work. The polenta with truffle and mushroom is outstanding in autumn.
  • St. JOHN (Smithfield) — Fergus Henderson's nose-to-tail pioneer. September brings game season — look for grouse, partridge, and wood pigeon on the ever-changing menu.
  • Rochelle Canteen (Shoreditch) — Hidden inside a former school bike shed, this gem serves simple, seasonal British cooking. The courtyard garden is delightful in early autumn sunshine.
  • Pub Sunday roasts — September is when a good roast really hits the spot again. The Harwood Arms (Fulham), The Eagle (Farringdon), and The Marksman (Hackney) all serve exceptional versions.
  • Wine bars for cooler evenings — Noble Rot (Bloomsbury and Soho), Sager + Wilde (Hackney), and Gordon's Wine Bar (Embankment, London's oldest) are perfect as the evenings draw in.
  • Coal Drops Yard restaurants (King's Cross) — This beautifully restored Victorian complex houses Barrafina, Hicce, and other excellent restaurants alongside independent shops.

Insider Tips

  • September is a sweet spot for value — Hotel prices drop after the August peak while the weather is still pleasant. You'll find better deals on flights and accommodation, especially after the first week when schools return.
  • Open House London books up fast — The most popular buildings (like the Gherkin observation deck) require advance booking and slots fill within minutes. Set a reminder for when bookings open and have a backup list.
  • Layer up — September mornings can be chilly (10–12 °C) but afternoons warm and sunny. Layers you can add and remove are essential.
  • Last chance for outdoor dining — Many restaurants keep their terraces open through September. Make the most of it before the winter closure; rooftop bars start winding down by month's end.
  • Transport tip — With schools back and tourists thinning, September is one of the best months to navigate the Tube comfortably outside rush hours.

What to Pack

  • Layers — a mix of t-shirts, long sleeves, and a medium-weight jacket
  • A waterproof jacket or trench coat (rain becomes more regular)
  • Comfortable walking shoes that can handle damp pavements
  • A light scarf for cooler evenings
  • Sunglasses — autumn sunshine can be low and blinding
  • A tote bag for market finds and design festival purchases
