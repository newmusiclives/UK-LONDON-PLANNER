London in August

Carnival energy, family-friendly festivals, outdoor cinema, and the last long days of a London summer.

Weather Overview

Average High: 22 °C (72 °F)  |  Average Low: 14 °C (57 °F)

Rainfall: Around 50 mm across 9–11 rain days. August is historically London's wettest summer month, though rain tends to come in bursts.

Daylight: 14–16 hours. Sunrise around 5:30 am early in the month, sunset around 8:30 pm. Days noticeably shorten by late August.

What to expect: Similar temperatures to July but often more humid. Thunderstorms can develop quickly on hot afternoons. The late August bank holiday weekend is one of the busiest periods of the year, especially around Notting Hill.

What's On in August

  • Notting Hill Carnival (late August bank holiday weekend) — Europe's largest street festival. Two million people, Caribbean food, steel bands, sound systems, and a spectacular parade. Sunday is family day; Monday is the main parade.
  • All Points East (Victoria Park) — Multi-day music festival in east London with top-tier headliners and free midweek neighbourhood events in between.
  • Film4 Summer Screen at Somerset House — Open-air cinema screenings in the stunning neoclassical courtyard. Films range from cult classics to new releases, with DJs and food stalls.
  • Kids Week (throughout August) — Children go free to top West End shows when accompanied by a paying adult. An incredible deal for families visiting London.
  • BBC Proms (continues from July) — The classical season is in full swing at the Royal Albert Hall with nightly concerts and lunchtime recitals.
  • South Bank Summer Festival — Free outdoor performances, installations, and family activities along the Thames at the Southbank Centre.
  • Regent's Park Open Air Theatre — The summer season continues with evening performances. Bring a picnic to enjoy in the park beforehand.
  • London Craft Beer Festival — Sample hundreds of beers from the UK's best independent breweries at this popular late-summer event.

Top Things to Do

  • Explore London's parks at their fullest — Richmond Park's deer roam freely, Hampstead Heath's swimming ponds are at their best, and the rose gardens in Regent's Park and Hyde Park are still in bloom.
  • Visit the museums (and escape the heat) — The British Museum, V&A, Natural History Museum, and Science Museum are all free and beautifully air-conditioned. August school holidays mean special family programmes.
  • Take a canal boat from Little Venice to Camden — A 45-minute narrowboat journey along Regent's Canal, passing through the zoo and London's hidden waterways. A peaceful escape from the summer crowds.
  • Day trip to Brighton — Just one hour by train from Victoria or London Bridge. Hit the beach, stroll the Lanes, and ride the Brighton Palace Pier rides.
  • Outdoor swimming — Beyond the Serpentine, try the Hampstead Heath ponds, Brockwell Lido in Brixton, London Fields Lido in Hackney, or the Parliament Hill Lido.
  • Walk the Jubilee Greenway — This 60-km trail loops through London's best green spaces and landmarks. Tackle it in sections; the Regent's Park to Little Venice stretch is particularly lovely in summer.
  • Catch a play at the Globe — Shakespeare's Globe Theatre runs its open-air season. Groundling tickets are just £5 and the August repertoire is usually at its broadest.
  • Night markets and street food — Brick Lane's Sunday Upmarket, Mercato Metropolitano in Elephant & Castle, and Dinerama in Shoreditch are all buzzing on warm August evenings.
  • Explore Greenwich — Visit the Cutty Sark, stand on the Prime Meridian line, enjoy the views from the Royal Observatory, and stroll through Greenwich Park.
  • Kayak on the Thames — Paddle from Battersea or Paddington Basin for a completely different perspective of the city. Several operators run guided sunset sessions in summer.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • Carnival food at Notting Hill — Jerk chicken, curried goat, fried plantain, rum punch, and coconut drops from street vendors. The food is half the reason to go.
  • Gelupo (Soho) — Possibly London's best gelato, with creative seasonal flavours like ricotta and sour cherry alongside classics. Perfect for a hot August afternoon.
  • Borough Market — Open Monday to Saturday. In August, look for British strawberries, cherries, and stone fruits at their peak alongside the year-round favourites.
  • The Anchor & Hope (Waterloo) — Renowned gastropub with no-reservations lunches. Hearty British cooking using seasonal ingredients. A post-Southbank walk reward.
  • Rooftop bars — Frank's Cafe on a Peckham car park, Netil360 in Hackney, and Jin Bo Law at the Dorsett City Hotel all offer drinks with spectacular skyline views on warm nights.

Insider Tips

  • Notting Hill Carnival strategy — Arrive early (before noon), wear comfortable shoes you don't mind getting dirty, carry cash for food, and keep valuables secure. Plan your exit route; roads close and the Tube gets extremely busy.
  • Family savings — Kids Week offers extend beyond theatre. Many attractions run reduced family tickets and free workshops during the school holidays.
  • Beat the tourist crowds — August is peak tourism. Visit major attractions at opening time or after 4 pm. The Tower of London and Buckingham Palace are especially busy; book timed tickets online.
  • The Tube is quieter in August — Many Londoners leave the city during the school holidays, so commuter rush hours are noticeably less packed than usual.
  • Bank holiday planning — The late August bank holiday Monday means a three-day weekend. Trains out of London fill up fast, so book in advance if you're planning day trips.

What to Pack

  • Light summer clothing — shorts, t-shirts, sundresses
  • A packable rain jacket or compact umbrella (August showers are common)
  • Sunscreen and a hat for sunny days in the parks
  • Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes
  • A light cardigan or hoodie for air-conditioned museums and cooler evenings
  • Swimwear if you plan to use the lidos or swimming ponds
