Greenwich Neighbourhood Guide

Where time begins — the Cutty Sark, Royal Observatory, Prime Meridian, a stunning park, and a UNESCO World Heritage riverside setting

Overview

Greenwich is one of London's most rewarding day-trip destinations. Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site, this riverside neighbourhood in southeast London is home to an extraordinary concentration of maritime history, scientific heritage, and architectural grandeur. From the Cutty Sark clipper ship to the Royal Observatory where time itself is measured, Greenwich tells the story of Britain's relationship with the sea and the stars.

The vibe: Historic, spacious, and surprisingly village-like. Despite its world-class attractions, Greenwich feels like a small town with its covered market, independent shops, and leafy park. The riverfront setting and views from the top of Greenwich Park over to Canary Wharf and the City are spectacular.

Best for: History enthusiasts, families with children, science lovers, architecture fans, and anyone who wants a full day of sightseeing with a market lunch and a beautiful park to explore. Greenwich easily fills an entire day.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Cutty Sark

    King William Walk, SE10 9HT

    The world's last surviving tea clipper, spectacularly preserved and raised on a glass canopy so you can walk beneath her copper-clad hull. Explore the ship from hold to deck and learn about the extraordinary speed races that brought tea from China. The collection of figureheads is remarkable.

  • 2. Royal Observatory Greenwich

    Blackheath Avenue, SE10 8XJ

    Stand astride the Prime Meridian Line — longitude zero — and have one foot in each hemisphere. The Observatory houses John Harrison's famous marine chronometers, the 28-inch Great Equatorial Telescope, and the Peter Harrison Planetarium. The views from the hilltop are London's finest.

  • 3. National Maritime Museum

    Romney Road, SE10 9NF

    The world's largest maritime museum, covering naval history from the Tudors to the modern day. Highlights include Nelson's coat with the bullet hole from Trafalgar, the huge Neptune Court atrium, and interactive galleries for children. Free entry.

  • 4. Greenwich Market

    5B Greenwich Market, SE10 9HZ

    A covered market under a beautiful Victorian iron and glass roof. Arts, crafts, and antiques during the week; street food from around the world at weekends. The food stalls are exceptional — Ethiopian injera, Thai curries, Argentine empanadas, and much more. Open daily.

  • 5. Greenwich Park

    Greenwich Park, SE10 8QY

    One of London's Royal Parks and the oldest enclosed royal park, with 183 acres of lawns, ancient trees, a deer park, a flower garden, and the finest panoramic view in London from the hill by the Observatory. The park was the equestrian venue for the 2012 Olympics.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. The Rivington Grill

    178 Greenwich High Road, SE10 8NN • British grill • $$

    A smart British grill restaurant serving well-executed classics — steaks, chops, fish and chips, and seasonal British dishes. The pre-theatre menu is good value, and the wine list is well chosen. Reliable and consistently good.

  • 2. Goddards at Greenwich

    22 King William Walk, SE10 9HU • Pie & mash • $

    A traditional London pie and mash shop that has been serving the community since 1890. Proper handmade pies with mash and liquor (parsley sauce). An authentic taste of old London that is increasingly hard to find.

  • 3. Craft London

    Peninsula Square, SE10 0SQ • Modern British • $$$

    Stevie Parle's restaurant on the Greenwich Peninsula focusing on seasonal, locally sourced British ingredients. The ground-floor cafe is great for coffee and pastries, while the upstairs restaurant offers more refined dining with views towards the O2.

  • 4. Buenos Aires Cafe

    86 Royal Hill, SE10 8RT • Argentine • $$

    An authentic Argentine restaurant with superb steaks, empanadas, and provoleta (grilled provolone cheese). The meat is cooked on a proper parrilla grill, and the chimichurri is made fresh. A Greenwich favourite for over 15 years.

  • 5. Tai Won Mein

    49 Greenwich Church Street, SE10 9BL • Chinese noodles • $

    A tiny, no-frills noodle bar serving enormous bowls of handmade noodle soup at very low prices. The dumplings and wonton soup are excellent. Simple, honest, and absolutely perfect for a warming lunch between sightseeing.

Best Pubs & Bars

  • 1. The Trafalgar Tavern

    Park Row, SE10 9NW

    A magnificent Regency-era riverside pub where Dickens set the wedding breakfast in Our Mutual Friend and where Liberal prime ministers held annual whitebait dinners. Grand rooms, river views, good ales, and a real sense of history.

  • 2. The Cutty Sark Tavern

    4-6 Ballast Quay, SE10 9PD

    A beautiful Georgian riverside pub slightly east of the main Greenwich strip, with a terrace overlooking the Thames. Less touristy than the pubs nearer the centre, with excellent real ales and a cosy wood-panelled interior. The river views at sunset are stunning.

  • 3. The Old Brewery

    The Pepys Building, Old Royal Naval College, SE10 9LW

    A brewery, cafe, and restaurant set within the grounds of the Old Royal Naval College. They brew their own beers on-site, and the setting in a Christopher Wren building is extraordinary. Good food and a lovely courtyard for summer drinking.

  • 4. The Greenwich Union

    56 Royal Hill, SE10 8RT

    The original home of Meantime Brewing Company, serving an excellent range of their craft beers alongside guest ales. A relaxed pub with knowledgeable staff, a good wine list, and a sunny beer garden on Royal Hill.

  • 5. Richard I

    52-54 Royal Hill, SE10 8RT

    A welcoming local pub with a pretty garden, well-kept Young's ales, and a good traditional pub menu. Less polished than some Greenwich drinking spots but all the better for it — a proper neighbourhood pub with real character.

Best Cafes

  • 1. The Greenwich Pantry

    45A Greenwich Church Street, SE10 9BL

    A charming independent cafe and bakery serving excellent coffee, freshly baked pastries, and light lunches. The cinnamon buns and sourdough toast are highlights. A cosy spot right in the heart of Greenwich's shopping streets.

  • 2. Cafe Rouge Greenwich

    30 Stockwell Street, SE10 9JN

    A reliable French-style cafe well-positioned near the market. Good for coffee, croissants, and a French-inspired breakfast or brunch before hitting the museums. Outdoor seating for sunny days.

  • 3. The Pavilion Cafe

    Greenwich Park, SE10 8QY

    A park cafe near the rose garden and playground with outdoor seating overlooking the park. Decent coffee, homemade cakes, and light meals. Perfect for families visiting Greenwich Park who need a mid-walk refreshment stop.

Shopping Highlights

  • Greenwich Market — The covered market is excellent for handmade jewellery, art prints, vintage clothing, and artisan food products. Different traders on different days, with weekends being the busiest.
  • Greenwich Church Street — The main independent shopping street with bookshops, vintage stores, galleries, and quirky gift shops.
  • Casbah Records — A fantastic independent record shop specialising in jazz, soul, funk, and world music, run by passionate music lovers.
  • Emporium — A vintage and retro emporium packed with mid-century furniture, vintage clothing, vinyl records, and retro homewares.
  • Royal Observatory Shop — Unique gifts including sundials, astronomical prints, navigation instruments, and time-themed souvenirs that you will not find anywhere else.

Getting There

Greenwich has several transport options, including the most scenic Tube journey in London:

  • Cutty Sark (DLR) — the closest station to the main sights, a short walk from the Cutty Sark and market
  • Greenwich (National Rail, Southeastern) — direct trains from London Bridge in about 12 minutes
  • Thames Clippers river bus — the most scenic route, from Westminster, Embankment, or Tower Pier to Greenwich Pier in about 30 minutes. Included on Travelcard with a discount
  • Greenwich Foot Tunnel — a Victorian pedestrian tunnel under the Thames from Island Gardens DLR on the Isle of Dogs, offering dramatic views of the Old Royal Naval College as you emerge

Insider Tips

  • Take the Thames Clipper from central London for the best arrival experience. The view of the Old Royal Naval College from the river is one of London's greatest architectural sights.
  • The National Maritime Museum, Queen's House, and the Painted Hall in the Old Royal Naval College are all free. Only the Royal Observatory and Cutty Sark require tickets — a combined ticket saves money.
  • The Painted Hall in the Old Royal Naval College is often called "Britain's Sistine Chapel." The ceiling painting by James Thornhill took 19 years to complete and is breathtaking. Small entry fee but absolutely worth it.
  • Walk through the Greenwich Foot Tunnel from Island Gardens DLR for free. The view of the Naval College as you emerge on the Greenwich side is unforgettable.
  • Greenwich Park's deer enclosure is in the southeast corner — a lovely surprise if you walk beyond the main hilltop area. The park is also one of the best spots in London for autumn colour.
