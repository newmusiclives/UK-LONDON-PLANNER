Best Day Trips from London by Train

Escape the capital for the day. These 12 destinations are all reachable by train in under two hours, with no car needed at the other end.

Train Booking Tips

UK train fares vary enormously depending on when you book and when you travel. These tips can save you significant money:

  • Book in advance: Advance tickets (available from 12 weeks before travel) are often 50–70% cheaper than buying on the day. Use Trainline or National Rail websites to compare.
  • Off-peak travel: Trains departing London after 9.30 am on weekdays (and anytime on weekends) are significantly cheaper. Off-peak return tickets are the best value for day trips.
  • Railcards: A 16–25, 26–30, or Two Together Railcard costs £30/year and gives a third off most fares. It pays for itself in 2–3 day trips.
  • Split tickets: Sometimes buying two tickets for different legs of the same journey is cheaper. The Trainline app does this automatically.

Fare estimates below are for off-peak return tickets booked a few days in advance. Advance fares can be significantly less.

1. Brighton

Train time: 1 hour • From: London Victoria or London Bridge • To: Brighton • Return fare: £20–£35

Best for: Couples, solo travellers, LGBTQ+ visitors, foodies

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. The Lanes & North Laine: A labyrinth of narrow lanes packed with independent shops, vintage stores, jewellery boutiques, and cafes. The Lanes are the historic quarter with antiques and jewellery; North Laine is the bohemian shopping area with vinyl records, vintage fashion, and quirky gifts. Allow 2 hours to explore both.
  2. Brighton Beach & Palace Pier: The iconic pebble beach, the ornate Victorian pier with fairground rides and arcades, and the ruined West Pier silhouetted against the sea. Fish and chips on the beach is obligatory. In summer, the beach bars along the seafront serve cocktails and play music.
  3. The Royal Pavilion: An extraordinary Indian-inspired palace built for King George IV, with a Chinese-style interior that is jaw-droppingly ornate. Entry £16. The surrounding gardens are free and beautiful for a picnic.

Also worth: Brighton Museum (free), the i360 observation tower (£19), and the food scene — Silo (zero-waste restaurant), Terre a Terre (vegetarian institution), and countless seafood restaurants along the coast.

2. Oxford

Train time: 1 hour • From: London Paddington • To: Oxford • Return fare: £20–£30

Best for: History buffs, Harry Potter fans, architecture lovers

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. The Bodleian Library & Radcliffe Camera: One of the world's oldest libraries, with the iconic circular Radcliffe Camera reading room. Guided tours (£9–£16) take you inside the medieval Duke Humfrey's Library — used as the Hogwarts library in the Harry Potter films. The view from the tower of the University Church of St Mary (next door, £5) is the best in Oxford.
  2. Christ Church College: The largest and grandest Oxford college. The Great Hall inspired the Hogwarts dining hall. The Tom Tower, the cathedral, and the meadow are all impressive. Entry £18. The college meadow walk down to the Thames is free and lovely.
  3. Ashmolean Museum: The world's oldest public museum (founded 1683). Free entry. Egyptian mummies, Anglo-Saxon treasures, Renaissance paintings, and an excellent rooftop restaurant. Allow 2 hours minimum.

Also worth: Punting on the Cherwell (from £25/hour for a boat), the Covered Market (open since 1774, great for lunch), and a pint at The Eagle and Child where Tolkien and C.S. Lewis met regularly.

3. Cambridge

Train time: 50 minutes • From: London King's Cross • To: Cambridge • Return fare: £18–£28

Best for: Couples, families, architecture lovers

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Punting on the Backs: A flat-bottomed boat along the River Cam, passing beneath bridges and along the backs of the historic colleges. Hire a punt (from £30/hour) or take a chauffeured tour (£20pp). The classic route passes King's College Chapel, the Mathematical Bridge, and the Bridge of Sighs. Quintessentially English.
  2. King's College Chapel: A masterpiece of late Gothic architecture with the largest fan-vaulted ceiling in the world. The stained glass and Rubens altarpiece are stunning. Entry £12. Attend Evensong (free, 5.30 pm during term) for one of the finest choral experiences in England.
  3. Fitzwilliam Museum: A world-class art museum with Egyptian antiquities, arms and armour, Impressionist paintings, and medieval manuscripts. Free entry. The Greek and Roman galleries on the upper floor are particularly impressive.

Also worth: Walking through the college grounds (many are open to visitors for a small fee), the market square for lunch, and the Eagle pub (where Watson and Crick announced the discovery of DNA's structure).

4. Bath

Train time: 1 hour 30 minutes • From: London Paddington • To: Bath Spa • Return fare: £30–£50

Best for: Couples, history buffs, spa lovers

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. The Roman Baths: Remarkably preserved Roman bathing complex built around natural hot springs. The audio guide (included in the £20 entry) is narrated by Bill Bryson and is excellent. The torchlit evening visits in summer are atmospheric. Allow 2 hours.
  2. The Royal Crescent & Circus: Two of the finest examples of Georgian architecture in Britain. The Royal Crescent is a sweeping 30-house crescent overlooking a park; The Circus is a circular terrace inspired by the Colosseum. No.1 Royal Crescent is a museum showing how a Georgian townhouse looked (£12). The surrounding streets are beautiful for a walk.
  3. Thermae Bath Spa: The only place in the UK where you can bathe in naturally hot spring water. The rooftop pool overlooking Bath Abbey and the surrounding hills is extraordinary. Sessions from £40 for 2 hours. Book ahead, especially for the rooftop pool at sunset.

Also worth: Bath Abbey (donation entry), Sally Lunn's (the oldest house in Bath, famous for its buns), Pulteney Bridge (shops on both sides, like Florence's Ponte Vecchio), and the excellent independent shops on Walcot Street.

5. Canterbury

Train time: 1 hour • From: London St Pancras (high-speed) or London Victoria • To: Canterbury West or Canterbury East • Return fare: £25–£40

Best for: History buffs, families, literary travellers

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Canterbury Cathedral: The seat of the Archbishop of Canterbury and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The site of Thomas Becket's murder in 1170 and a major pilgrimage destination for centuries. The Gothic nave, the medieval stained glass, and the atmospheric crypt are stunning. Entry £16. Audio guide included.
  2. Medieval City Walls & Westgate Towers: Walk along the remaining medieval city walls for views across the city. The Westgate Towers (£5 entry) are the largest surviving medieval gateway in England, with a rooftop viewing platform overlooking the River Stour.
  3. The Canterbury Tales (Chaucer experience): An interactive walk-through experience retelling Chaucer's Canterbury Tales in reconstructed medieval settings. Cheesy but fun, especially for families and literature fans. £13 entry.

Also worth: Punting on the River Stour (from £10pp), the ruined St Augustine's Abbey, and the excellent independent shops and pubs in the medieval centre. The Goods Shed (a daily farmers' market next to Canterbury West station) is a great spot for lunch.

6. Margate

Train time: 1 hour 30 minutes • From: London St Pancras (high-speed) or London Victoria • To: Margate • Return fare: £25–£40

Best for: Art lovers, families, vintage enthusiasts

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Turner Contemporary: A striking gallery on the seafront, named after J.M.W. Turner who painted the Margate skies. Free entry. Rotating exhibitions of contemporary art in light-filled galleries overlooking the sea. The building itself is beautiful.
  2. Dreamland: A restored vintage amusement park with a scenic railway (one of the oldest rollercoasters in the UK), retro rides, roller disco, and regular live music events. Entry from £5 (rides extra). The vintage seaside atmosphere is charming.
  3. Margate Old Town: A regenerated quarter of independent shops, vintage stores, galleries, and cafes. The Shell Grotto (a mysterious underground chamber covered in 4.6 million shells, origin unknown) is fascinating and peculiar. £5 entry.

Also worth: The sandy beach (one of Kent's best), the harbour arm for sunset drinks, and the growing food scene — Brill & Co for fish, Angela's for fine dining, Fort's for cafe culture.

7. Whitstable

Train time: 1 hour 30 minutes • From: London Victoria or London St Pancras • To: Whitstable • Return fare: £25–£35

Best for: Couples, foodies, relaxed day out

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Eat oysters: Whitstable has been famous for its oysters since Roman times. The Whitstable Oyster Company, The Forge, and numerous seafood stalls along the harbour serve them fresh. Oysters from £2 each. Even if you do not like oysters, the fish and seafood here is exceptional.
  2. Walk the harbour and beach: The colourful beach huts, the working harbour with fishing boats, and the shingle beach stretching east toward the iconic sunsets are all beautiful. Walk along the coast path toward Tankerton to see the street of slopes — a natural shingle causeway that appears at low tide.
  3. Browse the independent shops: Harbour Street is lined with independent galleries, vintage shops, bookshops, and homeware stores. Oxford Street it is not — this is gentle, characterful browsing. Whitstable Books is a particular highlight.

Also worth: The Whitstable Oyster Festival (late July) is a week-long celebration with live music, parades, and seafood. Booking restaurants in Whitstable is advisable at weekends — The Sportsman (a Michelin-starred pub in nearby Seasalter) requires booking months ahead.

8. Rye

Train time: 1 hour • From: London St Pancras (change at Ashford International) • To: Rye • Return fare: £25–£40

Best for: Couples, history buffs, photographers

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Explore the medieval old town: Rye is one of the best-preserved medieval towns in England. Cobbled Mermaid Street (one of the most photographed streets in the country) climbs steeply past timber-framed houses. The Mermaid Inn (built 1420, now a hotel and restaurant) is atmospheric. Wander the narrow lanes and discover antique shops, art galleries, and tea rooms.
  2. Climb St Mary's Church Tower: The church dates from the 12th century. Climb the tower (£4) for panoramic views across the terracotta rooftops, the surrounding marshes, and out to the English Channel. The church's 16th-century clock is the oldest working turret clock in England.
  3. Walk to Camber Sands: A 3-mile walk (or short bus ride) from Rye takes you to Camber Sands, one of the south coast's finest sandy beaches. The wide, flat sands backed by dunes feel like a different world from London. Quieter on weekdays; families fill it on summer weekends.

Also worth: The Rye Heritage Centre, Lamb House (where Henry James wrote), and the excellent pubs — The Ypres Castle Inn has a garden with marsh views. The antique shops along the high street are excellent for browsing.

9. Winchester

Train time: 1 hour • From: London Waterloo • To: Winchester • Return fare: £20–£35

Best for: History buffs, families, peaceful day out

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Winchester Cathedral: One of Europe's largest cathedrals, with the longest nave in England. Jane Austen is buried here. The medieval floor tiles, the illuminated Winchester Bible (one of the finest surviving 12th-century manuscripts), and the Anthony Gormley sculpture "Sound II" in the flooded crypt are highlights. Entry by donation (£10 suggested). Free guided tours daily.
  2. The Great Hall & Round Table: The only surviving part of Winchester Castle, housing a medieval round table that has hung on the wall since at least 1463. Once believed to be King Arthur's Round Table, it was actually made in the 13th century. Free entry. The medieval garden outside is peaceful.
  3. Walk along the River Itchen: The water meadows walk from the city centre to the Hospital of St Cross (a medieval almshouse, £5 entry) is one of the most beautiful short walks in southern England. Wildflower meadows, chalk streams, and thatched cottages. Allow 1 hour there and back.

Also worth: The Buttercross (medieval market cross in the High Street), Chesil Rectory (the oldest restaurant in Winchester, medieval timber-frame building), and the Christmas Market (November–December, one of the best in England).

10. Stratford-upon-Avon

Train time: 2 hours • From: London Marylebone • To: Stratford-upon-Avon • Return fare: £25–£45

Best for: Literature lovers, families, theatre fans

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Shakespeare's Birthplace: The timber-framed house where Shakespeare was born in 1564, now a museum with period rooms and exhibitions on his life. Entry £20 (or £26 for a combo ticket covering multiple Shakespeare properties). The garden hosts live performances in summer.
  2. Royal Shakespeare Company (RSC): See a world-class Shakespeare production at the RSC Theatre on the banks of the Avon. Matinee performances work well for day-trippers (check schedule). Tickets from £15 for restricted view. The tower has observation deck views (£4).
  3. Walk the River Avon: The riverside walk from the RSC theatre to Holy Trinity Church (where Shakespeare is buried, free entry) is beautiful. Rent a rowing boat (£8/hour) or take a river cruise. The Bancroft Gardens near the theatre are perfect for a picnic.

Also worth: Anne Hathaway's Cottage (a mile outside town, beautiful thatched farmhouse and garden), Hall's Croft (Shakespeare's daughter's home), and the many historic pubs in the town centre.

11. Bristol

Train time: 1 hour 45 minutes • From: London Paddington • To: Bristol Temple Meads • Return fare: £30–£55

Best for: Street art fans, foodies, independent culture lovers

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. Banksy and street art: Bristol is Banksy's home city and a global street art capital. Famous Banksy pieces include "The Mild Mild West" (Stokes Croft) and "Girl with a Pierced Eardrum" (Harbourside). The street art around Stokes Croft, Nelson Street, and the Bearpit is constantly evolving. Self-guided walking tours are free — maps available online.
  2. Clifton Suspension Bridge: Brunel's iconic bridge spanning the Avon Gorge is Bristol's most recognisable landmark. Walk across it (free) for breathtaking views. The Clifton Observatory nearby has a camera obscura and a tunnel descending to a cave overlooking the gorge (£4). The surrounding Clifton Village has excellent independent shops and cafes.
  3. Harbourside & food scene: The regenerated harbour area has the M Shed museum (free, excellent), the SS Great Britain (Brunel's ship, £18), and Cargo — a collection of shipping container restaurants and bars under the cranes. Wapping Wharf nearby has some of Bristol's best independent food businesses.

Also worth: St Nicholas Market (a covered market with great food stalls), the Arnolfini gallery (free contemporary art), and the independent brewery scene — Wiper and True, Left Handed Giant, and Moor Beer are all excellent.

12. Hastings

Train time: 1 hour 30 minutes • From: London St Pancras (high-speed to St Leonards) or London Charing Cross • To: Hastings • Return fare: £25–£40

Best for: Families, vintage lovers, creative types, budget travellers

Top 3 Things to Do

  1. The Old Town: Hastings' Old Town is a tangle of medieval lanes, independent shops, vintage stores, and pubs crammed into a narrow valley between two hills. The net huts on The Stade (tall black wooden structures used for drying fishing nets) are unique. The fishing beach is still a working beach with boats hauled up daily — the largest beach-launched fishing fleet in Europe.
  2. East Hill Cliff Railway & Hastings Country Park: Take the Victorian funicular railway (the steepest in Britain, £3.50 return) up East Hill, then walk into Hastings Country Park — dramatic clifftop walks with wildflowers, sea views, and the ruins of an ancient church. The Firehills section has Glen-like ravines cutting down to the beach.
  3. Jerwood Gallery (now Hastings Contemporary): A striking black-clad gallery on the seafront showing rotating exhibitions of modern and contemporary British art. Free entry. The building itself, clad in thousands of black ceramic tiles, is photogenic against the sea.

Also worth: The castle ruins (the site of the 1066 landing), the Smugglers' Adventure in St Clements Caves (£9, fun for families), and the adjacent town of St Leonards-on-Sea which has a growing independent food and drink scene. The two towns together make a full, varied day out.
