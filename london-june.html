London in June

The longest days, Trooping the Colour, Wimbledon queues, Pride celebrations, and outdoor everything — June is London at its most vibrant.

🌡️ Weather Overview

June delivers London's longest days and some of its best weather, though the British climate always keeps things interesting:

  • Average temperature: 12°C to 21°C (54°F to 70°F), with heatwaves occasionally pushing past 30°C
  • Rainfall: Around 45mm across 11 rainy days — similar to May, with warm showers that pass quickly
  • Daylight hours: Over 16 hours — sunrise before 4:45am, sunset after 9:20pm. The summer solstice (around 21 June) is the longest day of the year
  • Sunshine: Around 7 hours per day — London's sunniest month

The extraordinary length of June days is something visitors from lower latitudes find remarkable. It's still light enough to read outdoors at 9:30pm, and the twilight lingers past 10pm. This transforms the city — outdoor dining, evening walks along the Thames, and sunsets that seem to last forever.

🎭 What's On in June

June is one of London's busiest and most exciting event months:

  • Trooping the Colour (second Saturday in June): The King's official birthday parade along The Mall to Horse Guards Parade. Over 1,400 soldiers, 200 horses, and 400 musicians in a spectacular display. Free to watch from The Mall; ballot for seated tickets opens months ahead.
  • Wimbledon Championships (late June-early July): The world's most famous tennis tournament begins in late June. Enter the public ballot for tickets, or join the legendary Wimbledon Queue for same-day ground passes — an experience in itself.
  • Pride in London (late June): London's Pride parade and festival draws over a million people. The parade runs from Portland Place through Oxford Circus to Whitehall, with stages and events across Soho, Trafalgar Square, and beyond. Free to attend.
  • Royal Ascot (mid-June): Five days of world-class horse racing with extraordinary hats and high fashion. Ascot is a 25-minute train ride from Waterloo. General admission tickets are affordable; the Royal Enclosure requires formal dress and a member's invitation.
  • Outdoor Cinema Season (all month): Rooftop Film Club (various locations), Luna Cinema (parks and palaces), and The Nomad Cinema (pop-up locations) all screen films under the stars. Bring blankets and arrive early.
  • Open Garden Squares Weekend (mid-June): Over 200 normally private gardens across London open their gates to the public for one weekend. A unique chance to peek behind the hedges of hidden squares and private estates.
  • London Festival of Architecture (all month): Installations, tours, talks, and open buildings across the city celebrate London's architecture. Many events are free, and it's a fascinating way to see the city differently.
  • Meltdown Festival at Southbank Centre (mid-June): A guest-curated music festival at the Southbank Centre, featuring an eclectic mix of genres. Past curators include David Bowie, Patti Smith, and M.I.A.

✨ Top Things to Do in June

June rewards visitors who embrace the long days and outdoor living:

  • Watch the Changing of the Guard: Held daily in summer outside Buckingham Palace at 11am. Arrive by 10:15am for a good position. The ceremony takes about 45 minutes and is free to watch.
  • Swim in the Serpentine Lido: The open-water swimming area in Hyde Park opens for the season. Swimming in a Royal Park with ducks and deckchairs is a uniquely London experience. The Lido Cafe serves excellent breakfasts.
  • Explore the South Bank at sunset: From the National Theatre to Tower Bridge, the South Bank comes alive on June evenings. Street performers, the Underbelly Spiegeltent, and the BFI bar terrace create a festival atmosphere every night.
  • Visit the roses at Regent's Park: Queen Mary's Gardens peak in June with over 12,000 roses in bloom. The fragrance on a warm afternoon is extraordinary. Combine with a show at the Open Air Theatre next door.
  • Catch a match at The Oval or Lord's: June is peak cricket season. Test matches at Lord's and The Oval are legendary, but county matches offer a more relaxed atmosphere and easier tickets. Bring a picnic.
  • Explore Hampstead Heath swimming ponds: Three natural swimming ponds — men's, women's, and mixed — set among ancient woodland. Swimming here on a warm June morning is magical. Small entry fee.
  • See a show at the Open Air Theatre: Regent's Park Open Air Theatre's summer programme is in full swing. Musicals and Shakespeare under the stars, with the park's roses as a backdrop. Bring a picnic and a blanket.
  • Walk the Jubilee Greenway: This 60km walking trail circles London, linking Royal Parks and Olympic sites. Walk it in sections — the stretch from Little Venice through Regent's Park to Westminster is ideal for a June afternoon.
  • Visit Kensington Roof Gardens: One and a half acres of themed gardens on a rooftop in Kensington, with flamingos. Check opening times as they can be restricted for private events.
  • Take an evening Thames cruise: River buses run until late. A sunset cruise from Westminster to Greenwich, passing the illuminated bridges and the Tower of London, is one of London's great romantic experiences.

🍽️ Where to Eat & Drink

June dining is all about outdoor terraces, rooftops, and the season's finest British produce:

  • Skylon, South Bank: Floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the Thames from the Royal Festival Hall. The grill menu features excellent seasonal British cooking, and the bar terrace is perfect for a pre-dinner drink watching the river.
  • The Orangery at Kensington Palace: Afternoon tea in the elegant 18th-century orangery, surrounded by Kensington Gardens in full bloom. Book ahead for a window table.
  • Forza Wine, Peckham: A small, vibrant Italian wine bar with a few outdoor tables, excellent natural wines, and simple but superb food — burrata with peaches, grilled meats, seasonal pasta. Walk-ins only.
  • The Rooftop at Trafalgar St James: A rooftop bar with panoramic views over Trafalgar Square, Nelson's Column, and the London skyline. Cocktails are pricey but the view is free with your drink.
  • Padella, Borough Market: Still one of London's best-value meals. Fresh pasta, cooked to order, at remarkable prices. The summer menu might feature courgette and ricotta or nduja pappardelle. Queue from 11:45am.
  • The Guinea Grill, Mayfair: A historic pub and grill that's been serving some of London's finest steaks since the 15th century. The cosy pub at the front serves excellent Young's ales; the grill room at the back is a carnivore's paradise.

💡 Insider Tips

  • June is peak season — book everything early: Hotels, theatre tickets, restaurant reservations, and attraction slots all fill up faster in June. Book at least 4-6 weeks ahead for popular spots.
  • The Wimbledon Queue is an experience: If you don't have ballot tickets, you can queue from early morning (or overnight) for same-day ground passes. Bring camping chairs, food, and patience — the camaraderie in the queue is part of the fun.
  • Trooping the Colour viewing tips: The Mall fills up early. For the best view, position yourself near Horse Guards Parade by 9am. After the ceremony, walk to Buckingham Palace for the Royal Family's balcony appearance and the RAF flypast.
  • Use the parks to beat the heat: If London hits a heatwave (increasingly common in June), seek out shaded parks. St James's Park, Holland Park, and the Inns of Court gardens are cool refuges. Most shops and museums have air conditioning.
  • Evenings are the secret weapon: With daylight past 9:30pm, plan activities for the late afternoon and evening. Attractions are less crowded after 4pm, and al fresco dining at 8pm is a joy.

🎒 What to Pack

  • Light, breathable clothing — dresses, shorts, and t-shirts for warm days
  • A light jacket or cardigan for cooler evenings and air-conditioned interiors
  • Sunscreen (SPF 30+) and sunglasses — essential, not optional
  • Comfortable walking sandals and a pair of closed shoes for evening dining
  • A compact rain jacket — even June has showers
  • Hat for sun protection during outdoor events
  • Reusable water bottle — London has free refill stations across the city
  • Smart outfit if attending Royal Ascot or a nice dinner
