London in May

Warm days, two bank holidays, the Chelsea Flower Show, and pub gardens in full swing — May might just be London's most enjoyable month.

🌡️ Weather Overview

May is one of London's most pleasant months, with genuinely warm days and long, bright evenings:

  • Average temperature: 9°C to 17°C (48°F to 63°F), with warm spells pushing past 22°C
  • Rainfall: Around 49mm across 12 rainy days — showers are possible but rarely spoil a day
  • Daylight hours: 15 to 16 hours — sunrise before 5:15am, sunset after 8:45pm by late May
  • Sunshine: Around 6.5 hours per day — the sunniest month alongside June

May often delivers the best weather of the British summer. The air is fresh, parks are in full bloom, and the long evenings make it feel like each day has bonus hours. Late May bank holiday weekends can be genuinely hot.

🎭 What's On in May

May is festival season in London, with something happening almost every weekend:

  • RHS Chelsea Flower Show (late May): The world's most prestigious flower show takes over the grounds of the Royal Hospital Chelsea for a week. Tickets sell out months ahead. The final day offers bargain-priced plants as exhibitors sell off their displays.
  • Two Bank Holidays: The early May bank holiday (first Monday) and the late May/spring bank holiday (last Monday) both bring long weekends, special events, and a festive mood across the city.
  • Underbelly Festival (all month): The South Bank's free-entry festival of circus, cabaret, comedy, and street food is in full swing along the Thames. Grab a drink, catch a show, and soak up the riverside atmosphere.
  • Museums at Night (mid-May): For one weekend, museums and galleries across London open their doors after hours with special events, performances, and behind-the-scenes tours. Many are free.
  • London Craft Beer Festival (late May): Held at Tobacco Dock in Wapping, this festival brings together over 100 breweries from around the world with DJs and street food.
  • FA Cup Final (mid-May): Wembley Stadium hosts the climax of English football's oldest competition. Even without tickets, the atmosphere in pubs across London is electric.
  • Regent's Park Open Air Theatre (from mid-May): One of London's most magical theatre experiences — performances in the open air surrounded by the park's gardens. Musicals, Shakespeare, and new work under the trees.
  • Photo London (mid-May): Somerset House hosts this major photography fair, with galleries, exhibitions, and talks from leading photographers.

✨ Top Things to Do in May

May is the month when London lives outdoors. Here's how to make the most of it:

  • Drink in a pub garden: This is what May in London is all about. Top pub gardens include The Spaniards Inn (Hampstead), The Dove (Hammersmith), The Flask (Highgate), and The Albion (Islington).
  • Explore Regent's Park roses: Queen Mary's Gardens in Regent's Park contain over 12,000 roses across 85 beds. Late May sees the first blooms, building to a peak in June. Free entry and utterly beautiful.
  • Walk the Thames Path: From the Tate Modern to Greenwich via Bermondsey and Rotherhithe, the south bank Thames Path is spectacular on a May evening. Allow 2-3 hours and stop for drinks along the way.
  • Visit Columbia Road Flower Market: London's beloved Sunday flower market is at its most exuberant in May. The range of plants is extraordinary — arrive by 8:30am for the best selection.
  • Kayak on the Regent's Canal: Several operators offer kayaking from Limehouse to Little Venice or shorter sections. Paddling past the London Zoo aviary and through Camden is a unique perspective on the city.
  • Catch an open-air theatre show: Regent's Park Open Air Theatre is London's most enchanting stage. Bring a picnic to enjoy in the garden before the performance. Book early — popular shows sell out.
  • Climb the O2: Walk over the roof of the O2 arena on a guided climb. May's clear evenings reward you with views across the Thames, the Docklands, and the City skyline at sunset.
  • Explore Little Venice: Where the Grand Union and Regent's Canals meet, this leafy enclave of houseboats and waterside cafes feels miles from central London. Take a narrowboat from here to Camden Lock.
  • Visit Kyoto Garden in Holland Park: A tranquil Japanese garden with a waterfall, koi carp, and mature Japanese maples. Peacocks roam the park. Free and rarely crowded.
  • Ride the Emirates Air Line: London's cable car crosses the Thames between Greenwich Peninsula and the Royal Docks. On a clear May evening, the views are magnificent and it's included on Oyster/contactless.

🍽️ Where to Eat & Drink

May brings the best of British spring produce and the joy of eating outdoors:

  • Rochelle Canteen, Shoreditch: Eating in the garden of this hidden canteen — accessed through a former school gate — on a warm May afternoon is pure bliss. Simple, seasonal, perfect British cooking.
  • The Ivy Chelsea Garden: The courtyard garden is stunning in May, with wisteria overhead. The menu covers all bases from brunch to dinner. Book the garden terrace specifically.
  • Maltby Street Market, Bermondsey: A compact, locals' favourite market under the railway arches. Outstanding sourdough from Comptoir Gourmand, tacos from El Pastór, and natural wine from 40 Maltby Street. Saturdays only.
  • Crate Brewery, Hackney Wick: A canalside brewery and pizzeria in a converted warehouse. Sit outside with a pint of their lager and a sourdough pizza as boats drift past. Peak London canal vibes.
  • Spring, Somerset House: Skye Gyngell's restaurant lives up to its name in May. The daily-changing menu celebrates the season's first British asparagus, broad beans, and peas in an elegant, light-filled room.
  • The Narrow, Limehouse: Gordon Ramsay's riverside gastropub in a former dockmaster's house. The terrace overlooking the Thames is ideal on a warm May evening. British classics done well.

💡 Insider Tips

  • Bank holiday weekends get busy: Both May bank holidays bring crowds to parks, markets, and popular attractions. If you prefer quieter experiences, visit museums on the bank holiday Monday itself — most tourists head to outdoor attractions.
  • Chelsea Flower Show sells out: Tickets go on sale months in advance and popular days sell out within weeks. If you miss out, the surrounding streets of Chelsea are decorated with floral displays that are free to enjoy.
  • Book restaurants with outdoor seating now: Everyone wants to eat outside in May. Restaurant terraces and pub gardens fill up fast, especially on sunny weekends. Reserve ahead or aim for weekday lunches.
  • Bring sun protection: The UV index in May can be surprisingly high. British skin burns easily after a grey winter — bring sunscreen even if the sky looks overcast.
  • Late May is peak season pricing: From the late May bank holiday onward, London shifts into summer pricing for hotels and attractions. Early May offers the same weather at lower cost.

🎒 What to Pack

  • Light jacket or cardigan for cooler evenings
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen (SPF 30+)
  • Comfortable walking shoes — sandals for warm days, closed shoes for cooler ones
  • A light rain layer — showers still happen
  • Smart-casual outfit for evening dining
  • Daypack for carrying layers and a water bottle
  • Picnic blanket — you'll want one for the parks
