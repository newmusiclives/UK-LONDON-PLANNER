10 Money-Saving Secrets Locals Use

London is expensive — unless you know the tricks. These are the same strategies Londoners use to enjoy the city without spending a fortune.

Travel Off-Peak on the Tube

Save up to £2/day

Oyster and contactless fares drop after 9:30 AM on weekdays. A Zone 1–2 journey falls from £2.80 to £2.70, and the daily cap drops from £8.10 to £7.40. Since most tourist attractions do not require a 7 AM start, this is free money. On weekends and bank holidays, off-peak fares apply all day.

Pro tip: The Hopper fare lets you take unlimited bus rides within one hour of your first tap for just £1.75 total. Use buses for short hops and save the Tube for longer journeys.

Visit Free Museums (and Their Paid Exhibitions)

Save £20–40/day

London's major museums are free: the British Museum, National Gallery, Tate Modern, V&A, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Imperial War Museum, and more. Each has enough to fill an entire day. Even the paid special exhibitions are often cheaper than entry fees at comparable museums worldwide — typically £15–22 for blockbuster shows.

Pro tip: Many museums offer free late-night openings. The V&A is open until 10 PM on Fridays; the National Gallery until 9 PM on Fridays. These are perfect for avoiding crowds.

Use the TKTS Booth for Theatre

Save 25–50% on theatre

The official TKTS booth in Leicester Square sells same-day and next-day West End tickets at 25–50% off. Run by the Society of London Theatre, it is the only legitimate discount booth in the square (ignore touts). Available shows change daily, but you can often find top musicals and plays at significant discounts.

Pro tip: Arrive when the booth opens at 10 AM for the best selection. Alternatively, many theatres release their own rush tickets or day seats from £10–25 — check individual theatre websites.

Eat Lunch Set Menus at Top Restaurants

Save 40–60% vs dinner

Many of London's best restaurants offer lunch set menus at a fraction of their dinner prices. A two-course lunch at a Michelin-starred restaurant might cost £25–35, while the same quality dinner runs £70–100+. Dishoom's lunch menu, Padella's lunchtime pasta, and The Wolseley's prix fixe are outstanding value.

Pro tip: Pre-theatre menus (typically 5–6:30 PM) at many West End-area restaurants offer similar savings. Eat early, then head to your show.

Join Free Walking Tours

Save £15–30 per person

Free walking tours operate daily across London, covering areas like the City of London, Westminster, Shoreditch street art, Harry Potter filming locations, and Jack the Ripper's Whitechapel. Guides work for tips, so you pay what you think the tour was worth (typically £5–10). Companies like Strawberry Tours and Free Tours by Foot run excellent 2-hour walks.

Pro tip: These tours are also the best way to orient yourself on your first day. Book one for the morning of Day 1 and you will understand the city's layout far better than any map can teach you.

Shop at Lidl and Aldi Near Attractions

Save £10–20/day on food

A meal deal at a central London cafe costs £8–12. The same lunch from Lidl, Aldi, or even Tesco Express costs £3–5. There are budget supermarkets surprisingly close to major attractions: Lidl on Tottenham Court Road (near British Museum), Aldi on Kilburn High Road, and Tesco Metro locations everywhere. Stock up on breakfast items and picnic supplies.

Pro tip: London's parks are perfect for picnics. Buy supplies from a supermarket and eat in Hyde Park, Regent's Park, or the South Bank — far more enjoyable than a cramped tourist-trap sandwich shop.

Get Cheap Theatre via Evening Standard Offers

Save £20–50 per ticket

The Evening Standard (London's free newspaper) regularly partners with West End shows to offer exclusive discounted tickets, sometimes as low as £15 for shows that normally cost £60+. Check their website's Going Out section. Similarly, apps like TodayTix offer last-minute deals and lottery tickets for popular shows.

Pro tip: Many shows run ticket lotteries where you can win front-row seats for £10–25. Enter via the show's app or TodayTix. Hamilton, Les Misérables, and The Lion King all run regular lotteries.

Ask About Student and Young Person Discounts

Save 10–25% on attractions

If you are 16–25 or a student, always ask about discounts — most paid attractions, theatres, and even some restaurants offer them, but they are not always advertised. An ISIC student card works internationally. Some attractions also have Young Friends memberships that include free entry and special events.

Pro tip: A 16–25 Railcard (£30/year) saves 1/3 on all UK rail fares and can be loaded onto an Oyster card for off-peak Tube discounts too. It pays for itself within 2–3 train journeys.

Use Free Viewpoints Instead of Paid Ones

Save £25–35 per person

The Shard (£32), the London Eye (£32), and Sky Garden are London's famous viewpoints — but Sky Garden is free with advance booking. Other free options include: Primrose Hill (panoramic city view), the Tate Modern viewing platform (Level 10), Greenwich Observatory hill, Parliament Hill on Hampstead Heath, and the roof terrace at One New Change near St Paul's.

Pro tip: Sky Garden slots book up fast. Try releasing dates 3 weeks in advance — set a reminder and book as soon as they open. Morning slots have the best availability.

Bring a Refillable Water Bottle

Save £3–5/day

Bottled water costs £1.50–3 at London shops and cafes. Meanwhile, London tap water is perfectly safe to drink (and quite good). Carry a refillable bottle and use the free water fountains now installed across the city — most major parks, stations, and museums have them. The Refill app shows nearby free refill points.

Pro tip: Most cafes and restaurants will happily fill your bottle for free if you ask politely, even if you are not a customer. Londoners do this all the time.

Sample Budget Day in London

Total: under £35

Here is what a full, satisfying day in London looks like on a tight budget:

  • Breakfast: Pastry and coffee from Greggs or Pret (£3.50)
  • Morning: British Museum — free (2–3 hours)
  • Lunch: Tesco meal deal: sandwich, snack, drink (£3.50)
  • Afternoon: Walk through Covent Garden, cross Waterloo Bridge, explore South Bank — free
  • Tea break: Coffee from a supermarket (£1.50)
  • Evening: Free walking tour of Westminster, tip (£5–8)
  • Dinner: Dishoom lunch set menu leftovers or a £8–10 meal from a market stall
  • Transport: Oyster off-peak daily cap (£7.40)

Total: £30–35 for a full day including world-class cultural experiences, iconic sights, and decent food. London on a budget is absolutely doable — it just takes a little planning.

Free Events and Entertainment

Save £20–80 per event

London has an extraordinary amount of free entertainment if you know where to look:

Lunchtime concerts: St Martin-in-the-Fields (Trafalgar Square) hosts free lunchtime concerts on Mondays, Tuesdays, and Fridays at 1 PM. St James's Piccadilly and several other City churches also hold regular free recitals.

Comedy: Angel Comedy in Islington runs free comedy nights several evenings a week, featuring established comedians trying new material. Just turn up early to get a seat.

Changing of the Guard: The ceremony at Buckingham Palace is free to watch and happens most days at 11 AM (check the schedule online). Arrive 30 minutes early for a good viewing spot.

Speakers' Corner: Every Sunday morning at the northeast corner of Hyde Park, anyone can stand on a soapbox and speak on any topic. It has been running since 1872 and remains one of London's most uniquely British experiences.
