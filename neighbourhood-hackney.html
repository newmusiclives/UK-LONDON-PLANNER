Hackney Neighbourhood Guide

Broadway Market, London Fields, Victoria Park — East London's creative, food-obsessed, post-gentrification heartland

Overview

Hackney (the borough and the place) stretches from Shoreditch in the south to Stamford Hill in the north. Most visitors focus on the middle: Broadway Market, London Fields, Hackney Central, Hackney Wick, and Victoria Park.

Ten years ago it was affordable-and-creative; today it's expensive-and-creative — but the food, market, and bar scenes remain London's best. A single Saturday walking Broadway Market, eating at Climpson's Arch, drinking at Howling Hops, and wandering into a Chisenhale Gallery opening is a quintessential London day.

The vibe: Young, creative, queue-happy. Saturday market energy; Sunday roasts; Friday-night DJs. Still has bite, despite the changes.

Best for: Foodies, young travellers, music fans, families who've done the main sights.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Broadway Market

    Broadway Market, E8 4PH

    Saturdays 9am-5pm — 100+ food, fashion, and craft stalls plus a canal-side setting. A Hackney institution.

  • 2. Victoria Park

    Grove Rd, E3 5TB

    London's first public park (1845). 213 acres with a lake, playgrounds, a Pavilion café, and hosts All Points East festival in summer.

  • 3. Chisenhale Gallery

    64 Chisenhale Rd, E3 5QZ

    Cutting-edge commissioning gallery in a former veneer factory — many artists get their first major solo shows here.

  • 4. Sutton House

    2-4 Homerton High St, E9 6JQ

    The oldest surviving house in Hackney — a Tudor red-brick mansion (1535). National Trust.

  • 5. Hackney Empire

    291 Mare St, E8 1EJ

    Frank Matcham's 1901 variety theatre, saved and restored. London's best pantomime; excellent comedy and world music.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Brat

    First Floor, 4 Redchurch St, E1 6JL · Basque · $$$$

    Basque-style open-fire cooking — Michelin-starred. The whole-roasted turbot is unforgettable.

  • 2. The Marksman

    254 Hackney Rd, E2 7SJ · British · $$

    19th-century exterior, Michelin Bib Gourmand-approved gastropub cooking. Pub at its finest.

  • 3. Cafe Cecilia

    32 Andrews Rd, E8 4RL · British · $$$

    Max Rocha's airy canal-side restaurant — simple, seasonal, and hugely in demand. Book weeks ahead.

  • 4. Peg

    40 Morning Ln, E9 6NA · Japanese · $$$

    Hackney yakitori and natural-wine bar — hot counter seats overlooking the grill.

  • 5. Lyle's Shoreditch

    Tea Building, 56 Shoreditch High St, E1 6JJ · British · $$$$

    James Lowe's modern-British fine-dining canteen. Lunch tasting menu £45 — one of London's best fine-dining bargains.

Best Bars & Pubs

  • 1. MOTH Club

    Old Trades Hall, Valette St, E9 6NU

    Former British Legion hall — gold-glitter ceiling, cheap drinks, cult live gigs and DJ nights.

  • 2. Satan's Whiskers

    343 Cambridge Heath Rd, E2 9RA

    Bethnal Green cocktail bar with a daily-changing menu written on the wall. Walk-in; hip-hop soundtrack.

  • 3. Howling Hops

    Unit 9a Queens Yard, E9 5EN

    Hackney Wick brewery and taproom — the UK's first tank bar, serving unfiltered beer straight from fermenters.

  • 4. The Crown

    223 Grove Rd, E3 5SN

    Victoria Park gastropub on the west edge of the park — a solid Sunday roast and large beer garden.

  • 5. Netil360

    1 Westgate St, E8 3RL

    Rooftop bar above Netil Market — panoramic East-London skyline, sunset DJs, casual atmosphere.

Best Cafes

  • 1. E5 Bakehouse

    Arch 395, Mentmore Terrace, E8 3PH

    London Fields bakery and café under the railway arches — sourdough-first, natural-wine bar in the evenings.

  • 2. Climpson & Sons Broadway Market

    67 Broadway Market, E8 4PH

    Independent Hackney roaster and café. Saturday Broadway Market is just outside.

  • 3. Pavilion Café Victoria Park

    Grove Rd, E3 5TB

    Lake-side café in Victoria Park — classic full Englishes, dog-friendly, Hackney institution.

Shopping Highlights

  • Broadway Market (Saturdays) — the Saturday market: 100+ food, fashion, and craft stalls, plus weekly favourites Climpson's, E5 Bakehouse, Fin & Flounder.
  • Netil Market (Saturdays) — a tarpaulin-covered collection of street-food and indie fashion stalls adjacent to Broadway Market.
  • Mare Street — Hackney Central's high street. Burberry Factory Shop, LN-CC, and quirky independents.
  • Wilton Way — a cluster of independent shops near London Fields: Artwords bookshop, Triangle coffee, bespoke tailors.
  • Hackney Flea (quarterly) — a pop-up antique flea market at Stoke Newington Town Hall.
  • Rough Trade East (short walk south) — Brick Lane record shop.

Getting There

Main stations serving this neighbourhood:

  • Hackney Central (Overground) — central Hackney — most central of the Overground stations
  • London Fields (Overground) — Broadway Market and London Fields closest
  • Bethnal Green (Central) — south-west Hackney
  • Hackney Wick (Overground) — east side — for Howling Hops, Crate Brewery, Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park

Insider Tips

  • Broadway Market is Saturdays only (9am-5pm) — Sundays the stalls are gone but the pubs, cafés, and restaurants remain.
  • Walk the Regent's Canal from Broadway Market east to Victoria Park, then on to Hackney Wick — some of London's loveliest urban waterside.
  • Victoria Park hosts All Points East (May) and Field Day (at Brockwell Park) festivals — central Hackney gets gig-packed in summer.
  • Climpson's Arch (railway arch) does wood-fire pizza on summer-evening Saturdays — Hackney's best pizza outdoors.
  • Sunday in Hackney = brunch queue at Cafe Cecilia or The Marksman, stroll Victoria Park, pint in a beer garden. Plan your day around one long meal.
