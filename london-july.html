London in July

Peak summer, long golden evenings, world-class sport, and the city at its most vibrant outdoor best.

Weather Overview

Average High: 22 °C (72 °F)  |  Average Low: 14 °C (57 °F)

Rainfall: Around 45 mm across 8–10 rain days — usually short afternoon showers rather than all-day drizzle.

Daylight: Roughly 16 hours. Sunrise around 5:00 am, sunset after 9:15 pm. The extended twilight makes evenings feel endless.

What to expect: July is one of London's warmest months. Heatwaves above 30 °C are increasingly common. Most buildings lack air conditioning, so pack light, breathable clothing and stay hydrated. Humidity can be moderate and sudden thunderstorms pop up without much warning.

What's On in July

  • Wimbledon Championships (late June – mid-July) — The world's most prestigious tennis tournament at the All England Club. Queue early for ground passes or soak up the atmosphere on Henman Hill.
  • BBC Proms (mid-July – September) — Over 70 classical concerts at the Royal Albert Hall. Standing Promenade tickets from just £8 go on sale the morning of each performance.
  • BST Hyde Park — Major open-air music festival in the heart of London featuring headline acts from rock, pop, and R&B. Past headliners include Adele, The Rolling Stones, and Bruce Springsteen.
  • Lovebox Festival (Gunnersbury Park) — Two-day electronic, hip-hop, and dance festival with an eclectic line-up in west London.
  • Pride in London (late June or early July) — One of Europe's biggest LGBTQ+ celebrations with a colourful parade through the West End, plus parties and events across the city.
  • Hampton Court Palace Flower Show — The world's largest flower show set against the stunning Tudor palace and gardens.
  • Regent's Park Open Air Theatre — Acclaimed summer season of musicals and Shakespeare under the stars in one of London's most beautiful parks.
  • Wireless Festival (Finsbury Park) — London's premier urban music festival showcasing grime, rap, and electronic acts over a long weekend.

Top Things to Do

  • Hit the rooftop bars — Sip cocktails at the Sky Garden (free entry, book ahead), Madison on the rooftop near St Paul's, or the Boundary in Shoreditch. July evenings are perfect for outdoor drinking with panoramic views.
  • Swim in the Serpentine Lido — Cool off with a swim in Hyde Park's famous lake. The lido section is open to swimmers all summer and costs just a few pounds.
  • Explore the South Bank — Street performers, pop-up food stalls, and free exhibitions at the Southbank Centre, Tate Modern, and the National Theatre. Walk from Westminster Bridge to Tower Bridge.
  • Catch open-air cinema — Screenings at Rooftop Film Club (Bussey Building, Peckham), Luna Cinema at venues across London, and the Screen on the Canal in King's Cross.
  • Picnic in the Royal Parks — Grab supplies from Borough Market or a local deli and head to Regent's Park, St James's Park, or Hampstead Heath for a classic London summer afternoon.
  • Take a Thames river cruise — The long daylight makes an evening cruise magical. MBNA Thames Clippers run regularly, or splash out on a dinner cruise passing the illuminated Houses of Parliament.
  • Day trip to Kew Gardens — The gardens are in full bloom. The Temperate House and Palm House are spectacular, and the Treetop Walkway offers aerial views of the canopy.
  • Walk the Hampstead Heath to Highgate route — Start at Hampstead tube, cross the Heath for stunning views from Parliament Hill, swim in the ponds, then finish with a pint in Highgate village.
  • Evening at Shakespeare's Globe — Groundling tickets from £5 let you stand in the open-air pit just as Elizabethan audiences did. July's light evenings add to the magic.
  • Browse Columbia Road Flower Market — Every Sunday morning, this East London street bursts with flowers, plants, and independent shops. Arrive before 9 am to beat the crowds.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • KERB Camden Market — Dozens of street food traders serving everything from Argentinian asado to Japanese gyoza. Eat canal-side and watch the narrowboats drift by.
  • Maltby Street Market (Bermondsey) — A more local, less touristy alternative to Borough Market, tucked under railway arches with excellent charcuterie, cheese, and craft beer.
  • Bao (Soho, Fitzrovia, Borough) — Queue-worthy Taiwanese steamed buns and small plates. The original Soho branch is tiny; arrive early or try the larger Borough location.
  • Padella (Borough Market) — Hand-rolled pasta at remarkably fair prices. No reservations; the queue moves fast and it's worth every minute of waiting.
  • Pubs with beer gardens — The Spaniards Inn (Hampstead), The Dove (Hammersmith), and The Scolt Head (Dalston) all have lovely outdoor spaces perfect for a July pint.
  • Dishoom (multiple locations) — Bombay-inspired breakfast through dinner. The black daal is legendary. Book ahead or use the walk-in queue at King's Cross, which has a beautiful outdoor terrace.

Insider Tips

  • Book accommodation early — July is peak season. Hotel prices are at their highest and popular options sell out months in advance. Consider Airbnb in zones 2–3 for better value.
  • Use the Tube wisely — Underground trains have no air conditioning on most lines (except the Elizabeth, Jubilee, and newer sections of the Northern line). Travel outside rush hour or take buses with open windows.
  • Wimbledon queue strategy — For ground passes, arrive before 7 am and bring a blanket, snacks, and a book. Resale tickets for Centre and No.1 Court become available after 3 pm at reduced prices.
  • Carry a reusable water bottle — Free refill stations are dotted around the city, including at many museums and rail stations. Dehydration on the Tube is a real risk on hot days.
  • Evenings are the best time to sightsee — With sunset after 9 pm, you can visit attractions during the day and still have hours of golden-hour light for walking, photography, and al fresco dining.

What to Pack

  • Light, breathable layers — t-shirts and shorts for daytime, a light jacket for cooler evenings
  • Sunscreen (SPF 30+) and sunglasses — the sun is strong even when it doesn't feel it
  • A compact umbrella or packable rain jacket for sudden showers
  • Comfortable walking shoes — you'll easily clock 15,000+ steps a day
  • A reusable water bottle
  • A portable phone charger — essential for maps, tickets, and photos all day
