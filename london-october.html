London in October

Autumn colour blazes through the parks, world-class art and film take centre stage, and the cosy season begins.

Weather Overview

Average High: 14 °C (57 °F)  |  Average Low: 8 °C (46 °F)

Rainfall: Around 60 mm across 10–12 rain days. October is one of London's wetter months, with grey overcast skies becoming more common.

Daylight: 10–12 hours. Clocks go back one hour on the last Sunday of October (GMT begins), meaning earlier sunsets from around 5:45 pm by month's end.

What to expect: A transition month. Early October can still feel mild and pleasant, but temperatures drop steadily. Morning fog and misty conditions are common, lending a moody, atmospheric quality to the city. The first frosts may appear by late October.

What's On in October

  • Frieze London (Regent's Park) — One of the world's most important contemporary art fairs, housed in a striking temporary structure. Over 160 galleries from 30+ countries showcase cutting-edge work. Fringe events pop up across the city.
  • BFI London Film Festival — Two weeks of premieres, screenings, and Q&As at venues across the city, centred on BFI Southbank. See films before general release and spot celebrities on the red carpet at the Royal Festival Hall.
  • Halloween events (late October) — Scare attractions at the London Dungeon, the Tower of London, and Hampton Court Palace. Family-friendly events at Kew Gardens (after dark illuminations) and the Natural History Museum.
  • Diwali on the Square (Trafalgar Square) — London's Diwali celebrations with live music, dance performances, food stalls, and a spectacular lighting ceremony. One of the largest Diwali celebrations outside India.
  • Affordable Art Fair (Battersea Park) — A more accessible alternative to Frieze, with original artworks priced from £100. A great opportunity to start or grow an art collection.
  • London Cocktail Week — Hundreds of bars across the city offer special menus, masterclasses, and cocktail trails. A wristband unlocks exclusive drinks and events.
  • Autumn at Kew Gardens — The arboretum blazes with reds, oranges, and golds. The Japanese Gateway area is particularly stunning, and the Temperate House looks magical through morning mist.
  • London Restaurant Festival — Special set menus and dining experiences at restaurants across London, from neighbourhood bistros to Michelin-starred establishments.

Top Things to Do

  • Chase autumn colour in the parks — Richmond Park's ancient oaks turn gold, Hampstead Heath is carpeted in crunchy leaves, and St James's Park has beautiful reflections in its lake. Peak colour usually arrives mid-to-late October.
  • Immerse yourself in art — Beyond Frieze, October sees major autumn exhibitions open at the Royal Academy, Tate Modern, Tate Britain, the National Gallery, and the Barbican. Many gallery openings have free evening events.
  • Watch the deer rut in Richmond Park — October is rutting season. Red and fallow deer stags compete for mates with dramatic displays of antler-clashing and roaring. Keep a safe distance (at least 50 metres) and bring binoculars.
  • Explore Highgate Cemetery — The Victorian Gothic atmosphere is at its most evocative in autumn. The overgrown West Cemetery requires a guided tour (book ahead); the East Cemetery, with Karl Marx's grave, is open daily.
  • Warm up in London's best cafes — October is the month to discover London's coffee scene. Monmouth Coffee (Borough), Prufrock (Clerkenwell), Rosslyn (City), and Ozone (Shoreditch) are all excellent.
  • See a BFI London Film Festival screening — Public tickets go on sale a few weeks before the festival. Screenings at BFI Southbank, Curzon cinemas, and other venues across the city. Q&As with directors add real value.
  • Take a Jack the Ripper walking tour — The dark October evenings add atmosphere to this classic East End tour through Whitechapel's shadowy lanes. Several operators run nightly tours.
  • Visit the Sky Garden at sunset — Free entry (book online) to this 35th-floor garden with panoramic views. October sunsets are earlier, making a late-afternoon visit perfectly timed for golden hour.
  • Explore Dennis Severs' House (Spitalfields) — A candlelit time capsule of 18th-century Huguenot life. The "Silent Night" evening tours, lit entirely by candles, are unforgettable in the autumn darkness. Book well ahead.
  • Day trip to Oxford — Just one hour by train. The university colleges, Bodleian Library, and covered market are atmospheric in autumn. The surrounding countryside is beautiful with fall foliage.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • The Quality Chop House (Farringdon) — A Grade II-listed dining room serving progressive British cuisine. October brings game, root vegetables, and rich autumnal flavours to the menu.
  • Dishoom (multiple locations) — The warming house black daal, slow-cooked for 24 hours, is perfect comfort food as temperatures drop. The chai is outstanding.
  • Cosy pubs with real fires — The Holly Bush (Hampstead), The Lamb and Flag (Covent Garden), and Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese (Fleet Street) all have roaring fires and proper cask ale. October is when London's pub culture really shines.
  • Roti King (Euston) — This basement Malaysian canteen serves some of London's best roti canai and laksa. Warming, affordable, and always packed — a queue is inevitable but moves fast.
  • Hot chocolate at Said (Soho) — This Italian chocolatier serves impossibly thick, rich hot chocolate. A decadent October afternoon treat.

Insider Tips

  • October offers excellent value — It's shoulder season, so hotel prices are significantly lower than summer. Flights are cheaper too, especially mid-week. You can find 4-star hotels at 3-star prices.
  • Half-term crowds — School half-term (usually the last week of October) brings a spike in family visitors to museums and attractions. Plan around it if you prefer quieter visits.
  • Clock change alert — Clocks go back one hour on the last Sunday. Double-check any bookings for that day and enjoy the extra hour of sleep (or an extra hour at the pub).
  • Bring proper rain gear — October rain is more persistent than summer showers. A proper waterproof jacket and water-resistant shoes will serve you better than a flimsy umbrella.
  • Evening planning — With darkness falling by 5–6 pm, plan outdoor sightseeing for the morning and save museums, theatres, and restaurants for the afternoon and evening.

What to Pack

  • A warm, waterproof jacket — ideally one that layers over a jumper
  • A scarf and light gloves for late October mornings and evenings
  • Water-resistant shoes or boots with good grip for wet leaves
  • An umbrella (a sturdy one, not a flimsy fold-up)
  • Layers — long-sleeve tops, jumpers, and a base layer for colder days
  • A crossbody bag for hands-free walking in the rain
