London in December

Christmas magic at every turn — twinkling lights, festive markets, ice rinks, pantomimes, and a spectacular New Year's Eve.

Weather Overview

Average High: 7 °C (45 °F)  |  Average Low: 3 °C (37 °F)

Rainfall: Around 50 mm across 9–11 rain days. Snow is rare in central London but not impossible. More commonly expect grey skies, drizzle, and occasional frost.

Daylight: Just 7–8 hours. Sunrise after 7:45 am, sunset by 3:50 pm around the winter solstice (21 December). The shortest days of the year.

What to expect: Cold, dark, and often damp — but London's Christmas decorations and festive atmosphere more than compensate. The city looks its most magical after dark, which means you get many hours of twinkling lights. Wrap up well and embrace the season.

What's On in December

  • Winter Wonderland (Hyde Park, until early January) — London's flagship Christmas event with a giant observation wheel, ice rink, roller coasters, a circus, Christmas market stalls, and themed bars. Free entry; pay per ride. Go on weekday evenings to avoid the worst crowds.
  • Christmas markets — Southbank Centre Winter Market, Greenwich Market (with a special Christmas section), Leicester Square, and the Trafalgar Square Christmas tree (a gift from Norway since 1947) surrounded by carol singers.
  • Ice skating — Somerset House (the most iconic), the Natural History Museum rink (with the museum as a dramatic backdrop), Hampton Court Palace, and the Tower of London moat rink all operate through December.
  • Pantomimes — A uniquely British tradition. "Oh yes it is!" Hackney Empire, the Palladium, and the Lyric Hammersmith all stage spectacular pantomimes with celebrity casts. Family-friendly and hilariously interactive.
  • Kew Gardens — Christmas at Kew — An after-dark illuminated trail through the botanical gardens, with over a million lights, fire gardens, and a stunning tunnel of light. Book well in advance; it sells out quickly.
  • New Year's Eve fireworks (31 December) — The spectacular display on the Thames, centred on the London Eye, is ticketed (£15, sells out fast). Free viewpoints include Primrose Hill, Parliament Hill, and Greenwich Park.
  • Carol services — Westminster Abbey, St Paul's Cathedral, King's College Chapel, and the Royal Albert Hall all host carol services and concerts. Many are free; some require tickets booked weeks in advance.
  • Nutcracker at the Royal Opera House — The Royal Ballet's annual production is a December institution. Book months ahead for this one; it's a truly magical experience in a stunning venue.

Top Things to Do

  • Walk the Christmas lights — Regent Street's angel lights, Oxford Street's canopy, Carnaby Street's themed display, Bond Street's peacock lights, and Covent Garden's giant Christmas tree and mistletoe chandeliers. Best experienced after dark on a weekday evening.
  • Ice skate at Somerset House — The neoclassical courtyard rink is London's most beautiful. Sessions are 50 minutes; book the twilight slot for the best atmosphere. There's a Christmas arcade with food and drink alongside.
  • See a pantomime — A London December isn't complete without one. Hackney Empire's panto is legendarily good; the London Palladium goes for big-name stars. Ideal for families but enjoyed by all ages.
  • Christmas shopping in Liberty — The Tudor-revival department store on Great Marlborough Street is Christmas shopping at its most atmospheric. Their fabric, beauty, and homewares departments are exceptional. Harrods and Selfridges Christmas floors are also worth a wander.
  • Visit the Tate Modern — The Turbine Hall usually has a major installation, the views from the 10th-floor restaurant are spectacular (especially at sunset around 4 pm), and it's free and warm.
  • Attend a carol service at St Paul's Cathedral — The acoustics in Wren's masterpiece are breathtaking. Evensong services are free; the special Christmas concerts are ticketed but unforgettable.
  • Warm up at a historic pub — The George Inn (Borough, London's last galleried coaching inn), The Olde Mitre (Holborn, hidden down an alley), and The Grenadier (Belgravia) are all wonderfully cosy in December.
  • Explore the V&A after hours — Friday Lates run until 10 pm with special December programming, often including festive workshops, live music, and access to galleries with far fewer visitors.
  • Day trip to Bath Christmas Market — 90 minutes by train. Over 170 wooden chalets around the Roman Baths and Bath Abbey. One of the UK's best Christmas markets, with a focus on independent and local makers.
  • New Year's Eve — Beyond the fireworks, many restaurants offer special NYE dinners (book months ahead). Pubs throw parties. The South Bank comes alive. For a quieter celebration, the Geffrye Museum hosts candlelit evening events.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • The Ivy (West End) — Dressed to the nines for Christmas with a spectacular exterior display. The set lunch menu is surprisingly accessible. Book well ahead for December evenings.
  • Hawksmoor (multiple locations) — If the cold calls for a proper steak, this is London's best. The Air Street location near Piccadilly Circus has a beautiful Art Deco bar for pre-dinner cocktails.
  • Mulled wine crawl — Start at Gordon's Wine Bar (Embankment), walk along the South Bank hitting Christmas market stalls, and finish at the George Bankside or one of Borough Market's wine bars.
  • Christmas afternoon tea — Most hotels offer festive versions. Claridge's, The Ritz, and The Savoy are classic choices; Sketch and The Berkeley do creative themed versions. Book 4–6 weeks ahead.
  • Pie and mash — A traditional London winter warmer. M. Manze (Tower Bridge Road, the oldest surviving pie and mash shop, est. 1902) serves them the proper way — with liquor (parsley sauce).
  • Borough Market festive specials — The market runs special December programming with mince pies, mulled cider, and seasonal produce. Open Monday to Saturday; quietest on weekday mornings.

Insider Tips

  • Book NYE fireworks tickets immediately — They sell out within days of going on sale (usually in October). Without a ticket, you won't get close to the Thames. Alternative: book a rooftop bar or restaurant with a view.
  • Avoid Oxford Street on Saturdays — The crowds in December are genuinely overwhelming. Shop on weekday mornings or switch to Marylebone High Street, King's Road, or Seven Dials for a calmer experience.
  • Christmas Day is quiet — Almost everything closes, including public transport. If you're in London on 25 December, plan ahead: book a hotel that serves Christmas lunch, stock up on supplies, and enjoy the eerily empty streets for a walk.
  • Transport on NYE — TfL typically runs free services on the Tube, buses, and Overground from around 11:45 pm on 31 December until the early hours. Check the TfL website for the exact schedule each year.
  • Early December is the sweet spot — The first two weeks of December have all the Christmas atmosphere with lower hotel prices and fewer tourists than the period between Christmas and New Year. Most lights and markets are up from mid-November.

What to Pack

  • A heavy winter coat — wool or down, ideally waterproof
  • Thermal base layers, especially if you plan to be outdoors for markets or NYE
  • A warm hat, thick scarf, and insulated gloves
  • Waterproof, insulated boots with good grip
  • Hand and toe warmers for ice skating and fireworks
  • A portable umbrella for unpredictable rain and sleet
