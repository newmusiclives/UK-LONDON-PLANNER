Shoreditch Neighbourhood Guide

Street art, tech startups, vintage markets, and London's most creative nightlife scene

Overview

Shoreditch sits at the heart of East London's creative revolution. Once a gritty post-industrial area, it has transformed into London's most dynamic neighbourhood for art, tech, food, and nightlife. The streets around Shoreditch High Street, Brick Lane, and Rivington Street form a constantly evolving canvas of murals, galleries, pop-ups, and independent businesses.

The vibe: Creative, edgy, and always evolving. Shoreditch moves fast. The warehouse rave of last month becomes next month's coffee roastery. Street art appears and disappears overnight. The tech startup scene buzzes from co-working spaces alongside vintage markets that have traded for generations.

Best for: Street art lovers, tech workers, vintage shoppers, craft beer enthusiasts, and anyone who wants to experience London's cutting-edge culture. Shoreditch is best explored on foot, ideally on a Sunday when all the markets are in full swing.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Brick Lane

    Brick Lane, E1 6QL

    The famous East London street stretching from Bethnal Green to Whitechapel. Famous for its Bangladeshi restaurants, vintage shops, bagel bakeries, and the enormous Sunday market that spreads through surrounding streets and yards.

  • 2. Shoreditch Street Art

    Rivington Street, Great Eastern Street & surrounds

    Shoreditch is arguably Europe's street art capital. The area around Rivington Street, Curtain Road, and the alleys off Brick Lane feature works by ROA, Stik, Ben Eine, and rotating international artists. The walls change constantly.

  • 3. BoxPark Shoreditch

    2-10 Bethnal Green Road, E1 6GY

    The world's first pop-up shopping mall, built from shipping containers. Now a street food destination with stalls serving cuisines from around the world, plus a rooftop bar and regular events.

  • 4. Old Spitalfields Market

    16 Horner Square, E1 6EW

    A covered market dating back to 1638, now hosting independent designers, antiques, vinyl, and street food. Open daily but best on Thursdays (antiques) and Sundays (everything). The surrounding streets have excellent independent shops.

  • 5. Dennis Severs' House

    18 Folgate Street, E1 6BX

    A unique immersive experience in a Georgian townhouse frozen in time. Each room tells the story of a fictional Huguenot silk-weaving family through sights, sounds, and smells. The candlelit Monday evening visits are extraordinary. Book well ahead.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Dishoom Shoreditch

    7 Boundary Street, E2 7JE • Bombay cafe • $$

    The Shoreditch outpost of London's most beloved Indian restaurant chain, inspired by Bombay's Irani cafes. The black daal is legendary, the bacon naan roll at breakfast is life-changing, and the chai is perfect. Expect a queue at weekends.

  • 2. Smokestak

    35 Sclater Street, E1 6LB • BBQ • $$

    Serious low-and-slow BBQ from a former street food stall. The 35-day aged brisket, smoked over oak and hickory, is some of the best in Europe. The pickles and sides are equally outstanding. Industrial-chic space.

  • 3. Gunpowder

    11 White's Row, E1 7NF • Indian • $$

    Family-recipe Indian small plates in a tiny Spitalfields dining room. The spiced venison doughnut and Kashmiri lamb chops are exceptional. No reservations for parties under six, so arrive early.

  • 4. Brawn

    49 Columbia Road, E2 7RG • European • $$

    A neighbourhood restaurant serving honest, seasonal European cooking with a natural wine list that is one of London's best. The charcuterie and daily-changing menu are consistently excellent. Relaxed, buzzy atmosphere.

  • 5. Beigel Bake

    159 Brick Lane, E1 6SB • Bakery • $

    Open 24 hours, this Brick Lane institution has been hand-rolling bagels since 1974. The salt beef beigel is the essential Shoreditch eat, costing under three pounds. There is always a queue, and it always moves fast.

Best Bars & Pubs

  • 1. Happiness Forgets

    8-9 Hoxton Square, N1 6NU

    A basement cocktail bar on Hoxton Square regularly ranked among the world's best. Intimate, no-nonsense, with perfectly executed classic and original cocktails. Book a table or try walking in early evening.

  • 2. The Ten Bells

    84 Commercial Street, E1 6LY

    A beautifully preserved Victorian pub with ornate tiled walls, opposite Spitalfields Market. Notorious for its Jack the Ripper connections but now known for excellent craft beer, natural wines, and a great upstairs dining room.

  • 3. Discount Suit Company

    29A Wentworth Street, E1 7TB

    A speakeasy-style cocktail bar hidden in a basement beneath a former suit shop near Petticoat Lane. Exposed brick, dim lighting, and creative cocktails at very reasonable prices for the quality. Cash only for tabs under a certain amount.

  • 4. The Owl and Pussycat

    34 Redchurch Street, E2 7DP

    A cosy corner pub on one of Shoreditch's best streets. Great cask ales, a roaring fire in winter, and a more relaxed pace than the surrounding bars. Popular with locals rather than tourists.

  • 5. XOYO

    32-37 Cowper Street, EC2A 4AP

    One of London's best music venues and nightclubs, hosting a rotating cast of resident DJs every quarter alongside live gigs. The sound system is superb. Check their programme for everything from techno to hip-hop to experimental.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Allpress Espresso

    58 Redchurch Street, E2 7DP

    A New Zealand roaster's flagship London cafe in a converted Shoreditch warehouse. Watch beans being roasted while drinking some of East London's finest flat whites. The cabinet food is excellent too.

  • 2. Ozone Coffee Roasters

    11 Leonard Street, EC2A 4AQ

    A sprawling two-floor cafe and roastery with an all-day brunch menu that goes far beyond the usual. Excellent single-origin filter coffees and a full cocktail bar in the evenings.

  • 3. Nude Espresso

    26 Hanbury Street, E1 6QR

    A Shoreditch specialty coffee pioneer near Brick Lane. Their East blend is a staple of London's coffee scene. The small space fills fast on weekends, so visit on a weekday for a more relaxed experience.

Shopping Highlights

  • Brick Lane Sunday Market — A sprawling mix of vintage clothing, street food, bric-a-brac, and local crafts stretching through the Truman Brewery complex and surrounding streets.
  • Redchurch Street — The area's most curated shopping street with Sunspel, Aesop, Labour and Wait, and independent boutiques alongside excellent cafes.
  • Columbia Road Flower Market — Every Sunday morning, this quiet street erupts into London's most beautiful flower market. Arrive early (8am) to beat the crowds.
  • Rough Trade East — London's best independent record shop, inside the Truman Brewery. New releases, vinyl, in-store gigs, and a cafe.
  • Beyond Retro — A vast vintage clothing warehouse on Cheshire Street with thousands of pieces from every decade, sold by the kilo or per item.

Getting There

Shoreditch is well connected by Overground and Tube, with several stations around its edges:

  • Shoreditch High Street (London Overground) — right in the centre of the action
  • Liverpool Street (Central, Circle, Hammersmith & City, Metropolitan, Elizabeth lines) — a 10-minute walk south
  • Old Street (Northern line) — the northern edge, near the "Silicon Roundabout" tech hub
  • Hoxton (London Overground) — for Hoxton Square and the northern part of the area

Insider Tips

  • The best street art changes constantly. Walk the alleys between Rivington Street and Curtain Road and the Hanbury Street/Brick Lane junction for the freshest pieces.
  • Sunday is the ultimate Shoreditch day. Start at Columbia Road Flower Market at 8am, walk to Brick Lane Market by 10am, brunch on Redchurch Street, and finish with afternoon drinks on Hoxton Square.
  • Brick Lane's ground-floor curry houses that employ touts outside are generally tourist traps. Instead, head to Gunpowder, Tayyabs (nearby on Fieldgate Street), or the Lahore Kebab House.
  • For the best vintage shopping, explore the warehouses and side streets off Brick Lane rather than the main road stalls.
  • The area around Curtain Road and Great Eastern Street has the densest concentration of nightlife. Friday and Saturday nights are packed, but Thursday club nights often have better DJs and smaller crowds.
