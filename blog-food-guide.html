The Ultimate London Food Guide

From Borough Market to Brick Lane, neighbourhood by neighbourhood — where and what to eat in one of the world's great food cities

The old joke about British food being terrible has not applied to London for decades. The city is now one of the most exciting food destinations on the planet, with more than 70 Michelin-starred restaurants, street food from every corner of the globe, and a thriving independent restaurant scene that rivals New York, Tokyo, and Paris. Whether you want a three-pound samosa from a market stall or a two-hundred-pound tasting menu, London delivers. This guide takes you through the city's best food neighbourhoods, must-try dishes, and our top picks at every budget level.

Food by Neighbourhood

Borough Market — The Epicentre

London's oldest and most famous food market sits under the railway arches near London Bridge. It has been a market site for over 1,000 years, though its current incarnation as a gourmet food destination took shape in the 1990s. Visit on a weekday for a calmer experience or on Saturday for the full electric atmosphere.

Must-try: Raclette cheese from Kappacasein (melted cheese scraped over potatoes and pickles for around 8 pounds), a Scotch egg from Scotchtails, sourdough from Bread Ahead bakery, and a flat white from Monmouth Coffee. For a sit-down meal, Padella serves handmade pasta with queues that move quickly — the cacio e pepe for 6 pounds is one of the best value meals in London.

Brick Lane — Curry, Bagels, and Street Art

Brick Lane in East London has layers of food history. It was the heart of the Bangladeshi community from the 1970s and still has dozens of curry houses, though the quality varies widely. More recently, it has become a hub for trendy restaurants, vintage shops, and some of London's best street food.

Must-try: A salt beef bagel from Beigel Bake, open 24 hours and serving bagels since 1974 for just 5 pounds. For curry, skip the touts on the main road and head to Tayyabs on Fieldgate Street — their lamb chops are legendary and a meal costs around 15 pounds per person. On Sundays, the Backyard Market and Boiler House Food Hall have excellent global street food for 7 to 10 pounds.

Chinatown — Dim Sum Central

London's Chinatown around Gerrard Street in Soho is compact but packed with restaurants. It is the best area for affordable dim sum, roast duck, and hand-pulled noodles, all within walking distance of Leicester Square and the West End.

Must-try: Dim sum at Dumplings Legend (their xiao long bao are excellent), roast duck and char siu at Four Seasons on Wardour Street, and bubble tea from one of the many tea shops. A filling dim sum lunch costs around 12 to 18 pounds per person. For something special, try Barshu for fiery Sichuan cuisine — the mapo tofu and boiled fish with chilli are outstanding.

Brixton Village — Global Flavours Under One Roof

Brixton Village and the adjacent Market Row form a covered market arcade in south London that has transformed from a struggling market into one of London's most diverse and exciting food destinations. The atmosphere is vibrant, with restaurants from across the globe packed side by side.

Must-try: Jerk chicken at Fish, Wings and Tings, Colombian arepas at Casa Morita, and Ethiopian injera platters at Salon. Franco Manca, which started here before expanding across London, still serves some of the best sourdough pizza in the city for under 8 pounds. For cocktails afterwards, head to the nearby Shrub and Shutter.

Soho — The Restaurant District

Soho has the highest concentration of restaurants in London. It ranges from cheap eats to Michelin stars, with everything in between. The area is particularly strong for Asian food, Italian, and trendy modern British dining.

Must-try: Bao on Lexington Street for fluffy Taiwanese steamed buns (expect a queue), Kiln for fiery Thai grill dishes, Barrafina for tapas at the counter, and Koya for handmade udon noodles. For a special occasion, Brasserie Zedel offers a grand Parisian brasserie experience with a prix fixe menu starting at just 14 pounds — extraordinary value for the setting.

Shoreditch and Dalston — The Cutting Edge

East London is where the city's most adventurous chefs set up shop. The food scene here changes fast, but the quality remains consistently high. From Turkish ocakbasi grills on Kingsland Road to inventive tasting menus in converted warehouses, this is where London's food culture pushes forward.

Must-try: Mangal II on Kingsland Road for Turkish grilled meats, Brawn on Columbia Road for nose-to-tail French-British cooking, and Smokestak for brisket smoked for 20 hours. For street food, Boxpark Shoreditch has dozens of stalls under one roof.

Must-Try London Dishes

No visit to London is complete without trying these iconic dishes:

  • Full English Breakfast — Bacon, sausage, eggs, beans, toast, mushrooms, tomatoes, black pudding. Try it at The Regency Cafe near Westminster or E Pellicci in Bethnal Green.
  • Fish and Chips — Battered cod or haddock with thick-cut chips and mushy peas. Poppies in Spitalfields or The Golden Hind in Marylebone do it properly.
  • Sunday Roast — Roast beef, Yorkshire pudding, roast potatoes, and gravy. The Harwood Arms in Fulham has a Michelin star; The Anchor and Hope in Waterloo is a more affordable classic.
  • Pie and Mash — A true East End tradition. M Manze in Tower Bridge Road has been serving since 1902. The pie, mash, and liquor (parsley sauce) costs under 6 pounds.
  • Afternoon Tea — Finger sandwiches, scones with clotted cream and jam, and miniature cakes. The Ritz is the most famous (from 60 pounds), but Sketch in Mayfair has the best Instagram-worthy setting.
  • Chicken Tikka Masala — Britain's adopted national dish. Dishoom serves an excellent version alongside their signature black daal.
  • Sticky Toffee Pudding — The quintessential British dessert. Most pubs serve it, but The Ivy's version is particularly good.

Best Street Food Markets

  • Borough Market — The original and still the best. Open Wednesday to Saturday.
  • Maltby Street Market — Smaller, less touristy. Under the railway arches in Bermondsey. Saturdays and Sundays.
  • Camden Market — Huge variety of global street food. The canal-side stalls are atmospheric.
  • Mercato Metropolitano — A sustainable food market in Elephant and Castle with Italian and international food.
  • Kerb at King's Cross — Rotating street food traders in Granary Square. Weekday lunchtimes.
  • Broadway Market — Saturday market in Hackney with artisan food producers and prepared food stalls.
  • Berwick Street Market — A historic Soho market with excellent lunchtime street food options.

Fine Dining Worth the Splurge

If you want one special meal, these restaurants are worth every pound:

  • Dishoom — Not fine dining in the traditional sense, but the Bombay cafe experience is unforgettable. Book ahead. Around 30 to 40 pounds per person.
  • Brasserie Zedel — A palatial underground French brasserie with mains from 14 pounds. The setting alone is worth the visit.
  • The Clove Club — Modern British tasting menu in Shoreditch with a Michelin star. Around 150 pounds for the full experience.
  • St John — The original nose-to-tail restaurant in Smithfield. Roast bone marrow and parsley salad is a London icon. Around 50 to 60 pounds per person.
  • The River Cafe — Italian cooking at its most refined, in a Thames-side setting in Hammersmith. Splurge-worthy at around 80 to 100 pounds.

Best Budget Eats (Under 10 Pounds)

  • Padella — Handmade pasta from 4 pounds at Borough Market.
  • Beigel Bake — Salt beef bagels for 5 pounds on Brick Lane, open 24 hours.
  • Franco Manca — Sourdough pizza from 6.50 pounds. Multiple locations.
  • Flat Iron — A full steak dinner for about 14 pounds. Multiple locations.
  • Bao — Steamed buns from 4.50 pounds in Soho.
  • Koya — Handmade udon noodles from 9 pounds in Soho.
  • E Pellicci — A Bethnal Green institution. Full English breakfast for under 9 pounds in a listed Art Deco interior.
  • The Regency Cafe — Classic greasy spoon near Westminster. Generous portions under 8 pounds.

Best Brunch Spots

Londoners take brunch seriously. Here are the spots worth queuing for:

  • The Breakfast Club — Multiple locations. Pancake stacks, full English, and huevos rancheros. Expect queues at weekends.
  • Dishoom — Their bacon naan roll with cream cheese and chilli jam is one of London's best brunch dishes.
  • Sunday in Brooklyn — Notting Hill location of the famous New York brunch spot. The souffle pancakes are extraordinary.
  • Ottolenghi — Multiple locations. Mediterranean-inspired brunch with outstanding pastries and salads.
  • The Wolseley — Grand European-style cafe on Piccadilly. Their eggs Benedict in the Art Deco dining room is a London institution.
  • Caravan — King's Cross and other locations. Excellent coffee roasted in-house and globally inspired brunch dishes.
