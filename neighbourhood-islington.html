Islington Neighbourhood Guide

Gastropub heaven, antique treasures, Regent's Canal walks, world-class dance at Sadler's Wells, and the bustle of Upper Street

Overview

Islington is one of north London's most desirable neighbourhoods, centred on the mile-long Upper Street — one of London's best strips for dining, drinking, and independent shopping. Georgian townhouses line elegant squares, while the Regent's Canal provides a peaceful waterside walk connecting Angel to Camden. The area has a strong theatrical tradition, anchored by Sadler's Wells, the Almeida Theatre, and the Screen on the Green cinema.

The vibe: Cosmopolitan and convivial. Islington hums with the energy of after-work drinks on Upper Street, Saturday morning antique hunting in Camden Passage, and Sunday lunches in gastropubs. It is progressive, sociable, and has a genuine neighbourhood feel despite being minutes from central London.

Best for: Gastropub enthusiasts, theatre lovers, antique hunters, canal walkers, and anyone who enjoys a neighbourhood with excellent food, good independent shops, and a lively but not overwhelming atmosphere.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Sadler's Wells Theatre

    Rosebery Avenue, EC1R 4TN

    The world's leading dance theatre, presenting everything from classical ballet to cutting-edge contemporary dance, hip-hop, and flamenco. The 1,500-seat main auditorium and the intimate Lilian Baylis Studio host extraordinary performances year-round. Check listings for same-day rush tickets.

  • 2. Camden Passage Antiques Market

    Camden Passage, N1 8EA

    A charming pedestrianised lane of antique shops, vintage boutiques, and market stalls. Not to be confused with Camden Market — this is a much more refined affair. Wednesday and Saturday are the main market days, but the permanent shops are open throughout the week.

  • 3. Regent's Canal Walk

    Towpath from Angel to Camden, N1

    The towpath section from Islington through the 878-metre Islington Tunnel to Camden Lock is one of London's most enjoyable walks. Pass narrowboats, waterside cafes, and the beautiful gardens behind the townhouses. About 30 minutes from Angel to Camden.

  • 4. Almeida Theatre

    Almeida Street, N1 1TA

    One of London's most respected fringe theatres, producing bold new writing and adventurous revivals in an intimate 325-seat auditorium. Productions regularly transfer to the West End and Broadway. Pre-show dining at the attached Almeida restaurant is a treat.

  • 5. Chapel Market

    Chapel Market, N1 9EZ

    A traditional London street market running Tuesday to Sunday with fruit, vegetables, flowers, clothing, and household goods. One of the last proper working-class markets in the area, offering an authentic slice of local life alongside the more gentrified parts of Islington.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Ottolenghi

    287 Upper Street, N1 2TZ • Mediterranean • $$

    The original Ottolenghi deli-restaurant that launched Yotam Ottolenghi's food empire. The counter display of salads and cakes is legendary. Queue for a table and enjoy inventive Middle Eastern-Mediterranean dishes or take away from the deli counter.

  • 2. Trullo

    300-302 St Paul's Road, N1 2LH • Italian • $$

    A much-loved neighbourhood Italian making fresh pasta daily, with a charcoal grill turning out exceptional meats. The pappardelle with beef shin ragu and the charcoal-grilled lamb chops are highlights. Relaxed, well-priced, and consistently excellent.

  • 3. Smokehouse Islington

    63-69 Canonbury Road, N1 2DG • Barbecue • $$

    A gastropub dedicated to the art of smoking and barbecue. The 14-hour smoked brisket is extraordinary, and the Sunday roasts are among the best in London. The upstairs dining room is more refined; the ground-floor bar is casual and lively.

  • 4. Sabor

    36 Heddon Street, but Islington branch: 232 Upper Street, N1 1RL • Spanish • $$

    Outstanding Turkish-inspired grill and meze from the Islington stretch of Upper Street. Charcoal-grilled lamb chops, aubergine with tahini, and freshly baked pide make this a standout for casual, flavour-packed dining.

  • 5. Afghan Kitchen

    35 Islington Green, N1 8DU • Afghan • $

    A tiny, spartan restaurant serving a short menu of exceptional Afghan dishes — lamb stews, spinach with rice, and aromatic soups. Just a handful of tables and no frills, but the food is deeply flavourful and astonishingly cheap. Cash only.

Best Pubs & Bars

  • 1. The Drapers Arms

    44 Barnsbury Street, N1 1ER

    One of London's finest gastropubs, occupying a handsome Georgian building on a quiet residential street. The food is superb — think whole roast chicken for two and exceptional Sunday roasts. The large beer garden is one of north London's best-kept secrets.

  • 2. The Charles Lamb

    16 Elia Street, N1 8DE

    A tiny, charming corner pub named after the essayist who lived nearby. Outstanding seasonal British cooking, well-kept ales, and a genuinely local atmosphere. The small size means it fills quickly — arrive early, especially for Sunday lunch.

  • 3. 69 Colebrooke Row

    69 Colebrooke Row, N1 8AA

    A tiny, beautifully designed cocktail bar known as "The Bar With No Name." Tony Conigliaro's inventive, science-driven cocktails are extraordinary — the Terroir martini made with distilled clay is legendary. Book ahead or arrive early for one of the few bar stools.

  • 4. The Old Queen's Head

    44 Essex Road, N1 8LN

    A beautiful Elizabethan-era pub with carved fireplaces, wood panelling, and a lively modern atmosphere. Good craft beer, regular DJ nights, a popular weekend brunch, and one of the most impressive historic interiors on Essex Road.

  • 5. The Canonbury Tavern

    21 Canonbury Place, N1 2NS

    A grand Victorian pub overlooking Canonbury Square with a sprawling beer garden, good ales, and a reliable pub food menu. The Sunday quiz is popular, and the garden is one of the largest in the area. A proper neighbourhood local.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Towpath Cafe

    Regent's Canal Towpath, N1 7EG (between Whitmore Bridge and Kingsland Road Bridge)

    A legendary canalside cafe occupying a row of lock-keeper's arches along the Regent's Canal. Simple, seasonal food — bruschetta, soups, baked goods — served at outdoor tables overlooking the water. Cash only, weather dependent, and well worth seeking out.

  • 2. Redemption Roasters

    84 Lamb's Conduit Street, WC1N 3LR

    A specialty coffee roastery with a social mission, training prison inmates as baristas. The coffee is excellent — roasted in HMP The Mount — and the cafe is a bright, welcoming space. A short walk from Angel, and a coffee with genuine purpose.

  • 3. The Fields Beneath

    52A Prince of Wales Road, NW5 3LN

    A tiny specialty coffee shop tucked under a railway arch near Kentish Town West. Outstanding single-origin espresso and batch brew, with a focus on ethically sourced beans. Minimal seating but maximum coffee quality.

Shopping Highlights

  • Camden Passage — A charming pedestrianised lane packed with antique shops, vintage dealers, and specialist collectible stores. The outdoor market runs Wednesday and Saturday mornings.
  • Upper Street — Islington's main drag with a mix of high street brands, independent boutiques, bookshops, and homewares stores stretching from Angel to Highbury Corner.
  • Annie's Vintage — A beautifully curated vintage clothing shop on Camden Passage specialising in 1920s-1970s fashion, accessories, and costume jewellery.
  • Aria — A gorgeous homewares and gift shop on Barnsbury Hall stocking contemporary design, ceramics, glassware, and jewellery from independent makers.
  • Essex Road — The scruffier, more characterful parallel street to Upper Street with vinyl record shops, charity shops with good pickings, and independent food shops.

Getting There

Islington is very well connected to central London:

  • Angel (Northern line) — the main station for Islington, at the southern end of Upper Street and right next to Camden Passage
  • Highbury & Islington (Victoria line, London Overground) — for the northern end of Upper Street and connections to east London via Overground
  • Essex Road (National Rail, Great Northern) — a quiet alternative station useful for the middle of Islington and Essex Road shops

Insider Tips

  • Camden Passage is best on Wednesday mornings and Saturday mornings when the outdoor antique stalls are set up. Arrive early for the best finds — dealers start from 7am.
  • Walk the Regent's Canal from Angel to Camden Lock (about 30 minutes). Start at the Colebrooke Row bridge near Angel and follow the towpath west. You will pass through the Islington Tunnel — take the street-level detour signed on the towpath.
  • For pre-theatre dining near Sadler's Wells, try the restaurants on St John Street and Exmouth Market, a five-minute walk south of the theatre.
  • The Screen on the Green (83 Upper Street) is one of London's most beautiful single-screen cinemas. Catch a film from one of its sofas for a vintage cinema experience.
  • Cross Street and Liverpool Road, running parallel to Upper Street, have many of Islington's best pubs and restaurants away from the main drag's crowds.
