48 Hours in London: The Ultimate Weekend Guide

A complete Saturday and Sunday itinerary to experience the very best of London in just one weekend

London is a city that could take a lifetime to explore, but what if you only have a weekend? Whether you are flying in for a quick break or just passing through, 48 hours is enough to experience the highlights, sample incredible food, and soak up the atmosphere that makes London one of the greatest cities on earth. This guide gives you a realistic, tested itinerary that balances iconic landmarks with local favourites, great meals with affordable options, and packed sightseeing with moments to simply enjoy the city.

Saturday: Icons, Markets, and the South Bank

Saturday Morning (8:00 AM - 12:00 PM)

Start your weekend at Borough Market, which opens at 8 AM on Saturdays. This is London's oldest food market, dating back over 1,000 years, and it is the perfect place to grab breakfast. Pick up a bacon roll from one of the street food stalls, or try the famous raclette cheese from Kappacasein. Wash it down with a flat white from Monmouth Coffee, widely considered one of the best coffee roasters in London.

From Borough Market, walk across Tower Bridge — it takes about 15 minutes. The views from the bridge itself are free and spectacular. If you want to go inside the Tower Bridge Exhibition and walk across the glass floor, tickets cost around 12 pounds. Continue to the Tower of London on the north bank. Even if you do not go inside (tickets are about 33 pounds), the exterior and grounds are impressive. The Crown Jewels are inside if you decide to splurge.

Budget tip: If you plan to visit the Tower of London, book online in advance for a discount of around 10 percent.

Saturday Afternoon (12:00 PM - 5:00 PM)

Hop on the Tube to Westminster station. As you exit, the Palace of Westminster and Big Ben rise dramatically in front of you — one of the most photographed views in the world. Walk through Parliament Square past the statues of Churchill, Mandela, and Gandhi, then continue to Westminster Abbey. The exterior is free to admire; entry to the Abbey costs around 27 pounds.

Stroll through St James's Park, London's most beautiful royal park, towards Buckingham Palace. The park is free and gorgeous in any season. Check if the Changing of the Guard is happening (usually at 11 AM on certain days, so you may catch the tail end or plan to see it on Sunday). Walk up The Mall to Trafalgar Square and pop into the National Gallery — it is completely free and houses masterpieces by Van Gogh, Monet, Turner, and da Vinci.

For lunch, head to Chinatown, just a five-minute walk from Trafalgar Square. Grab dim sum at Four Seasons or Dumplings Legend. A filling lunch here costs around 10 to 15 pounds per person.

Saturday Evening (5:00 PM - Late)

Walk south to the South Bank along the Thames. This riverside stretch is magical in the evening. Pass the London Eye, the Southbank Centre, and the Tate Modern (free entry, open until 10 PM on Fridays and Saturdays). The views from the Tate Modern's viewing platform on level 10 are free and stunning.

For dinner, Flat Iron in Covent Garden serves an excellent steak for around 14 pounds — arguably the best value steak in London. Alternatively, head to Dishoom in King's Cross for outstanding Indian food; try the black daal and a bacon naan roll.

End your Saturday evening at a West End show. TKTS in Leicester Square sells same-day discounted tickets. Expect to pay 25 to 50 pounds for a good seat. If theatre is not your thing, explore the cocktail bars of Soho — try the French House on Dean Street for a genuinely old-school London pub experience, or Cahoots for a 1940s-themed speakeasy.

Budget tip: Many West End shows offer day seats or lottery tickets for under 25 pounds. Check the specific show's website on the morning of your visit.

Sunday: Royalty, Markets, and Hidden Gems

Sunday Morning (9:00 AM - 12:00 PM)

Start with brunch at The Breakfast Club in Soho or Angel — their pancake stacks and full English breakfasts are legendary. Arrive early or expect a queue; it is that popular.

Take the Tube to Notting Hill and wander through Portobello Road Market. On Sundays the market is slightly quieter than Saturdays, focused on vintage clothing, antiques, and bric-a-brac. The colourful houses along the road are iconic. Walk through the charming side streets and browse the independent bookshops and boutiques.

From Notting Hill, it is a short walk to Kensington Gardens and Hyde Park. Wander through the Italian Gardens, visit the Peter Pan statue, and walk along the Serpentine. In good weather, hire a rowing boat on the Serpentine for around 14 pounds per hour.

Sunday Afternoon (12:00 PM - 5:00 PM)

Time for a proper Sunday roast — the quintessential British weekend meal. The Lamb and Flag in Covent Garden, The Eagle in Farringdon, or The Harwood Arms in Fulham (the only Michelin-starred pub in London) all serve outstanding roasts. Expect to pay 15 to 22 pounds for a main course at most pubs, or around 30 pounds at the Harwood Arms.

After lunch, visit the British Museum — it is free and you could spend days inside. Focus on the highlights: the Rosetta Stone, the Elgin Marbles, the Egyptian mummies, and the stunning Great Court designed by Norman Foster. Two hours here is enough to see the essentials.

If you prefer something quirkier, head to Camden Town instead. The Camden Market is open on Sundays and is packed with street food from around the world, vintage clothing, and live music. The canalside setting is atmospheric and the food stalls offer meals for 6 to 10 pounds.

Sunday Evening (5:00 PM - Late)

For your final evening, head to Shoreditch for a taste of London's creative, edgy side. Start at Boxpark Shoreditch for street food and drinks in a buzzing atmosphere made from repurposed shipping containers. Walk down Brick Lane for curry mile and some of London's best street art.

For a farewell drink, try Sky Garden at 20 Fenchurch Street — the highest public garden in London with panoramic views across the city. Entry is free but you must book online in advance. Alternatively, head to the Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese on Fleet Street, a 17th-century pub rebuilt after the Great Fire of London, where Dickens and Dr Johnson used to drink.

If you have energy left, catch some live music at the Jazz Cafe in Camden, comedy at the Comedy Store in Leicester Square, or late-night drinks at Gordon's Wine Bar — London's oldest wine bar, set in a candlelit cellar near Embankment.

Budget Breakdown

Here is a realistic budget for a 48-hour London weekend:

  • Transport: Get an Oyster card or use contactless. Weekend daily cap is around 8.10 pounds on the Tube. Total: 16 to 20 pounds.
  • Food: Budget 50 to 80 pounds for two days if you mix street food, pubs, and one nicer meal.
  • Attractions: Many top attractions are free. If you visit the Tower of London and a West End show, add 60 to 80 pounds.
  • Drinks: A pint averages 6 to 7 pounds. Budget 30 to 50 pounds for the weekend.
  • Total: 120 to 230 pounds per person (excluding accommodation), depending on how much you splurge.

Essential Tips for Your 48 Hours

  • Use contactless payment everywhere. London is essentially cashless. Your bank card or phone will work on the Tube, buses, and in virtually every shop and restaurant.
  • Walk between attractions. Central London is surprisingly compact. Walking from Westminster to the South Bank to Covent Garden takes about 30 minutes and you see far more than on the Tube.
  • Book popular restaurants in advance. Dishoom, Flat Iron, and similar spots fill up quickly at weekends. Book online a few days ahead.
  • Download the TfL Go app. It shows real-time Tube and bus information and helps you plan routes.
  • Carry a light rain jacket. London weather is unpredictable. Even in summer, a brief shower is always possible.
  • Avoid black cabs for long journeys. The Tube is faster and far cheaper. Use cabs only for short hops or late at night.
