London in February

Chinese New Year fireworks, Six Nations rugby roars, and Valentine's romance — February packs more punch than you'd expect.

🌡️ Weather Overview

February is still firmly winter, but the days are noticeably lengthening and early signs of spring start to appear:

  • Average temperature: 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) — similar to January, but occasionally milder spells push into double digits
  • Rainfall: Around 40mm across 11-12 rainy days — actually one of London's driest months
  • Daylight hours: 9 to 10.5 hours, noticeably longer than January, with sunset extending past 5pm by month's end
  • Wind: February can be gusty — wrap up if walking along the Thames or across bridges

By late February you may spot the first crocuses in Hyde Park and St James's Park. The lengthening evenings make a real psychological difference after the dark weeks of January.

🎭 What's On in February

February is one of London's most culturally vibrant months:

  • Chinese New Year (late Jan/early Feb): Europe's largest Chinese New Year celebrations take over the West End. Expect a grand parade through Chinatown to Trafalgar Square, lion dances, firecrackers, food stalls, and a stunning stage show. Free to attend.
  • Six Nations Rugby (weekends in Feb-Mar): England play at Twickenham, and the atmosphere across London's rugby pubs is electric. The Cabbage Patch in Twickenham and The Harlequin in Farringdon are top spots to watch.
  • Valentine's Day (14 Feb): London pulls out all the stops. Special menus appear across the city, the London Eye offers champagne capsules, and Kew Gardens hosts romantic evening events.
  • London Fashion Week (mid-Feb): While shows are industry-only, the energy spills onto the streets. Spot the fashion crowd around Somerset House, The Strand, and Soho. Pop-up events and exhibitions are often open to the public.
  • Pancake Day / Shrove Tuesday (date varies): Pancake races take place across the city, including the famous Great Spitalfields Pancake Race and races at The Houses of Parliament. Many restaurants offer special pancake menus.
  • Half-Term Activities (mid-Feb): School holidays mean museums and attractions lay on extra family programming. The Natural History Museum, Science Museum, and London Zoo all run special events.
  • Kew Orchids Festival (Feb-Mar): The Princess of Wales Conservatory at Kew Gardens is transformed with thousands of orchids in dazzling displays themed around a different country each year.

✨ Top Things to Do in February

February rewards visitors who embrace the cold with unforgettable experiences:

  • Watch the Chinese New Year parade: Position yourself along Shaftesbury Avenue or in Trafalgar Square by noon for the best views. The energy and colour are extraordinary.
  • Explore Chinatown: Visit the restaurants and bakeries of Gerrard Street and Lisle Street. Try dim sum at Dumplings' Legend or roast duck at Four Seasons.
  • Visit the Tate Modern for free: One of the world's great modern art museums, the Turbine Hall installations are always worth a visit. The viewing platform on Level 10 of the Switch House offers free panoramic views.
  • Take a romantic Thames cruise: City Cruises and Thames Clippers run year-round. An evening cruise past the illuminated Houses of Parliament and Tower Bridge is magical.
  • Discover Dennis Severs' House: This extraordinary Huguenot townhouse in Spitalfields is staged as if the inhabitants just left the room. Candlelit evening sessions (Monday evenings) are atmospheric — book ahead.
  • Browse the bookshops of Cecil Court: This pedestrian alley between Charing Cross Road and St Martin's Lane is lined with antiquarian bookshops — a perfect February afternoon activity.
  • Warm up at Wilton's Music Hall: The world's oldest surviving music hall in Wapping hosts an eclectic programme of theatre, music, and cabaret. The building itself is magnificent.
  • Walk Hampstead Heath to Kenwood House: On a crisp February morning, the views from Parliament Hill are spectacular. Warm up with a hot chocolate at the Kenwood House cafe, then explore the free art collection inside.
  • Catch a gig at the Jazz Cafe or Ronnie Scott's: February evenings are made for live music. Ronnie Scott's in Soho is legendary; the Jazz Cafe in Camden is more relaxed and great value.
  • Visit the Sir John Soane's Museum: This eccentric, treasure-packed house museum in Lincoln's Inn Fields is free and rarely crowded. The Saturday candlelit openings are unforgettable.

🍽️ Where to Eat & Drink

February dining in London is all about warmth, comfort, and — if Valentine's is on the agenda — romance:

  • Brasserie Zedel, Piccadilly: A stunning Art Deco basement brasserie serving classic French food at astonishingly fair prices. The prix fixe menu is one of central London's best-value meals. Perfect for a romantic evening.
  • The Dove, Hammersmith: A riverside pub with a roaring fire, excellent Fuller's ales, and a terrace overlooking the Thames. Cosy and quintessentially English.
  • Hoppers, Soho: Sri Lankan hoppers (crispy bowl-shaped pancakes) with egg, dhal, and sambols — ideal comfort food on a cold February day. Arrives steaming hot. No reservations; queue from 5pm.
  • Andrew Edmunds, Soho: A candlelit, wood-panelled restaurant that feels timeless. The handwritten seasonal menu changes daily. Exceptional wine list at fair mark-ups. Arguably London's most romantic affordable restaurant.
  • Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese, Fleet Street: A historic pub rebuilt after the Great Fire of 1666. Dark, atmospheric cellars and a warming pint of real ale — Dickens drank here.
  • My Neighbours the Dumplings, Clapton: Handmade dumplings with fillings like Sichuan chilli and black vinegar pork. Tiny, buzzing, and deeply satisfying on a cold night.

💡 Insider Tips

  • Hotel prices remain low: February is still off-peak for tourism, so hotel rates stay competitive. Book Valentine's weekend early though — those dates do sell out at romantic spots.
  • Chinese New Year is packed: If you want to see the parade, arrive at Trafalgar Square by 11am. If crowds aren't your thing, visit Chinatown on a different day that week for decorations and special menus without the crush.
  • Half-term means busy attractions: UK school half-term falls in mid-February. Museums and family attractions get significantly busier. Visit early in the morning or go in the first or last week of the month to avoid it.
  • Dress for wind, not just cold: February wind chill can make 7°C feel like 2°C. A windproof jacket and a decent scarf make all the difference when crossing Westminster Bridge.
  • Restaurant Week deals: Many restaurants participate in special dining promotions in late January and February, offering set menus at reduced prices. Check SquareMeal and Time Out for current offers.

🎒 What to Pack

  • Warm, windproof coat
  • Scarf, gloves, and hat — wind chill is real
  • Layers for variable indoor and outdoor temperatures
  • Waterproof shoes or boots
  • Compact umbrella
  • Smart outfit if dining out for Valentine's Day
  • Portable charger for photo-heavy days at Chinese New Year
