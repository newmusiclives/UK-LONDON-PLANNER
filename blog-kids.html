London with Kids: A Family Survival Guide

Everything you need to know to keep the whole family happy, fed, and entertained in London

London with kids is brilliant, chaotic, exhausting, and deeply rewarding — often all in the same afternoon. The city has an extraordinary number of free museums, vast parks for running off energy, and enough interactive attractions to keep children of any age engaged. But it also has packed Tube trains, expensive restaurants, and distances that can defeat small legs. This guide is designed to help families plan a London trip that works for everyone, from toddlers to teenagers, with practical advice on getting around, eating out, saving money, and choosing the right attractions for your children's ages.

Best Attractions for Toddlers (Under 5)

Toddlers need space, stimulation, and regular snack breaks. London delivers on all three.

  • Diana Memorial Playground, Kensington Gardens — The best playground in London, themed around Peter Pan with a huge wooden pirate ship, teepees, and sensory trails. Free entry. Enclosed and safe for toddlers. It can get busy on weekends, so visit on a weekday morning if possible.
  • Natural History Museum — Free entry and toddlers love the dinosaur gallery. The blue whale model in Hintze Hall creates genuine wonder. The museum also has a dedicated Investigate Centre for young children. Avoid weekends and school holidays when it is packed.
  • London Zoo — The penguin beach, butterfly house, and children's zoo with farmyard animals are perfect for little ones. Tickets are around 27 pounds for adults; children under 3 go free. Book online for a discount.
  • Coram's Fields — A unique seven-acre park in Bloomsbury where adults are only admitted if accompanied by a child. There is a sandpit, paddling pool in summer, a small city farm, and plenty of space. Completely free.
  • Thames Clipper Boat Ride — Toddlers love boats. The Thames Clipper river bus from Greenwich to Westminster passes under Tower Bridge and past the major landmarks. Use an Oyster card for cheaper fares. Children under 5 travel free.
  • Mudchute Park and Farm — A free city farm on the Isle of Dogs with llamas, donkeys, pigs, and chickens. Far less crowded than the main tourist spots and a great break from sightseeing.

Best Attractions for Children (Ages 5-11)

This age group wants interactivity, excitement, and a bit of independence. London has countless options.

  • Science Museum — Free entry and packed with interactive exhibits. The Wonderlab gallery (ticketed at around 10 pounds) has hands-on experiments that children adore. The space gallery, the Stephenson's Rocket, and the flight simulator are all hits with this age group.
  • Tower of London — The Crown Jewels, the armour collections, the ravens, and stories of beheadings and prisoners keep school-age children fascinated. The Yeoman Warder tours are free with entry and brilliantly entertaining. Tickets are around 33 pounds for adults, 16 pounds for children.
  • Warner Bros. Studio Tour: The Making of Harry Potter — Located in Watford, about an hour from central London. An absolute must for any Harry Potter fan. Walk through the Great Hall, fly on broomsticks, and drink Butterbeer. Tickets must be booked well in advance (often weeks or months) and cost around 53 pounds for adults, 43 pounds for children.
  • HMS Belfast — A real World War II warship moored on the Thames. Children can explore the gun turrets, engine room, and crew quarters. Tickets are around 26 pounds for adults, 13 pounds for children.
  • London Transport Museum — In Covent Garden, with old buses and Tube trains that children can climb into and pretend to drive. The All Aboard play zone is designed for under-7s. Around 21 pounds for adults; children under 18 go free.
  • Changing of the Guard — Free and thrilling for children who are interested in soldiers, horses, and pageantry. Arrive early for a good viewing spot at Buckingham Palace. Check the schedule as it does not happen every day.
  • Sea Life London Aquarium — On the South Bank near the London Eye. Sharks, rays, penguins, and a glass-floor shark walk. Around 28 pounds online; save by booking ahead.

Best Attractions for Teenagers

Teenagers want cool, Instagram-worthy, and slightly independent experiences. London excels at this.

  • Camden Market — Vintage clothing, street food from around the world, and a buzzing atmosphere that teenagers love. Free to explore. The food stalls offer meals for 6 to 10 pounds, and the vintage shops are affordable.
  • Tate Modern — Free entry and the kind of contemporary art that can genuinely impress teenagers. The Turbine Hall installations are massive and photogenic. The viewing platform has free panoramic views.
  • Shoreditch Street Art Walk — Self-guided walk around some of the world's best street art. Free. Brick Lane and the surrounding streets are covered in murals by internationally known artists. Combine with the Brick Lane vintage market on Sundays.
  • The London Dungeon — Live actors, special effects, and gruesome stories from London's dark history. Teenagers who enjoy being scared will have a great time. Tickets around 28 pounds online.
  • Platform 9 and Three Quarters, King's Cross — A free photo opportunity with the half-disappeared luggage trolley and a Harry Potter shop. Quick, free, and all teenagers know the reference.
  • West End Shows — Many shows appeal to teenagers: Hamilton, Wicked, The Phantom of the Opera, and Les Miserables are perennial hits. TKTS in Leicester Square has same-day discounted tickets. Budget 25 to 50 pounds per ticket.
  • Sky Garden — Free panoramic views from the top of the Walkie Talkie building. Book online (free) in advance. Teenagers appreciate the views and the Instagram potential.

Family-Friendly Restaurants

Eating out with children in London does not have to mean chain restaurants. These places genuinely welcome families and serve good food.

  • Dishoom — Indian restaurant with locations across London. The menu is varied enough for fussy eaters, the atmosphere is lively, and children are warmly welcomed. The chicken tikka and naan bread are universally popular with kids. Around 15 to 20 pounds per person.
  • Flat Iron — Simple menu focused on steak, served with a free side salad and a creamy dessert. Children who eat meat tend to love it. Around 14 pounds for a steak. Multiple locations.
  • Pizza Pilgrims — Neapolitan pizza that adults and children both enjoy. Locations across London. A margherita costs around 9 pounds. Some branches have a make-your-own-pizza experience for children.
  • Giraffe — A family-oriented chain with a varied global menu, colouring sheets, and high chairs. Locations in South Bank, Southgate, and others. Children's meals around 7 pounds.
  • Wahaca — Mexican street food that works for families. Tacos, quesadillas, and burritos that children can assemble. Bright, casual atmosphere. Around 12 to 15 pounds per person.
  • Borough Market — Not a restaurant, but the market stalls let each family member choose something different. Grab a bench and eat together. Street food costs 6 to 10 pounds per portion.
  • The Regency Cafe — A classic greasy spoon near Westminster. Children love the shouted orders, the chips, and the no-nonsense atmosphere. A full meal costs under 8 pounds per person.

Parks and Playgrounds

Children need to burn off energy. London's parks are the answer, and most have excellent playgrounds.

  • Diana Memorial Playground, Kensington Gardens — The best in London. A massive pirate ship, teepees, and sensory trails. Free. Suitable for all ages up to 12.
  • Hyde Park — Paddle boats on the Serpentine in summer, the Princess Diana Memorial Fountain (essentially a circular water feature children can paddle in), and wide open spaces for running.
  • Regent's Park — Excellent playgrounds near the Broad Walk, plus a boating lake and proximity to London Zoo.
  • Greenwich Park — A playground with views over the city, plus the Cutty Sark and National Maritime Museum nearby.
  • Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park — Purpose-built playgrounds from the 2012 Olympics, a splash park in summer, and the ArcelorMittal Orbit slide (the world's tallest and longest tunnel slide at 178 metres). Slide tickets around 17 pounds.
  • Hampstead Heath — Wild, hilly, and adventurous. Older children love exploring the ponds and woodlands. The playground at Parliament Hill is good for younger children.

Getting Around London with Children

The Tube with a Pushchair

Navigating the Tube with a pushchair is possible but requires planning. Many stations have no step-free access, meaning you will need to carry the pushchair up and down stairs. Here are practical tips:

  • Check the TfL step-free guide before you travel. Stations with step-free access include King's Cross, Westminster, Stratford, Green Park, and Canary Wharf. Many older stations like Covent Garden and Angel are step-free only in one direction.
  • Use the bus instead. All London buses are step-free with a designated pushchair and wheelchair space. Buses are also cheaper than the Tube and children see more of the city.
  • Travel off-peak. Avoid the 7:30 to 9:30 AM and 5 to 7 PM rush hours. Trains are packed and stressful with children.
  • Consider a lightweight, foldable pushchair rather than a large travel system. You will need to fold it on escalators and in tight spaces.
  • Children under 11 travel free on the Tube, buses, DLR, and London Overground when accompanied by an adult with a valid ticket.

Alternative Transport

  • Thames Clipper — River bus from Greenwich to Westminster. Children under 5 travel free, ages 5 to 15 get a child fare. Scenic and fun.
  • Black cabs — All London black cabs can fit a pushchair in the luggage area. Useful for short hops when everyone is tired. Expect to pay 8 to 15 pounds for a short central London journey.
  • Walking — Central London is compact. Walking from the South Bank to Covent Garden takes about 20 minutes. The South Bank path is flat and pushchair-friendly.

Rainy Day Activities

It will rain at some point during your London trip. Here is where to go when it does:

  • Natural History Museum — Free entry, dinosaurs, and the blue whale. Enough to fill a rainy morning.
  • Science Museum — Free entry with paid interactive Wonderlab gallery. Easily three to four hours of entertainment.
  • KidZania, Westfield — An indoor city where children aged 4 to 14 role-play adult jobs (pilot, firefighter, doctor). Around 25 to 35 pounds per child for four-plus hours of entertainment.
  • London Transport Museum — In Covent Garden. Old buses and trains to climb on, plus the All Aboard play zone. Free for under-18s.
  • Hamleys — Seven floors of toys on Regent Street. Free to browse (though leaving without buying something is a challenge). Toy demonstrations on every floor.
  • Discover Children's Story Centre — In Stratford, with immersive storytelling experiences for under-8s. Around 8 pounds per person.
  • Cinema — Many London cinemas offer kids' morning screenings at reduced prices on weekends and school holidays. Check Odeon Kids and Picturehouse Little Bugs Club for deals.

Money-Saving Family Passes and Tips

  • Merlin Annual Pass — If you plan to visit the London Eye, Madame Tussauds, Sea Life Aquarium, and the London Dungeon, a Merlin pass can save significant money versus individual tickets. Check the website for current family deals.
  • London Pass — Includes entry to over 80 attractions. Worth it only if you plan to visit multiple paid attractions in a short time. Do the maths for your specific plans before buying.
  • 2-for-1 offers with National Rail — If you travel into London by train, the Days Out Guide offers 2-for-1 entry to many attractions including the Tower of London, London Zoo, and the London Eye. You just need a valid National Rail ticket.
  • Free museums — The British Museum, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, V&A, Tate Modern, Tate Britain, National Gallery, and many more are completely free. You could fill an entire family holiday without paying an entry fee.
  • Supermarket lunches — A Tesco or Sainsbury's meal deal (sandwich, drink, snack for around 3 to 5 pounds) feeds a child for a fraction of restaurant prices. Eat in a park for a free picnic experience.
  • Children under 11 travel free on all TfL services (Tube, bus, DLR, Overground) when with an adult using a valid Oyster or contactless card.
  • Book everything online — Almost every paid attraction offers a discount of 10 to 20 percent for advance online booking versus buying at the door.

Sample Family Day Itinerary

Here is a tried-and-tested family day that balances sightseeing, fun, and sanity:

  • 9:30 AM — Natural History Museum (free, arrives before the crowds).
  • 11:30 AM — Walk through Hyde Park, stop at the Diana Memorial Playground.
  • 1:00 PM — Lunch at Pizza Pilgrims or a park picnic from a supermarket.
  • 2:30 PM — Bus to the South Bank. Walk along the river, stop for ice cream.
  • 3:30 PM — Tate Modern (free, the Turbine Hall is enough for a quick visit).
  • 4:30 PM — Walk across the Millennium Bridge to St Paul's Cathedral (exterior is free and impressive).
  • 5:30 PM — Early dinner at Flat Iron or Wahaca.
  • Cost for a family of four: Around 40 to 60 pounds total (lunch + dinner + transport), with all the main attractions free.
