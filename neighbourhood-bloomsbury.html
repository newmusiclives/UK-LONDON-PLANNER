Bloomsbury Neighbourhood Guide

British Museum, British Library, and the garden squares of the Bloomsbury Group — London's intellectual heartland

Overview

Bloomsbury sits between Oxford Street and King's Cross — a grid of Georgian garden squares and streets developed by the Bedford Estate in the 18th and 19th centuries. The area is named after the Bloomsbury Group (Virginia Woolf, Keynes, the Stracheys) who lived here in the early 20th century.

Its three crown jewels: the British Museum (the world's oldest public museum), the British Library (the UK's national library), and UCL (one of Britain's top research universities). In between: garden squares, literary pubs, and a village feel.

The vibe: Intellectual, unhurried, green. Academics and tourists weave through the same streets.

Best for: Readers, history buffs, architecture fans, anyone who wants a slower, more thoughtful London experience.

Top Attractions

  • 1. British Museum

    Great Russell St, WC1B 3DG

    One of the world's greatest museums — 8 million works spanning human history. Free entry. The Rosetta Stone and Egyptian mummies are the star attractions.

  • 2. British Library Treasures Gallery

    96 Euston Rd, NW1 2DB

    Free exhibition — the Magna Carta, Beatles lyric sheets, Shakespeare First Folios, Jane Austen's writing desk. Extraordinary.

  • 3. Charles Dickens Museum

    48 Doughty St, WC1N 2LX

    The only Dickens home open to the public — where he wrote "Oliver Twist" and "Nicholas Nickleby" 1837-1839.

  • 4. Foundling Museum

    40 Brunswick Sq, WC1N 1AZ

    The story of London's first children's home — art donated by Hogarth and Handel, tokens left by destitute mothers in the 1700s.

  • 5. Wellcome Collection

    183 Euston Rd, NW1 2BE

    Free museum of "medicine, life and art" — Napoleon's toothbrush, Darwin's walking stick, Freud's couch, contemporary exhibitions.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Noble Rot Bloomsbury

    51 Lamb's Conduit St, WC1N 3NB · European · $$$

    Smart wine-focused restaurant — a wine list that reads like a collection. Excellent modern British cooking.

  • 2. Ciao Bella

    86-90 Lamb's Conduit St, WC1N 3LZ · Italian · $$

    Candlelit cult trattoria since 1982 — piano nightly, classic Italian menu, tables close together. Big tables for groups.

  • 3. Otto's French Restaurant

    182 Gray's Inn Rd, WC1X 8EW · French · $$$

    The only London restaurant still serving canard à la presse (pressed duck) tableside. Theatrical. Order 24h ahead.

  • 4. Hakkasan Hanway Place

    8 Hanway Pl, W1T 1HD · Chinese · $$$$

    The original 2001 Hakkasan — Michelin-starred Cantonese in a moody-lit basement. Dim sum lunch more affordable.

  • 5. Bao Fitzrovia

    31 Windmill St, W1T 2JN · Taiwanese · $$

    Taiwanese gua bao in a stylish corner — bookable, with the full menu and downstairs karaoke rooms.

Best Bars & Pubs

  • 1. The Lamb

    94 Lamb's Conduit St, WC1N 3LZ

    Victorian stained-glass pub with etched-glass snob-screens on the bar. Dickens mentioned it in "Barnaby Rudge".

  • 2. The Museum Tavern

    49 Great Russell St, WC1B 3BA

    Directly opposite the British Museum — Karl Marx drank here while writing Das Kapital across the road.

  • 3. The Princess Louise

    208 High Holborn, WC1V 7EP

    Astonishing 1872 Victorian gin palace — etched mirrors, ornate tilework, horseshoe bar. Cheapest pub in central London (Sam Smith's prices).

  • 4. The Seven Stars

    53 Carey St, WC2A 2JB

    One of the oldest pubs in London (1602), survived the Great Fire. Tiny, opinionated landlady, superb cask ales.

  • 5. The Perseverance

    63 Lamb's Conduit St, WC1N 3NB

    Gastropub on Bloomsbury's best high street. Outstanding upstairs dining room; excellent Sunday roast.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Store Street Espresso

    40 Store St, WC1E 7DB

    Bloomsbury student-favourite specialty coffee. Long wood benches, calm room, great for laptop work.

  • 2. The Cake Shop at London Review Bookshop

    14-16 Bury Pl, WC1A 2JL

    Tiny Bloomsbury café next to the London Review Bookshop. Some of London's best cakes (try the lamingtons).

  • 3. Fortitude Bakehouse

    35 Colonnade, WC1N 1JA

    Sourdough, flaky pastries, filter coffee, quiet back-room — a Bloomsbury hidden gem.

Shopping Highlights

  • Lamb's Conduit Street — independent-shop paradise: Persephone Books, The Lamb pub, Ciao Bella, and several bespoke menswear specialists.
  • London Review Bookshop (14-16 Bury Pl) — the bookshop of the London Review of Books, razor-sharp curation.
  • Persephone Books (59 Lamb's Conduit St) — publisher and shop selling rediscovered 20th-century women writers in signature dove-grey covers.
  • Gay's the Word (66 Marchmont St) — the UK's oldest LGBTQ+ bookshop (1979).
  • The British Museum Shop — broad, beautifully curated. Egyptian reproductions are classic gifts.
  • Brunswick Centre — a Brutalist shopping-and-housing complex with a Curzon Bloomsbury cinema and Waitrose.

Getting There

Main stations serving this neighbourhood:

  • Russell Square (Piccadilly) — at the heart of Bloomsbury; 3-min walk to British Museum
  • Holborn (Central, Piccadilly) — south side — Lincoln's Inn Fields nearby
  • Tottenham Court Road (Central, Northern, Elizabeth) — west side — British Museum nearby
  • Euston (Northern, Victoria) — north side; also mainline station

Insider Tips

  • The British Museum is free and usually quietest on weekday mornings opening (10am) — focus on Egypt, the Rosetta Stone, and the Enlightenment Gallery.
  • The British Library Treasures Gallery (free) rotates Beatles manuscripts, Beethoven sketches, and Gutenberg Bibles — 30 minutes well spent.
  • Garden squares — Russell, Bedford, Bloomsbury, Gordon, Tavistock — are free to walk through and were the setting for the Bloomsbury Group.
  • UCL's "Auto-Icon" (the preserved body of philosopher Jeremy Bentham, in the Student Centre cabinet) is free to visit and genuinely unusual.
  • Skip the Covent Garden tourist restaurants — walk 10 minutes north to Lamb's Conduit Street for a calmer, better meal.
