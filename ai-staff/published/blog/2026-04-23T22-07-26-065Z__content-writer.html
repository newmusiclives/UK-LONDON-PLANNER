Seven Spring Walks in London That Locals Actually Do

Published 23 April 2026 · London Planned

There's a roughly three-week window every spring when London becomes unreasonably beautiful. The wisteria drapes itself over terraced houses in Kensington. The cherry blossoms in Greenwich Park go off like pink fireworks. And the canal towpaths — muddy all winter — finally dry out enough that you won't lose a trainer.

Late April into mid-May is that window. If you're planning a trip during it, walking should be your primary mode of transport. Not because the Tube is bad (it's fine), but because this is the one season where London genuinely rewards you for being outside.

Here are seven routes I'd actually send a friend on — with distances, start/end points, and where to stop for a pint or a coffee.

1. Regent's Canal: Little Venice to Camden Lock

Distance: 2.7 km / roughly 35 minutes
Start: Warwick Avenue station
End: Camden Town station

This is probably London's single best casual walk, and it's not close. You start among the colourful narrowboats moored at Little Venice — which is less "Venice" and more "charming puddle," but it works. The towpath takes you past Regent's Park's northern edge, through the back of London Zoo (you can hear the gibbons, which is honestly enough zoo for most people), and deposits you into the controlled chaos of Camden Lock Market.

The path is flat, paved, and almost impossible to get lost on. In spring, the canal-side trees leaf out in that electric green that only lasts a fortnight.

Coffee stop: Café Laville, right at the Little Venice junction — it's built on a bridge over the canal. Coffees around £3.50–£4.20.

Pub finish: The Edinboro Castle on Mornington Terrace has a proper beer garden. Get there before 5 pm on a sunny day or you're standing.

2. Hampstead Heath: Parliament Hill Loop

Distance: 4 km loop / about 50 minutes
Start & end: Gospel Oak station or Hampstead Heath Overground

Parliament Hill gives you one of the best views of the London skyline — the Shard, the Gherkin, the Walkie Talkie, St Paul's dome, all of it spread out below you like an architect's model. On a clear spring morning the visibility is absurd.

From the top, loop north toward Kenwood House (free entry, a Vermeer on the wall, proper cream teas at the Brew House café for about £8). The surrounding meadows are full of buttercups and people who look suspiciously happy for Londoners.

If you're feeling ambitious, detach from the loop and walk down into Highgate Village for a pint at The Flask. If you're feeling less ambitious, the mixed bathing pond opens in spring and a cold-water dip with the resident coots is an experience you won't forget — voluntarily or otherwise.

3. Richmond: The Thames to Richmond Park via Terrace Gardens

Distance: 5 km one-way / about 75 minutes
Start: Richmond station
End: Richmond Park (Robin Hood Gate or wherever a deer makes you stop)

Richmond is technically London. I know. The Zone 4 designation and the presence of actual deer make it feel like the Cotswolds, but your Oyster card works here.

Walk along the Thames past the famous Richmond Bridge, then cut up through Terrace Gardens — a steep, tiered garden with views over the river bend that Turner painted multiple times. Continue along the Petersham Road past Petersham Nurseries (the café does a £16 lunch that justifies itself) and enter Richmond Park through Petersham Gate.

The park is 2,500 acres. You will see red and fallow deer. They've been here since Charles I enclosed the land in 1637. They do not care about you. In late April, the Isabella Plantation inside the park hits peak azalea season — banks of orange, pink, and red reflected in shallow ponds. It's free and it's genuinely one of the most photogenic spots in greater London.

4. South Bank: Tate Modern to Tower Bridge

Distance: 3.2 km / about 40 minutes
Start: Tate Modern (Southwark station)
End: Tower Bridge

Yes, this is a tourist route. I'm including it because it's a good tourist route, especially in spring when the light comes in low and golden off the Thames around 6 pm.

Pass the Millennium Bridge (it no longer wobbles, they fixed that), Shakespeare's Globe, the Golden Hinde replica, Borough Market (grab something to eat — the Kappacasein cheese toastie at about £7 is justifiably famous), Southwark Cathedral, HMS Belfast, and finally Tower Bridge.

Every single thing I just named is either free or cheap. The walk itself costs nothing. On a warm spring evening, this stretch of the river is the best free show in London.

Pub detour: The Anchor, Bankside. Yes, it's often packed. The upstairs terrace is less so, and the river views justify the slight premium on a pint (about £6.50–£7).

5. Primrose Hill via Regent's Park

Distance: 3.5 km / about 45 minutes
Start: Baker Street station
End: Chalk Farm station

Cross through Regent's Park — the Avenue Gardens near the southern entrance are immaculate in spring — then head north past the boating lake to Primrose Hill. The hill itself is a steep-ish grass mound that gives you a 180-degree panorama of central London.

It's where every Londoner goes when the first warm Saturday arrives. Bring a picnic. Don't bring a Bluetooth speaker (seriously, don't). Afterwards, walk down into Primrose Hill village for coffee at Greenberry Café or a proper meal at The Engineer on Gloucester Avenue.

6. Hackney: Victoria Park and the Hertford Union Canal

Distance: 4 km loop / about 55 minutes
Start & end: Mile End station

Victoria Park — "the People's Park" — is East London's answer to Hyde Park, minus the tourist coaches. The spring flower beds around the lake are genuinely impressive, and the Pavilion Café (lakeside, flat whites around £3.80) is one of the best park cafés in the city.

Loop back along the Hertford Union Canal, a narrow cut that connects to the Regent's Canal. It's quieter, a bit scruffy, lined with houseboats. Very East London. You'll pass under a few low bridges that smell faintly of damp brick and history.

7. Greenwich: The Royal Observatory Loop

Distance: 3 km / about 40 minutes
Start: Cutty Sark DLR
End: Greenwich Park summit

Walk through the grounds of the Old Royal Naval College (free, Baroque, ridiculously grand for something you just wander into), past the National Maritime Museum (also free), and up the steep hill in Greenwich Park to the Royal Observatory.

You can stand on the Prime Meridian line for free outside the Observatory gates — you don't need to pay the £18 entry unless you want to see the Flamsteed telescopes (which are excellent, for the record). The view from the top across the Thames to Canary Wharf and the Isle of Dogs is particularly striking in spring afternoon light.

The cherry blossom avenue on the eastern side of the park typically peaks in the third week of April. Timing it right is mostly luck, but if you're reading this in late April, go now.
