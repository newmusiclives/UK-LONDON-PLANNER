Zone-by-Zone Breakdown

Zone 1 — Central London

The heart of the city. You are walking distance from most major attractions, world-class dining, and iconic landmarks. The trade-off is price and noise — streets are busy late into the evening, and you will pay a premium for everything from hotels to coffee.

  • Walk to major sights
  • Lowest transport costs
  • Maximum dining options
  • Vibrant evening atmosphere
  • Most expensive accommodation
  • Noisy, especially weekends
  • Smaller rooms for the price
  • Tourist-heavy areas can feel crowded

Best Neighbourhoods

Covent Garden South Bank Soho Westminster Mayfair
Zone 2 — Inner London

The sweet spot for most visitors. Zone 2 neighbourhoods have genuine local character — independent cafes, markets, and parks — while still being 10–20 minutes from the centre by Tube. This is where Londoners actually live, eat, and socialise.

  • 25–35% cheaper than Zone 1
  • Authentic neighbourhood feel
  • Great food and bar scenes
  • Quick Tube access to centre
  • Slightly higher transport spend
  • Some areas have limited late-night options
  • Quality varies by neighbourhood
  • May need bus + Tube combos

Best Neighbourhoods

Shoreditch Camden Brixton Greenwich Notting Hill Bermondsey
Zone 3 — Outer Inner London

Significant savings with a longer commute. Zone 3 includes some genuinely charming areas — think leafy suburbs, family-friendly parks, and high streets with character. Best for longer stays, families, or budget-conscious travellers who do not mind 25–40 minutes on the Tube.

  • 40–55% cheaper than Zone 1
  • Quieter, more spacious
  • Larger rooms and apartments
  • Family-friendly parks
  • 25–40 min commute to attractions
  • Higher daily transport cap
  • Fewer late-night options
  • Can feel disconnected from city buzz

Best Neighbourhoods

Our Recommendation

First-time visitors (1–3 nights): Stay in Zone 1. The convenience of walking to everything is worth the premium for a short trip. You will save on transport and maximise sightseeing time.

Repeat visitors or 4–7 nights: Stay in Zone 2. You get the best balance of price, local character, and accessibility. Areas like Shoreditch, Camden, and Notting Hill are destinations in their own right.

Families or 7+ nights: Consider Zone 2 or Zone 3. Larger apartments at lower prices mean more comfort and savings, especially for groups. The transport cost difference is modest when you factor in the accommodation savings.

Hidden Gems by Zone

Zone 1 Secrets

Beyond the obvious landmarks, Zone 1 hides quiet corners that tourists miss. Postman's Park near St Paul's has a memorial wall of hand-painted tiles. The Sir John Soane's Museum in Lincoln's Inn Fields is a jaw-dropping house museum (free, small, and unforgettable). The Barbican Conservatory is a tropical greenhouse hidden inside London's brutalist arts centre.

Zone 2 Secrets

Columbia Road Flower Market on Sunday mornings is one of London's most magical experiences. God's Own Junkyard in Walthamstow is a neon wonderland. The Geffrye Museum (now the Museum of the Home) in Hoxton traces domestic life through the centuries. Peckham Levels is a multi-storey car park converted into studios, bars, and street food.

Zone 3 Secrets

Hampstead Heath's swimming ponds let you swim outdoors in natural water, summer or winter. Richmond Park is home to 630 free-roaming red and fallow deer. The Horniman Museum in Forest Hill has an eccentric collection including an overstuffed walrus. Eltham Palace combines Art Deco glamour with a medieval great hall.
