London in March

Spring arrives with daffodils in the parks, the Boat Race on the Thames, and that first warm afternoon that makes the whole city smile.

🌡️ Weather Overview

March marks the transition from winter to spring. The weather is changeable but trending warmer:

  • Average temperature: 4°C to 11°C (39°F to 52°F), though warm spells can push past 15°C
  • Rainfall: Around 37mm across 11 rainy days — one of the driest months
  • Daylight hours: 11 to 12.5 hours — a dramatic improvement. Clocks spring forward on the last Sunday of March
  • Sunshine: Increasing noticeably, with an average of 4 hours of sun per day

The clocks changing at the end of March is a turning point — suddenly evenings feel long and bright. Early March can still feel wintry, but by late March, London's parks are carpeted with daffodils, crocuses, and early magnolias.

🎭 What's On in March

March brings a mix of spring celebrations and sporting tradition:

  • St Patrick's Day Parade & Festival (17 Mar or nearest Sunday): A colourful parade winds through central London from Piccadilly to Trafalgar Square, followed by a festival with live music, food stalls, and Irish dancing. Free to attend.
  • The Boat Race (late Mar or early Apr): Oxford vs Cambridge on the Thames from Putney to Mortlake. Hundreds of thousands line the riverbanks. Best viewing from Putney Bridge, Duke's Meadows, or the pubs of Hammersmith.
  • Kew Gardens Spring Blooms (all month): The Rock Garden and Woodland areas at Kew burst into colour with crocuses, magnolias, and early camellias. The glasshouses are also in peak form.
  • Mother's Day / Mothering Sunday (mid-Mar, date varies): Restaurants across London offer special Mother's Day menus and afternoon teas. Book well in advance — it's one of the busiest dining days of the year.
  • Six Nations Rugby Finals (mid-Mar): The final rounds of the Six Nations bring electric atmosphere to Twickenham and to pubs across the city.
  • Flare Film Festival at BFI Southbank (mid-Mar): The BFI's LGBTQ+ film festival screens powerful and thought-provoking cinema from around the world.
  • Head of the River Race (late Mar): Over 400 crews race the Boat Race course in reverse — an incredible sight from Chiswick Bridge or the Thames Path.

✨ Top Things to Do in March

March is the sweet spot — spring energy without summer crowds:

  • Walk through St James's Park: The daffodil displays around the lake are stunning. Watch the pelicans being fed daily at 2:30pm — a tradition since 1664. Buckingham Palace provides a magnificent backdrop.
  • Explore Greenwich: Walk through Greenwich Park to the Royal Observatory for free views across the city. Visit the Cutty Sark and the National Maritime Museum. Arrive via Thames Clipper for the best approach.
  • Browse Columbia Road Flower Market: Every Sunday, this Bethnal Green street transforms into a riot of colour and fragrance. Go early (8am) to avoid the worst crowds, and explore the independent shops that open along the street.
  • Visit the Tower of London: March queues are a fraction of summer's. Allow 3 hours to see the Crown Jewels, the White Tower, and the Yeoman Warder (Beefeater) tours — included in your ticket.
  • Walk the Regent's Canal: The towpath from Little Venice to Camden Lock is one of London's finest urban walks — around 3 miles, flat, and lined with houseboats, cafes, and street art.
  • See cherry blossoms at Greenwich Park: Early cherry varieties start blooming in late March. The avenue near the Ranger's House is particularly photogenic.
  • Catch a Six Nations match: Even without tickets for Twickenham, watching England play in a packed London pub is a memorable experience. Try The Admiralty in Trafalgar Square or Flat Iron Square near Borough.
  • Tour the Houses of Parliament: Guided tours of the Palace of Westminster run on Saturdays and during recess. Seeing the Lords and Commons chambers up close is genuinely impressive.
  • Visit the Horniman Museum: This underrated gem in Forest Hill has a fantastic natural history collection, an aquarium, and beautiful gardens with views across the city. Free entry.
  • Explore Hampstead Village: Wander the Georgian streets, browse Daunt Books, have a pint at The Holly Bush, and walk across the Heath to Kenwood House. A perfect March day out.

🍽️ Where to Eat & Drink

March menus start to lighten up as the first spring ingredients appear:

  • The Anchor & Hope, Waterloo: A legendary gastropub with a no-bookings policy (except Sunday lunch). The seasonal British menu might feature spring lamb, wild garlic, or jersey royals as March progresses.
  • The Marksman, Hackney: Michelin Bib Gourmand pub serving inventive British food. The beef and barley bun is a signature, and the Sunday roast is superb. Book ahead for weekends.
  • Rochelle Canteen, Shoreditch: Hidden inside a former school bike shed, this calm, elegant canteen serves simple British food with exceptional ingredients. Look for spring greens and new-season produce.
  • The Spaniards Inn, Hampstead: A 16th-century pub on the edge of Hampstead Heath, perfect for a post-walk pint. The garden opens up in March when the weather allows. Keats and Dickens were regulars.
  • Kiln, Soho: Thai grill cooking over charcoal and wood. Intense, smoky flavours — the clay-pot baked glass noodles and massaman curry are standouts. No reservations at dinner; queue from 5:30pm.
  • The River Cafe, Hammersmith: Ruth Rogers' Italian masterpiece celebrates seasonal ingredients. March brings early asparagus, purple sprouting broccoli, and wild garlic. A splurge, but unforgettable.

💡 Insider Tips

  • Watch for the clock change: On the last Sunday of March, clocks spring forward one hour (GMT to BST). Don't miss your morning plans — and enjoy the suddenly longer evenings.
  • Easter can fall in March: If Easter is early, expect holiday crowds and higher prices from Good Friday onward. Museums and attractions run special programmes but get significantly busier.
  • Book the Boat Race early: Pubs along the route — especially in Putney, Hammersmith, and Barnes — fill up hours before the race. Some require pre-booked tickets. Plan ahead or watch from the free towpath.
  • Layer for unpredictable weather: March can deliver sunshine, sleet, and wind in a single afternoon. A packable waterproof layer you can stuff in a bag is essential.
  • Cheap flights abound: March is still shoulder season for flights, so fares from most origins remain competitive. Fly mid-week for the best deals.

🎒 What to Pack

  • Waterproof jacket — showers come and go quickly
  • Layers: t-shirt, jumper, light jacket for variable temperatures
  • Comfortable walking shoes (waterproof preferred)
  • Sunglasses — March sunshine can be surprisingly bright
  • Light scarf for cooler mornings and evenings
  • Compact umbrella
  • Camera — spring blooms and golden Thames light are photogenic
