8 London Spring Walks That Are Actually Worth Doing

Published 24 April 2026 · ~1,700 words · 8 min read

London in April does this thing where it tricks you into believing the weather has finally turned. You get three consecutive days of 16°C sunshine, you buy a linen shirt, and then it hails sideways on a Thursday. Classic.

But there's a three-week window — roughly late April into mid-May — when the city becomes unreasonably beautiful. The wisteria drapes itself over terraced houses in Kensington. Cherry blossoms in Greenwich Park go off like pink fireworks. And the canal towpaths, muddy all winter, finally dry out enough that you won't lose a trainer.

Here are eight routes I'd actually send a friend on, with real distances and a decent place to eat or drink at the end of each one.

All prices in £. Use the currency switcher in the top right if you'd prefer USD, EUR, AUD, or CAD.

1. Regent's Park to Primrose Hill (3.2 km / ~45 minutes)

Start at the south gate of Regent's Park near Great Portland Street station, walk north through the Avenue Gardens (the Italian-style bit with the urns — properly beautiful in April when the tulips are at peak performance), continue past the boating lake, and exit via the north-west corner onto Primrose Hill.

The climb up Primrose Hill is gentle — maybe 65 metres of elevation — but the reward is one of the best skyline views in London. You can see from the Shard to Canary Wharf, and on a clear day, all the way to the Crystal Palace transmitter in the south. It's the kind of view that makes you understand why people pay £2 million for a flat in NW1.

Finish at: The Engineer, 65 Gloucester Avenue, NW1 8JH. A gastropub with a proper garden. Main courses £16–£22. Book if it's a weekend.

2. The Regent's Canal: Little Venice to King's Cross (3.6 km / ~50 minutes)

Speaking of canals. This towpath walk is London's great cheat code — flat, car-free, and so far below street level that you sometimes forget you're in a city of nine million people. Start at Little Venice (Warwick Avenue tube), walk east past the painted narrowboats and the café barges, through the short tunnel under Edgware Road, past London Zoo's aviary (you'll hear the macaws before you see anything), and into Camden Lock.

Most people stop at Camden. Don't. Keep going east. Past the graffiti murals of Camden Road, past the gasholders at St Pancras, and you'll arrive at Granary Square at King's Cross — one of the best pieces of urban regeneration London has pulled off in the last decade. The fountains are already running in April and kids are already getting soaked.

Finish at: Caravan King's Cross, 1 Granary Square, N1C 4AA. All-day brunch with international flavours. The cornbread with jalapeño butter (£5.50) is unreasonably good.

3. South Bank: Tate Modern to Tower Bridge (3 km / ~40 minutes)

Yes, this is the obvious one. I'm including it anyway because it genuinely works, especially on a weekday afternoon when the pavement performers haven't fully set up camp yet.

Start at Tate Modern (free entry, always). Walk east along the Thames Path past the replica of Shakespeare's Globe Theatre (worth popping in — groundling tickets for standing performances are just £5, and the season starts in April). Continue under Southwark Bridge, past Borough Market — absolutely worth a detour for lunch, though budget £12–£18 for a proper feed — and on to Tower Bridge.

The Glass Floor walkway at Tower Bridge costs £12.30 for adults. It's fine. It's a bridge with a glass floor. If you're afraid of heights it'll be memorable; if you're not, you'll take one photo and move on. I wouldn't queue more than ten minutes for it.

Finish at: The Anchor Bankside, 34 Park Street, SE1 9EF. A 17th-century pub right by the river. Pint of cask ale around £6.50. Sit outside if the weather gods are cooperating.

4. Hampstead Heath Circular (5.5 km / ~90 minutes)

This is the big one. If you only do one walk on this list, and you've got the legs for it, make it Hampstead Heath.

Start at Hampstead tube station, walk up Flask Walk (pretty cobbled lane, good charity shops), enter the heath from the south, and head uphill to Parliament Hill — 98 metres above sea level and arguably the finest free viewpoint in London. On an April morning, with the city laid out below you and the ponds glinting in the middle distance, it borders on cinematic.

From Parliament Hill, loop north through the woods to Kenwood House (free entry, English Heritage). The Vermeer alone is worth the walk. Then circle back south via the mixed bathing pond — die-hard swimmers are already in by April, though the water's still around 12°C, which is character-building.

Finish at: The Holly Bush, 22 Holly Mount, NW3 6SG. A proper Hampstead pub since the 1800s. Low ceilings, real fires (still lit in April — trust me), and a decent wine list. Sunday roast is £18–£22.

5. Richmond: The Thames to Richmond Park via Terrace Gardens (5 km / ~75 minutes)

Richmond is technically London. I know. The Zone 4 designation and the presence of actual deer make it feel like the Cotswolds, but your Oyster card works here.

From Richmond station, walk along the Thames past the famous Richmond Bridge, then cut up through Terrace Gardens — a steep, tiered garden with views over the river bend that Turner painted multiple times. Continue along the Petersham Road past Petersham Nurseries (the café does a £16 lunch that justifies itself) and enter Richmond Park through Petersham Gate.

The park is 2,500 acres. You will see red and fallow deer. They've been here since Charles I enclosed the land in 1637. They do not care about you. In late April, the Isabella Plantation inside the park hits peak azalea season — banks of orange, pink, and red reflected in shallow ponds. It's free, and it's genuinely one of the most photogenic spots in greater London.

6. Hackney: Victoria Park and the Hertford Union Canal (4 km loop / ~55 minutes)

Victoria Park — "the People's Park" — is East London's answer to Hyde Park, minus the tourist coaches. The spring flower beds around the lake are genuinely impressive, and the Pavilion Café (lakeside, flat whites around £3.80) is one of the best park cafés in the city.

Loop back along the Hertford Union Canal, a narrow cut that connects to the Regent's Canal. It's quieter, a bit scruffy, lined with houseboats. Very East London. You'll pass under a few low bridges that smell faintly of damp brick and history.

Start & end: Mile End station.

7. Greenwich: Royal Observatory to the Cutty Sark (2.5 km / ~35 minutes)

Take the DLR to Cutty Sark station, but walk uphill first — through Greenwich Park to the Royal Observatory. Straddle the Prime Meridian line (free in the courtyard; the observatory museum is £18). Then walk downhill through the park, past the flower gardens which are at their ludicrous best in mid-to-late April, to the National Maritime Museum (free) and The Old Royal Naval College (the Painted Hall ceiling is £15 and genuinely stunning — comparable to anything in Rome, which is a bold claim I'm comfortable making).

The cherry blossom avenue on the eastern side of the park typically peaks in the third week of April. Timing it right is mostly luck, but if you're reading this in late April, go now.

Finish at: The Trafalgar Tavern, Park Row, SE10 9NW. Right on the river. Whitebait is the traditional order here — a small plate is about £9. Pair with a pint and watch the Thames roll past.

8. Bloomsbury Squares Loop (3 km / ~45 minutes)

This one's for the days when you want architecture and bookshops, not rolling hills. Start at Russell Square, then walk a figure-eight through Bedford Square (the best-preserved Georgian square in London), Gordon Square, Tavistock Square, and Cartwright Gardens. Every square has its own character, its own garden, its own roster of blue plaques commemorating people who were cleverer than us.

Slot in a visit to the British Museum (free — though the temporary exhibitions run £15–£22 and vary wildly in quality). The Parthenon Marbles and the Rosetta Stone are the headliners, but the Enlightenment Gallery on the ground floor is where I'd actually send you.

Finish at: The Lamb, 94 Lamb's Conduit Street, WC1N 3LZ. A Victorian pub with the original etched-glass snob screens still intact. Pint of Young's around £6.20. Lamb's Conduit Street itself is worth browsing — independent shops, zero chains.

One More Thing
