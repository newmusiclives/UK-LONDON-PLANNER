We'll Never Send You to a Hot Spring That Doesn't Exist

A travel company just routed tourists to a fake geothermal wonder in the Tasmanian wilderness. The culprit was unreviewed AI content — and it's the founding reason LondonPlanned exists.

In January 2026, a group of 24 tourists detoured across remote northeast Tasmania in pursuit of Weldborough Hot Springs — a "tranquil haven rich in therapeutic minerals," according to the travel website they'd trusted. They arrived to find a freezing river, a bewildered hotel owner, and absolutely no hot springs. Not then. Not ever. The springs had been invented, wholesale, by an AI content generator. The tour company's owner later told the Australian Broadcasting Corporation: "Our AI has messed up completely."

It's almost funny. Until you picture those 24 people in their hire cars, having spent hours on Tasmanian backroads on the strength of a confident, beautifully written, entirely fabricated recommendation.

The quiet epidemic of confident wrongness

The Weldborough incident isn't a freak accident — it's a symptom. AI writing tools are extraordinarily good at producing travel content that reads with authority. Flowing prose, vivid descriptors, sensible-sounding recommendations. The problem is that generative AI doesn't verify. It pattern-matches. It produces the kind of text that would exist if the thing were real — which, for a place like Weldborough, is a near-perfect impersonation of genuine knowledge.

London is a different kind of problem. Unlike remote Tasmanian wilderness, London is so thoroughly documented that AI can produce plausible-sounding itineraries without obvious fabrications. But plausible isn't the same as right. The pub that was wonderful is now a chain gastropub. The "hidden" market stall has had a TripAdvisor queue since 2019. The jazz bar closes on Tuesdays. None of that makes it into the algorithm.

Every itinerary on this site is written by a human who has actually been there — recently, not theoretically. We verify opening hours. The Soho pub that hasn't changed since 1974 and has no incentive to. The exact right time to visit Maltby Street Market before it's overrun. These aren't secrets — they're just the kind of texture that comes from knowing a place, not processing it.
