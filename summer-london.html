Summer in London 2026

Long days, warm evenings, and a city that comes alive outdoors. From rooftop cocktails to open-air Shakespeare, here is everything to do in London between June and August.

Outdoor Cinema and Theatre

Summer evenings in London stay light until nearly 10pm, making it the perfect season for open-air entertainment.

Outdoor Cinema

London's outdoor cinema scene runs from May through September, with screenings in some of the city's most spectacular settings:

Luna Cinema: The largest outdoor cinema operator in the UK, hosting screenings at Kensington Palace, the Natural History Museum, Greenwich Observatory, and other iconic venues. Films range from new releases to beloved classics. Bring blankets and picnic provisions. Tickets from around 17-20 pounds. Rooftop Film Club: Screenings on rooftops in Peckham, Shoreditch, and Stratford with wireless headphones, deckchairs, and bar service. A more urban, social experience. The Nomad Cinema: A pop-up cinema that moves to a different secret London location each week. Locations revealed shortly before the event.

Tip: Screenings typically start at sunset (9-9:30pm in June). Bring layers as it cools down quickly after dark, even in summer.

Regent's Park Open Air Theatre

One of London's most magical theatre experiences. This 1,256-seat open-air auditorium in the heart of Regent's Park has been running since 1932 and produces consistently excellent work. The summer season (May-September) typically features a Shakespeare production, a major musical, and a family show. Past productions have won multiple Olivier Awards.

Price: Tickets from 25-65 pounds. The on-site bar and picnic area open from 6pm, and many people make an evening of it with food and drinks in the park before the show. Weather policy: Performances go ahead in light rain (ponchos provided). Refunds offered only if the show is cancelled.

Shakespeare's Globe

Standing tickets (called "groundling" tickets) at Shakespeare's Globe are just 5 pounds and put you right at the foot of the stage in this faithful reconstruction of Shakespeare's original theatre on the South Bank. The open-air experience with the sky above is authentic and thrilling. The summer season runs April-October with a mix of Shakespeare and new writing. Standing for 2+ hours is the trade-off for the low price, but the energy of the groundlings section is unmatched.

Rooftop Bars and Beer Gardens

Best Rooftop Bars

When the sun comes out, Londoners head up. The city's rooftop bar scene is exceptional:

Sky Garden (Walkie Talkie building): Free entry (book online in advance) with panoramic views from the 35th floor. Three bars and a restaurant amid lush tropical gardens. Frank's Cafe (Peckham): A beloved south London institution on top of a multi-storey car park. Simple drinks, incredible sunset views, and a buzzy local crowd. Open May-September. Cash and card. Netil360 (Hackney): Laid-back rooftop with views of the London skyline, a hot tub (bookable), and a roster of DJs. Madison (One New Change): Overlooks St Paul's Cathedral. The terrace is stunning at golden hour. Bussey Rooftop Bar (Peckham): Multi-level creative space with rooftop events, art, and music.

Tip: Popular rooftop bars operate walk-in only and can have long queues on sunny evenings. Arrive before 5pm on weekdays, or check if advance booking is available.

London's Best Beer Gardens

The Great British beer garden is a summer institution. London has some brilliant ones:

The Spaniards Inn (Hampstead): A 16th-century pub with a leafy garden on the edge of Hampstead Heath. Reportedly frequented by Keats, Dickens, and Dick Turpin. The Dove (Hammersmith): Thames-side pub with a terrace right on the river. One of London's most scenic pints. People's Park Tavern (Victoria Park): Huge garden overlooking the park, with a brewery on-site and regular barbecues. The Flask (Highgate): A historic pub with a large terraced garden on the edge of Highgate Village. Edinboro Castle (Camden): Spacious garden with fairy lights, barbecue food, and outdoor bar.

Pro tip: Sunday afternoon in a London beer garden is a quintessential summer experience. Pair it with a roast dinner.

Lidos, Swimming, and Outdoor Activities

Lidos and Outdoor Swimming

London's outdoor swimming scene has enjoyed a huge revival. These open-air pools and natural swimming spots are summer highlights:

Hampstead Heath Ponds: Three natural swimming ponds (men's, women's, and mixed) fed by underground springs. Swimming in the mixed pond surrounded by trees and wildlife is a uniquely London experience. Water is unheated and cool even in summer. Parliament Hill Lido: A large, unheated 60-metre stainless steel pool on the south side of Hampstead Heath. Open year-round but peak season is June-August. Brockwell Lido (Herne Hill): A beautiful 1930s Art Deco lido in Brockwell Park with a poolside cafe. One of London's most popular. Book in advance in summer. Serpentine Lido (Hyde Park): Swim in the Serpentine lake in the heart of central London. Open June-September.

Price: Most lidos charge 4-9 pounds per swim. Bring your own towel and padlock for lockers.

Thames Beach and River Activities

At low tide, the Thames reveals small sandy and pebbly beaches, particularly along the south bank. The most accessible is at Gabriel's Wharf near the OXO Tower. While swimming in the Thames is not recommended in most areas, you can enjoy the river in other ways: kayaking and paddleboarding are available from several operators along the river, with routes taking you past the Houses of Parliament, Tower Bridge, and the Shard. Thames boat tours and river bus services are a great way to see the city from the water.

Mudlarking: At low tide, you can legally search the Thames foreshore for historical artefacts (pottery shards, clay pipes, Victorian coins). A fascinating and free activity — check tide times before going.

Wimbledon (Late June - Early July)

The Championships at the All England Club are the most prestigious tennis tournament in the world. Getting tickets is notoriously difficult but not impossible:

The Queue: Wimbledon's famous queue system allocates a limited number of tickets each day on a first-come, first-served basis. Arrive very early (many people camp overnight). You can get grounds passes for around 25 pounds on the first week. The Ballot: The public ballot for centre court tickets opens the preceding autumn. Entry is free but success is a lottery. Henman Hill / Murray Mound: Even with just a grounds pass, you can watch matches on the big screen from the hill with a Pimm's in hand — a quintessential British summer experience. Resale tickets: Returned tickets are resold at reduced prices from 3pm each day at the grounds.

Festivals, Concerts, and Carnival

Hyde Park Concerts (BST Hyde Park)

BST Hyde Park (British Summer Time) is London's premier outdoor concert series, held across two weekends in late June and early July. Major headliners perform to crowds of 65,000 in the heart of central London. Past acts have included The Rolling Stones, Adele, Bruce Springsteen, and Taylor Swift. Between headline shows, free "Open House" days offer food, activities, and entertainment in the park.

Tickets: From around 65-120 pounds depending on the artist. VIP and premium packages available. Announced in late autumn for the following summer. Getting there: Marble Arch, Hyde Park Corner, or Lancaster Gate tube stations.

Notting Hill Carnival (August Bank Holiday Weekend)

Europe's largest street festival celebrates Caribbean culture over the August bank holiday weekend (last weekend in August). Two million people fill the streets of Notting Hill for two days of sound systems, steel bands, costumed dancers, and incredible Caribbean food. Sunday is the family-friendly day. Monday is the main parade day with massive sound systems.

What to expect: Jerk chicken, curry goat, rum punch, reggae, soca, calypso, and an atmosphere unlike anything else in London. It is free to attend. Tips: Wear comfortable shoes and clothes you do not mind getting paint on. Bring cash (many food stalls are cash only). Use the Westbourne Park or Ladbroke Grove stations (Notting Hill Gate gets dangerously crowded). Stay hydrated. Leave valuables at home.

Edinburgh Fringe Day Trips (August)

While not in London, the Edinburgh Festival Fringe (throughout August) is reachable as a day trip or overnight excursion. The world's largest arts festival features over 3,000 shows across comedy, theatre, music, and spoken word. Many shows are free.

Getting there: LNER trains from King's Cross take around 4.5 hours. Book in advance for fares from 30-50 pounds each way. Flying takes about 1.5 hours (London City or Luton to Edinburgh). An overnight stay is recommended to make the most of it. Budget tip: The "Free Fringe" programme offers hundreds of shows at no charge (tip the performers). The Royal Mile street performances are free and entertaining.

More Summer Events

BBC Proms (July-September): Eight weeks of classical music concerts at the Royal Albert Hall. Standing "Proms" tickets are just 8 pounds (queue on the day). Wireless Festival: Urban music festival in Finsbury Park featuring major hip-hop, R&B, and grime acts. Field Day: Electronic and indie music in Victoria Park. Lovebox: Eclectic lineup in Gunnersbury Park. Pride in London (late June/early July): The annual parade through central London is one of the city's most colourful and joyful events.

Summer Markets and the Longest Day

Summer Markets

London's markets are at their best in summer, with outdoor stalls in full swing and seasonal produce at its peak:

Borough Market: London's most famous food market is open Wednesday-Saturday. Summer brings British strawberries, artisan ice cream, and fresh oysters alongside the year-round favourites. Broadway Market (Hackney): Saturday market with street food, vintage clothing, and a community atmosphere. Walk through London Fields afterwards for sunbathing and barbecues. Columbia Road Flower Market: Sunday mornings only, this east London street fills with flowers and plants. Arrive early (8am) to browse, or come at 2pm for bargains as sellers cut prices. Maltby Street Market: A smaller, less crowded alternative to Borough Market, tucked under the railway arches in Bermondsey. Outstanding food stalls. Portobello Road: The famous antiques market on Saturdays, stretching through colourful Notting Hill.

Summer Solstice and Longest Day (Around 21 June)

The longest day of the year gives London over 16 hours of daylight, with sunrise around 4:45am and sunset after 9:20pm. Special events mark the occasion:

Sunrise at Primrose Hill: Join early risers watching the sun come up over the London skyline. Stonehenge (day trip): English Heritage opens Stonehenge for free on the solstice. Trains from Waterloo to Salisbury take 1.5 hours. Longest Day walks: The Jubilee Greenway, Thames Path, and Regent's Canal walks are all spectacular in the extended daylight. Late dining: Many restaurants with outdoor terraces extend their service hours. Eating outdoors at 9pm in broad daylight feels wonderfully continental.

Practical Tips for Summer in London

Weather: Summer temperatures range from 18-30 degrees Celsius, but rain remains a possibility. Pack layers, sunscreen, and a light waterproof jacket. Heatwaves (30+ degrees) are becoming more common. The Tube has no air conditioning on older lines — travel can be uncomfortable in extreme heat. Accommodation: Summer is peak season. Book hotels at least 2-3 months ahead. Parks: London's royal parks are at their most beautiful. Hyde Park, Regent's Park (especially the rose garden), Richmond Park (deer spotting), and Hampstead Heath are all free. Hydration: Carry a refillable water bottle. Free water refill stations are available across the city.
