Clerkenwell Neighbourhood Guide

London's design capital — medieval monastery, Victorian factories, Exmouth Market, and the country's most concentrated foodie district

Overview

Clerkenwell sits just north of the City — a medieval religious quarter (Clerk's Well, Charterhouse, Priory of St John) converted through the industrial era into a Victorian printing-and-clockmaking district, now reconverted into the country's densest design/architecture/publishing quarter.

Sandwiched between Smithfield Market and Exmouth Market, it's a foodie magnet — St. JOHN, Quality Chop House, Morito, Moro, Sessions Arts Club, and a rotating cast of London's most ambitious new restaurants.

The vibe: Creative, design-forward, confident. Suits mixing with architects, publishers, and chefs. Evenings buzzy.

Best for: Foodies, design lovers, architecture fans, people who want a substantial neighbourhood experience away from tourist crowds.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Charterhouse

    Charterhouse Square, EC1M 6AN

    Medieval monastery turned Tudor mansion turned almshouse — still housing 40 "brothers". Guided tours only (£15), book ahead.

  • 2. Museum of the Order of St John

    St John's Gate, St John's Lane, EC1M 4DA

    The only surviving part of the 12th-century Priory of St John. Free museum on the Crusader Knights Hospitaller and the origin of St John Ambulance.

  • 3. Marx Memorial Library

    37a Clerkenwell Green, EC1R 0DU

    Where Lenin edited Iskra from 1902-03 — a tiny radical history museum and library, tours available.

  • 4. Smithfield Market

    Grand Ave, EC1A 9PS

    The 800-year-old meat market, operating nightly. Vast Victorian iron-and-glass structure best seen 3am-7am. Due to close permanently 2027.

  • 5. Sadler's Wells

    Rosebery Ave, EC1R 4TN

    The world's leading dance house — contemporary, ballet, flamenco, hip-hop, on a site where a spring-fed theatre first opened in 1683.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. St. JOHN Smithfield

    26 St John St, EC1M 4AY · British · $$$$

    Fergus Henderson's nose-to-tail British restaurant (1994) — the birthplace of modern British cooking. Bone marrow and parsley salad is legendary.

  • 2. Quality Chop House

    94 Farringdon Rd, EC1R 3EA · British · $$$$

    A 1869 working-class chop house, Grade II-listed, now seasonal British in the original wooden booths. Quality Wines wine-shop next door serves £14 lunches.

  • 3. Moro

    34-36 Exmouth Market, EC1R 4QE · Spanish · $$$

    The Clerkenwell restaurant that invented Moorish-Mediterranean cooking for London (1997). Sister Morito next door is tapas-focused and walk-in friendly.

  • 4. Sessions Arts Club

    24 Clerkenwell Green, EC1R 0NA · European · $$$$

    A former courtroom above Old Sessions House — faded grandeur, candle-lit, a Florian-Nouveau interior. Book weeks ahead.

  • 5. The Modern Pantry

    47-48 St John's Square, EC1V 4JJ · Fusion · $$$

    Anna Hansen's Kiwi-fusion all-day café. Sugar-cured prawn omelette is signature.

Best Bars & Pubs

  • 1. The Jerusalem Tavern

    55 Britton St, EC1M 5UQ

    A 1720 gem — St Peter's Brewery's tiny tap, feels like a time capsule. Squeeze onto a bench.

  • 2. The Betsey Trotwood

    56 Farringdon Rd, EC1R 3BL

    Victorian pub with basement-gig singer-songwriter scene. Gigs £5-10 most nights.

  • 3. Fox & Anchor

    115 Charterhouse St, EC1M 6AA

    Smithfield pub open from 7am for "market breakfasts" — the City Boy breakfast includes half a pint of Guinness.

  • 4. Oriole

    East Poultry Ave, Smithfield, EC1A 9LH

    Underground cocktail bar from the Nightjar team. Live jazz nightly, global cocktail menu.

  • 5. Workshop (Three Crowns)

    8 East Rd, N1 6AD

    Hoxton-border neighbourhood pub-hotel — excellent craft beer range and a Sunday roast beloved of Clerkenwell locals.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Workshop Coffee Clerkenwell

    27 Clerkenwell Rd, EC1M 5RN

    Flagship roastery of one of London's most influential specialty coffee companies. Rotating single-origin espresso, filter flights.

  • 2. Caravan Exmouth Market

    11-13 Exmouth Market, EC1R 4QD

    Original Caravan — a weekend brunch queue, New Zealand-inspired menu, in-house coffee roastery. Cornbread with maple butter is the classic.

  • 3. Prufrock Coffee

    23-25 Leather Ln, EC1N 7TE

    A Gwilym Davies-founded Chancery Lane specialty coffee bar. Regular brewing classes for coffee nerds.

Shopping Highlights

  • Exmouth Market — pedestrianised street of independent shops and restaurants. Farmers' market on Saturdays.
  • Leather Lane Market — a weekday food-truck and clothing market popular with Clerkenwell office workers.
  • Family Tree (53 Exmouth Market) — jewellery and homewares boutique showcasing young British makers.
  • Clerkenwell Design Week (May) — the UK's biggest design festival opens showrooms across the district.
  • Magma Bookshop (117-119 Clerkenwell Rd) — specialist design, architecture, and graphic arts bookshop.
  • Ottolenghi Islington (short walk north) — the original deli of Yotam Ottolenghi, with jewel-like salads and cakes.

Getting There

Main stations serving this neighbourhood:

  • Farringdon (Circle, Metropolitan, Hammersmith & City, Elizabeth) — the Clerkenwell gateway — also Thameslink mainline
  • Barbican (Circle, Metropolitan, Hammersmith & City) — east side — adjacent to Barbican and Museum of London
  • Angel (Northern) — north side — Sadler's Wells five-minute walk
  • Chancery Lane (Central) — south side — Leather Lane nearby

Insider Tips

  • Clerkenwell Design Week (May) opens 300+ design showrooms across the district — mostly free.
  • St. JOHN is easier to book for lunch than dinner; bakery-café on site sells their famous Eccles cakes.
  • Postman's Park (Aldersgate, just south) is a beautiful hidden garden with the Watts Memorial — plaques commemorating ordinary Londoners who died saving others.
  • The secret Medieval Hall at Charterhouse is visible only on guided tours — book weeks ahead.
  • Exmouth Market is at its best on weekday lunchtimes or Saturday morning farmers' market.
