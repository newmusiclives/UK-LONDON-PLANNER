London's Best Markets: A Complete Guide

From ancient Borough Market to the flower-scented lanes of Columbia Road, London's markets are where the city's food, fashion, and creative culture come alive.

Planning Your Market Visits

London has over 200 markets. This guide focuses on the 16 that are genuinely worth a trip. Most markets operate on specific days, so plan accordingly. We have grouped them by what they are best known for: food, vintage and fashion, antiques, and flowers. Arrive early (before 10 am) for the best picks and smallest crowds, or late (after 3 pm) for reduced prices as traders pack up.

Food Markets

1. Borough Market

Location: 8 Southwark Street, SE1 1TL • Nearest Tube: London Bridge

Days: Mon–Sat (limited Mon–Tue; full market Wed–Sat, 10 am–5 pm)

Best for: Artisan food, fresh produce, gourmet street food

Top picks: Kappacasein raclette (the famous melted cheese), Bread Ahead doughnuts, Shellseekers fresh oysters (£2 each), Monmouth Coffee, Ethiopian injera wraps from Baladi. The cheese and charcuterie stalls are world-class.

Tips: Wednesday and Thursday mornings are the sweet spot — full market with manageable crowds. Saturday before 10 am is fine; Saturday at noon is elbow-to-elbow. Eat as you go rather than sitting down — the best food is from the stalls, not the restaurants.

2. Maltby Street Market

Location: Ropewalk, SE1 3PA • Nearest Tube: Bermondsey / London Bridge

Days: Sat 10 am–5 pm, Sun 11 am–4 pm

Best for: Gourmet food from small producers, natural wine

Top picks: The Waffle Station (Liege waffles), St John Bakery (legendary doughnuts), Tozino (Iberico ham), Little Bird Gin distillery tastings, Kappacasein (their second location, usually less of a queue than Borough).

Tips: The locals' alternative to Borough Market. Smaller, cooler, and less touristy. It runs along two railway arches — the atmosphere is industrial-chic. Saturday is busier but has more stalls.

3. Broadway Market

Location: Broadway Market, E8 4PH • Nearest rail: London Fields

Days: Sat 9 am–5 pm (the big market day); some shops open daily

Best for: Brunch, artisan food, trendy East London vibe

Top picks: Vietnamese banh mi, Fin & Flounder (fishmonger with incredible fish finger sandwiches), wild mushroom toasties, artisan sourdough, and handmade pasta. The surrounding cafes and shops are excellent — Climpson & Sons coffee, L'Eau a la Bouche patisserie.

Tips: Walk through London Fields park afterwards. The combination of the market and the park makes a perfect Saturday morning. Arrive by 10 am for food stalls at their freshest.

4. Mercato Metropolitano

Location: 42 Newington Causeway, SE1 6DR • Nearest Tube: Elephant & Castle

Days: Daily, 8 am–11 pm (varies by stall)

Best for: International street food, all-day eating, community vibe

Top picks: Wood-fired Neapolitan pizza, Argentinian empanadas, craft beer, Korean fried chicken, plant-based burgers, and a full Italian deli. The community garden and cinema screenings add to the atmosphere.

Tips: Open daily (unlike most markets), making it a reliable option any day of the week. The evening atmosphere with live music and communal tables is the best time to visit. Good for groups with varied tastes.

5. Berwick Street Market

Location: Berwick Street, Soho, W1F • Nearest Tube: Piccadilly Circus / Oxford Circus

Days: Mon–Sat, 8 am–6 pm

Best for: Lunchtime street food in central London, fresh produce

Top picks: Rotating street food traders (Thai, Mexican, Japanese), excellent fresh fruit and veg, and a long-standing fabric stall. The surrounding Soho streets have some of London's best independent restaurants.

Tips: One of the few markets right in the West End. Perfect for a quick, cheap, quality lunch between sightseeing. Weekday lunchtimes are busiest (office workers) but the food is worth the queue.

Vintage, Fashion & General

6. Camden Market

Location: Camden Lock Place, NW1 8AF • Nearest Tube: Camden Town

Days: Daily, 10 am–6 pm (busiest weekends)

Best for: Alternative fashion, vintage clothing, international street food, live music

Top picks: The Stables Market for vintage and alternative fashion, Camden Lock Market for crafts and art, the food stalls along the canal for an incredible range of global street food. The leather goods, band t-shirts, and handmade jewellery stalls are Camden institutions.

Tips: Camden is actually several markets merged together — Stables Market, Lock Market, Buck Street, and Inverness Street. Budget 2–3 hours to explore properly. Weekday mornings are dramatically quieter than weekends. Haggling is acceptable at many stalls.

7. Portobello Road Market

Location: Portobello Road, W11 • Nearest Tube: Ladbroke Grove / Notting Hill Gate

Days: Mon–Sat (antiques mainly Sat; general market daily except Sun)

Best for: Antiques (Saturdays), vintage fashion, colourful street atmosphere

Top picks: Antique silverware, vintage clothing at the Portobello Green end, retro posters and prints, and the food stalls near the flyover. The antiques section (Notting Hill end) is one of the world's largest. Alice's Antiques (the famously overstuffed shop) is an icon.

Tips: Saturday is THE day — the full market stretches nearly two miles. Start at the Notting Hill Gate end for antiques, walk north for food, vintage, and fashion. Arrive by 9 am for the best antiques. The street is beautiful even without the market — the pastel-coloured houses are iconic.

8. Brick Lane Market

Location: Brick Lane, E1 6QL • Nearest Tube: Shoreditch High Street / Liverpool Street

Days: Sun 10 am–5 pm (main market); some indoor venues open daily

Best for: Vintage fashion, street art, multicultural street food, vinyl records

Top picks: The Vintage Market (indoor, daily) for curated vintage clothing. The Sunday Upmarket for handmade crafts and design. The Backyard Market for art and homewares. Beigel Bake (open 24/7) for the famous salt beef bagel (£5). Bengali curry houses line the street.

Tips: Sunday is the main event when the entire street becomes a market. Combined with Spitalfields (10-min walk), you could spend a full day market-hopping. The street art in the surrounding lanes is some of London's best.

9. Old Spitalfields Market

Location: 16 Horner Square, E1 6EW • Nearest Tube: Liverpool Street

Days: Daily (different themes each day)

Best for: Independent fashion, art, design, and covered market browsing

Top picks: Thursday for antiques and vintage. Friday for independent fashion designers. Weekends for the biggest selection of everything. The food stalls inside the market hall are excellent — look for the Ethiopian stall and the ramen vendor.

Tips: Fully covered, making it perfect for rainy days. Combine with Brick Lane (5-min walk) on a Sunday for the ultimate East London market experience. The surrounding streets have excellent coffee shops and restaurants.

10. Greenwich Market

Location: 5b Greenwich Market, SE10 9HZ • Nearest: Cutty Sark DLR / Greenwich rail

Days: Daily, 10 am–5.30 pm

Best for: Arts, crafts, handmade jewellery, antiques (Tue/Thu/Fri), street food

Top picks: Handmade jewellery and ceramics from local artisans, vintage maps and prints, the churros stall, Ethiopian coffee ceremony, and handmade fudge. The craft quality here is generally higher than at the larger markets.

Tips: Combine with a visit to the Royal Observatory, the Cutty Sark, and the National Maritime Museum — all within a 10-minute walk. Getting here by Thames Clipper from central London is half the fun. Weekends are busiest; Tuesday antiques market is a hidden gem.

Antiques & Collectables

11. Bermondsey Antiques Market (Bermondsey Square)

Location: Bermondsey Square, SE1 3UN • Nearest Tube: Borough / Bermondsey

Days: Fri 6 am–2 pm

Best for: Serious antiques, silverware, porcelain, art

Top picks: Georgian silver, vintage jewellery, oil paintings, rare books, mid-century ceramics. This is where London's antiques dealers come to buy stock — the quality and authenticity are high.

Tips: The early bird gets the bargain — serious buyers arrive at dawn with torches. By 10 am the best pieces are gone. This is not a tourist market; it is a trade market that the public can attend. Bring cash — many dealers prefer it. Haggling is expected.

12. Alfie's Antiques Market

Location: 13–25 Church Street, NW8 8DT • Nearest Tube: Marylebone / Edgware Road

Days: Tue–Sat, 10 am–6 pm

Best for: 20th-century design, vintage fashion, Art Deco, mid-century furniture

Top picks: Over 75 dealers across four floors of a former department store. Standouts include vintage fashion from the 1920s–1980s, Art Deco lighting, mid-century Scandinavian furniture, vintage posters, and costume jewellery. The rooftop cafe is a hidden gem.

Tips: Less well-known than Portobello but often better value. The dealers are knowledgeable and happy to chat about their stock. Allow 1–2 hours to browse properly. The surrounding Church Street has more antiques shops.

Specialist Markets

13. Columbia Road Flower Market

Location: Columbia Road, E2 7RG • Nearest Tube: Hoxton (Overground) / Shoreditch High Street

Days: Sun 8 am–3 pm only

Best for: Flowers, plants, garden accessories, the atmosphere

Top picks: Cut flowers (often half the price of florists), potted herbs, orchids, succulents, and seasonal blooms. The independent shops lining the street — vintage homewares, artisan bread, perfume, ceramics — only open on Sundays during market hours.

Tips: Arrive at 8 am for the best selection and breathing room, or after 2 pm for reduced prices (traders sell off remaining stock cheaply). By 10 am the narrow street is packed shoulder-to-shoulder. This is one of London's most atmospheric markets — the barrow boys calling out prices is pure East End theatre.

14. Brixton Village & Market Row

Location: Coldharbour Lane, SW9 8PS • Nearest Tube: Brixton

Days: Daily (hours vary by shop; many closed Mon)

Best for: International food (Caribbean, Colombian, Vietnamese, West African), independent restaurants, vibrant atmosphere

Top picks: Franco Manca (the original, pizza from £7), Fish, Wings & Tings (Caribbean), Casa Morita (Mexican), Salon (modern British), and the many Afro-Caribbean grocery stalls selling ingredients you will not find anywhere else in London.

Tips: Two interconnected covered arcades — Brixton Village and Market Row — forming a labyrinth of small restaurants and shops. Evenings are magical when the arcade is lit up and buzzing. This is one of London's most culturally diverse food destinations.

15. Southbank Book Market

Location: Under Waterloo Bridge, SE1 • Nearest Tube: Waterloo

Days: Daily, 10 am–7 pm (weather permitting)

Best for: Second-hand and antiquarian books

Top picks: Vintage Penguin paperbacks, old maps, antique prints, art books, and first editions. Prices are reasonable — paperbacks from £1, hardbacks from £3, rare editions priced individually.

Tips: Sheltered under the bridge so it works in light rain. Combine with a walk along the South Bank, the Tate Modern, and the BFI. Browsing the tables is a meditative pleasure — take your time.

16. Leadenhall Market

Location: Gracechurch Street, EC3V 1LT • Nearest Tube: Bank / Monument

Days: Mon–Fri (closed weekends)

Best for: Architecture, cheese, wine bars, Harry Potter location

Top picks: The Cheese at Leadenhall (artisan British cheeses), wine merchants, and several excellent wine bars and pubs including The Lamb Tavern. The painted Victorian ironwork and glass roof are stunning.

Tips: Primarily a City lunchtime destination — peak buzz is 12–2 pm on weekdays. Used as Diagon Alley in the Harry Potter films. Worth a visit even if you are not buying anything, just for the architecture.

Markets by Day of the Week

  • Monday: Borough Market (limited), Berwick Street, Leadenhall
  • Tuesday: Borough (limited), Alfie's Antiques, Greenwich (antiques day), Leadenhall
  • Wednesday: Borough (full), Berwick Street, Leadenhall
  • Thursday: Borough (full), Old Spitalfields (antiques), Greenwich (antiques), Leadenhall
  • Friday: Borough (full), Bermondsey Antiques (early morning), Old Spitalfields (fashion), Leadenhall, Maltby Street (some stalls)
  • Saturday: Borough (full & busiest), Portobello Road (full with antiques), Camden, Broadway Market, Maltby Street, Alfie's Antiques, Greenwich
  • Sunday: Columbia Road Flowers, Brick Lane (full), Camden, Maltby Street, Greenwich, Old Spitalfields (busiest day)

Best day for market-hopping: Saturday gives you Borough Market, Broadway Market, and Maltby Street all in one area south of the river, plus Camden or Portobello in the afternoon. Sunday gives you Columbia Road, Brick Lane, and Spitalfields in one East London circuit.
