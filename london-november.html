London in November

Fireworks light up the skies, Christmas sparkle arrives, and London's cultural scene offers warmth against the chill.

Weather Overview

Average High: 10 °C (50 °F)  |  Average Low: 5 °C (41 °F)

Rainfall: Around 55 mm across 10–12 rain days. A mix of drizzle and grey overcast days, though crisp, sunny days do appear.

Daylight: 8–10 hours. Sunrise after 7:00 am, sunset by 4:00 pm by late November. The short days make it essential to plan outdoor activities for midday.

What to expect: November is properly cold, damp, and dark — but that's part of London's charm this time of year. Morning fog over the Thames, the smell of roasting chestnuts on street corners, and the first Christmas lights flickering to life create a special atmosphere you won't find in summer.

What's On in November

  • Bonfire Night / Guy Fawkes Night (5 November) — Fireworks displays across the city. The best free views are from Primrose Hill, Parliament Hill, and Alexandra Palace. Paid events at Battersea Park, Victoria Park, and Blackheath have fairground rides and food stalls.
  • Lord Mayor's Show (second Saturday in November) — A spectacular 800-year-old pageant through the City of London with floats, marching bands, military detachments, and a fireworks finale over the Thames. Completely free to watch.
  • Christmas lights switch-ons — Oxford Street, Regent Street, Carnaby Street, Bond Street, and Covent Garden all switch on their lights in November. Each has its own ceremony, often with celebrity appearances and performances.
  • Remembrance Sunday (second Sunday) — The solemn ceremony at the Cenotaph on Whitehall, with the Royal Family and veterans. Arrive very early if you wish to watch; the two-minute silence at 11 am is deeply moving.
  • EFG London Jazz Festival (mid-November) — Ten days of jazz, soul, and world music at venues including the Barbican, Ronnie Scott's, the Royal Festival Hall, and intimate clubs across the city.
  • Regent Street Motor Show — A free display of classic, vintage, and modern cars along Regent Street, which closes to traffic for the day.
  • Winter Wonderland opens (mid-November) — Hyde Park's massive Christmas festival launches with rides, an ice rink, Christmas markets, a circus, and bars. Free entry; pay per ride and activity.
  • Christmas markets begin — Southbank Centre Winter Market, Leicester Square Christmas Market, and the German-style market at Kingston upon Thames all open in November.

Top Things to Do

  • Watch fireworks on Bonfire Night — Head to Alexandra Palace for a stunning display with panoramic views over London. Wrap up warm, arrive early, and enjoy mulled wine and hot food from the stalls.
  • Walk the Christmas lights trail — Start at Oxford Circus, walk down Regent Street to Piccadilly Circus, cut through to Carnaby Street, then continue to Covent Garden. The lights are best after 4:30 pm when it's fully dark.
  • Catch a West End show — November is an excellent time for theatre. New productions are well-established, and it's before the Christmas rush. Book matinees for a cosy afternoon out of the cold.
  • Explore the Barbican Conservatory — London's secret tropical garden, free to visit on Sundays and select dates. Lush greenery and warmth on a cold November day. Book a free timed slot online.
  • Visit the Churchill War Rooms — The underground bunker where Churchill directed WWII operations. Poignant any time, but especially around Remembrance Sunday. Quieter on weekday mornings.
  • Museum late nights — Many museums host late-night events with DJs, talks, and special access. The V&A Friday Late, Science Museum Lates, and Natural History Museum after-hours events are all excellent.
  • Sample mulled wine at a Christmas market — The Southbank Centre Winter Market has an outstanding selection, with fairy lights strung along the Thames. Combine with a Southbank walk and a show at the National Theatre.
  • See a film at the BFI Southbank — Four screens showing everything from new releases to rare classics. The Mediatheque (free) lets you browse the BFI's vast archive. The bar and restaurant overlooking the Thames are great spots to linger.
  • Explore the Soane Museum (Lincoln's Inn Fields) — Sir John Soane's eccentric house-museum, crammed with art and antiquities, is free and atmospheric. The candlelit evening openings on the first Tuesday of each month are magical.
  • Ice skating — Seasonal rinks open at the Natural History Museum, Somerset House, Hampton Court Palace, and the Tower of London. Book in advance; Somerset House is the most photogenic.

Where to Eat & Drink

  • Rules (Covent Garden) — London's oldest restaurant (est. 1798). November is game season at its peak: roast grouse, venison, and woodcock in an opulent Edwardian dining room. Book ahead.
  • Brasserie Zédel (Piccadilly) — A grand French brasserie hidden underground near Piccadilly Circus. Astonishingly affordable prix-fixe menus in a jaw-dropping Art Deco space. Perfect for a pre-theatre dinner.
  • Warming street food at Southbank — The Winter Market serves raclette, bratwurst, churros, and Dutch pancakes. Grab a portion and watch the Thames glitter with reflections from the Christmas lights.
  • The Lamb (Bloomsbury) — A perfectly preserved Victorian pub with original "snob screens" (etched glass swivel panels). Cosy, warm, and serving excellent Young's ales. A November evening essential.
  • Hot pot at Shuang Shuang (Chinatown) — Individual hot pot stations where you cook your own selection of meats, vegetables, and noodles in bubbling broth. Deeply warming on a cold November night.
  • Afternoon tea at Fortnum & Mason (Piccadilly) — Their Diamond Jubilee Tea Salon serves an iconic afternoon tea. November is quieter than December, so you'll have a more relaxed experience before the Christmas rush.

Insider Tips

  • November is the cheapest month to visit — It's deep low season. Hotels offer their best rates, and you'll find excellent flight deals. Many hotels run "Black Friday" promotions for immediate or future stays.
  • Start Christmas shopping early — London's shops are beautifully decorated but far less crowded than December. Liberty, Selfridges, Harrods, and Fortnum & Mason all unveil their Christmas windows and displays in November.
  • Dress for the cold and damp — November's chill is damp rather than crisp, which makes it feel colder than the temperature suggests. Thermal layers, waterproof boots, and a good coat are non-negotiable.
  • Maximise short daylight — With only 8–9 hours of daylight, plan your itinerary carefully. Do outdoor sightseeing and parks between 10 am and 3 pm; save indoor attractions for mornings and late afternoons.
  • Book Bonfire Night events early — Paid fireworks displays sell out weeks in advance. Free viewpoints get crowded; arrive at least an hour before the display starts to secure a good spot.

What to Pack

  • A warm, waterproof winter coat — ideally windproof too
  • Thermal base layers for particularly cold days
  • A warm hat, scarf, and gloves
  • Waterproof boots or shoes with warm linings
  • An umbrella (wind-resistant is essential; cheap ones will invert instantly)
  • Hand warmers for fireworks nights and evening walks
