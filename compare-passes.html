What Is Included?

London Pass (80+ Attractions)

The London Pass gives unlimited entry to 80+ attractions during your chosen number of consecutive days. It works on a "use it or lose it" model — once activated, the clock starts ticking. Key inclusions:

  • Westminster Abbey (£27)
  • St Paul's Cathedral (£23)
  • The Shard (£32)
  • Tower Bridge Exhibition (£12.30)
  • Hampton Court Palace (£29.50)

Go City Explorer Pass (Choose 2–7)

Rather than all-inclusive, you pre-select how many attractions you want (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, or 7) and use them at your own pace within 60 days of first use. Overlapping but not identical to the London Pass:

  • Tower of London
  • Westminster Abbey
  • The Shard
  • St Paul's Cathedral
  • London Zoo
  • Thames River Cruise
  • Madame Tussauds (£36)
  • SEA LIFE London (£27)
  • London Eye (£32)

  • Kew Gardens

Individual Tickets

Remember that many of London's best attractions are completely free: the British Museum, National Gallery, Tate Modern, V&A, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Imperial War Museum, and most major parks. If your itinerary leans towards free attractions, individual tickets for 2–3 paid sights will almost always be cheaper than a pass.

Break-Even Analysis

London Pass (1-day, £94): You need to visit roughly 3–4 premium paid attractions in a single day to break even. That means something like Tower of London (£33.60) + St Paul's (£23) + The Shard (£32) = £88.60. Add a Thames cruise (£21) and you are ahead. This pace is exhausting but doable.
London Pass (3-day, £148): You need about £50/day in paid attractions. More realistic pace — 2 major sights per day. You will comfortably break even if you avoid spending full days in free museums.
London Pass (6-day, £198): You need just £33/day, which is one major attraction per day. Easily achievable. The 6-day pass offers the best value per day.
Go City Explorer (4 choices, £94): You need £23.50 average per attraction. Since most premium sights cost £25–35, you break even on almost any combination of 4 paid attractions.
Individual tickets: Always cheapest if you plan to visit 2 or fewer paid attractions per day, or if your itinerary is mostly free museums and parks.

Our Verdict by Itinerary Type

First-Time Sightseeing Blitz (3–5 days)

London Pass 3-day or 6-day. If you want to cram in the Tower, Westminster Abbey, St Paul's, The Shard, and more, the all-inclusive pass saves money and lets you skip some queues.

Relaxed Culture Trip (4–7 days)

Individual tickets or Go City Explorer 3–4. If you plan to spend half-days in the free museums (British Museum, Tate Modern, V&A), you only need a handful of paid entries. Cherry-pick with the Explorer pass or just buy tickets individually.

Weekend City Break (1–2 days)

Individual tickets. With only 1–2 days, you will likely visit 2–3 paid sights at most. Buying individually is cheaper unless every stop is premium.

Families with Children

London Pass or Go City. Children's passes are significantly cheaper, and the skip-the-queue benefit at popular attractions like the Tower of London and London Zoo saves valuable family time and patience.

Repeat Visitors

Individual tickets. You have already seen the blockbusters. Pick 1–2 new attractions, buy individual tickets, and spend the rest of your time in free galleries, markets, and neighbourhoods.

Budget Travellers

Individual tickets + free attractions. London has more world-class free attractions than any other city. You can have an incredible week without buying a single pass. Save your money for food and experiences instead.

Tips for Getting the Most from Passes

Plan your itinerary before buying. List every attraction you want to visit, add up the individual prices, and compare against the pass cost. This takes 10 minutes and can save you £50+.

Start early on activation day. The London Pass runs on calendar days, not 24-hour periods. Activate it at 9 AM, not 2 PM, to maximise your time.

Book timed entries where required. Some attractions (like The Shard and the London Eye) require timed slots even with a pass. Book these in advance to avoid disappointment.

Check for seasonal closures. Some included attractions have limited winter hours or close for maintenance. Verify opening times before building your schedule around them.

Sample Itineraries: Is the Pass Worth It?

Day 1: Historic London

Tower of London (£33.60) + Tower Bridge (£12.30) + Thames Cruise (£21) + Shakespeare's Globe (£17) = £83.90 individual. A 1-day London Pass at £94 is not quite worth it for this itinerary alone, but add any extra stop and you break even.

Day 2: Royal & Religious London

Westminster Abbey (£27) + Churchill War Rooms (£28) + Kensington Palace (£21) = £76 individual. Combined with Day 1 above, a 2-day London Pass (£124) saves you £35.90 versus buying everything separately.

Day 3: Museums & Markets (Mostly Free)

British Museum (free) + National Gallery (free) + Covent Garden (free) + Borough Market (free) = £0. This is the day that tips the balance. If your itinerary includes a free day like this, individual tickets or a shorter pass may be better value than a 3-day all-inclusive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I pause my London Pass and use it on non-consecutive days?

No. The London Pass runs on consecutive calendar days from activation. Once you tap it at your first attraction, the countdown begins. The Go City Explorer Pass is more flexible — you have 60 days to use your chosen number of attraction entries.

Do passes include skip-the-line access?

The London Pass includes fast-track entry at select attractions (notably the Tower of London and Westminster Abbey). However, this is not universal — you may still queue at popular spots during peak times. The Go City Explorer Pass does not generally include skip-the-line benefits.

Where do I buy them?

Both passes are bought online and delivered digitally to your phone. You can also buy the London Pass at the London Pass Redemption Centre near Leicester Square. We recommend buying online at least a day before — activation does not start until your first use.
