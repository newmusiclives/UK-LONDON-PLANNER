Mayfair Neighbourhood Guide

Bond Street glamour, world-class galleries, Michelin-starred dining, and classic pubs in London's most exclusive postcode

Overview

Mayfair is London at its most refined. Bounded by Oxford Street, Regent Street, Piccadilly, and Park Lane, this square mile of prime real estate has been synonymous with wealth and elegance since the 18th century. Today it houses the densest concentration of luxury hotels, Michelin-starred restaurants, blue-chip art galleries, and designer flagship stores in the country.

The vibe: Polished and quietly confident. Mayfair does not shout — it whispers from behind the doors of private members' clubs, bespoke tailors, and auction houses. Yet it is surprisingly accessible, with beautiful green squares, historic pubs, and free-entry galleries welcoming everyone.

Best for: Luxury shoppers, art enthusiasts, fine-dining devotees, architecture fans, and anyone who wants to experience the most prestigious side of London. Mayfair rewards slow exploration of its elegant streets and hidden mews.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Royal Academy of Arts

    Burlington House, Piccadilly, W1J 0BD

    Founded in 1768, the RA hosts blockbuster exhibitions and the famous Summer Exhibition — the world's longest-running open-submission art show. The recently opened galleries in Burlington Gardens doubled the display space. Free collection displays throughout the year.

  • 2. Bond Street

    Old Bond Street & New Bond Street, W1S

    London's most famous luxury shopping street, home to Cartier, Tiffany, Chanel, Louis Vuitton, and Burberry. Even if you are not buying, the window displays and architecture are worth the stroll. Look for the Churchill and Roosevelt bench sculpture midway along.

  • 3. Handel & Hendrix in London

    25 Brook Street, W1K 4HB

    A wonderfully improbable museum in two adjoining townhouses where George Frideric Handel lived from 1723 and Jimi Hendrix from 1968. Beautifully restored rooms tell the stories of both musical legends.

  • 4. Gagosian Gallery

    20 Grosvenor Hill, W1K 3QD

    The London outpost of the world's most powerful gallery shows major works by contemporary heavyweights like Damien Hirst, Jeff Koons, and Richard Serra. Free entry to all exhibitions in a stunning purpose-built space.

  • 5. Shepherd Market

    Shepherd Market, W1J 7QN

    A hidden village-like enclave of narrow lanes, independent restaurants, and traditional pubs tucked behind Piccadilly. Named after the May Fair that once took place here, it feels like a secret corner of old London.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. The Wolseley

    160 Piccadilly, W1J 9EB • European grand cafe • $$$

    A Mayfair institution housed in a magnificent 1920s car showroom. The Viennese-inspired menu covers everything from breakfast kedgeree to wiener schnitzel. Impeccable service and a real sense of occasion without stuffiness.

  • 2. Gymkhana

    42 Albemarle Street, W1S 4JH • Indian • $$$

    Michelin-starred Indian cuisine inspired by Indian gymkhana clubs. The kid goat methi keema, wild muntjac biryani, and tandoori dishes are outstanding. Colonial-chic interiors and an excellent cocktail list.

  • 3. Scott's

    20 Mount Street, W1K 2HE • Seafood • $$$$

    One of London's most glamorous restaurants, serving impeccable seafood in an art-filled dining room. The dressed crab and Dover sole are legendary. Terrace tables on Mount Street are some of the most coveted in London.

  • 4. Kitty Fisher's

    10 Shepherd Market, W1J 7QF • Modern British • $$$

    An intimate, candlelit restaurant named after an 18th-century courtesan. The wood-grilled cooking is superb, with standout dishes including the smoked butter flatbread and Galician beef. Book well ahead — it only seats 30.

  • 5. Tamarind

    20 Queen Street, W1J 5PR • Indian • $$$

    One of the first Indian restaurants to earn a Michelin star, Tamarind serves refined North Indian cuisine with exceptional tandoor-cooked dishes. The lunch set menu is an excellent way to experience Michelin-starred Indian cooking without the full dinner price.

Best Pubs & Bars

  • 1. Ye Grapes

    16 Shepherd Market, W1J 7QQ

    A wonderfully traditional Victorian pub hidden in Shepherd Market. Low ceilings, wood panelling, and an unpretentious atmosphere that feels worlds away from the luxury that surrounds it. Excellent cask ales and a warm welcome.

  • 2. Connaught Bar

    The Connaught, Carlos Place, W1K 2AL

    Regularly named among the world's best bars. The art deco-inspired interior by David Collins is stunning, and the tableside martini trolley service is theatrical and memorable. Smart dress code. Reservations essential.

  • 3. The Punchbowl

    41 Farm Street, W1J 5RP

    A characterful Georgian pub dating from 1750 on a quiet Mayfair backstreet. Formerly owned by Guy Ritchie, it retains its traditional pub feel downstairs with a smarter dining room above. Good beer and honest pub food.

  • 4. Claridge's Bar

    Claridge's Hotel, Brook Street, W1K 4HR

    An art deco masterpiece in one of London's most famous hotels. The cocktails are exquisite if expensive, and the room itself is reason enough to visit. Afternoon tea in the foyer is another way to experience Claridge's glamour.

  • 5. The Running Horse

    50 Davies Street, W1K 5JE

    One of Mayfair's oldest pubs with records dating to 1738. Recently refurbished but retaining its character, with good ales, a solid wine list, and elevated pub food. A welcome down-to-earth option in a rarefied neighbourhood.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Richoux

    172 Piccadilly, W1J 9EJ

    A classic Mayfair patisserie and tea room that has been serving cakes and light meals since 1909. Old-fashioned charm with white-clothed tables, display cabinets of pastries, and properly brewed tea.

  • 2. Sketch (The Parlour)

    9 Conduit Street, W1S 2XG

    The ground-floor patisserie and cafe at Sketch is an extravagant, Instagram-famous space serving outstanding pastries, cakes, and specialty coffees. The afternoon tea is legendary, but even a coffee and cake here is a treat.

  • 3. Allpress Espresso

    8 Weighhouse Street, W1K 5LU

    A New Zealand specialty coffee roaster tucked on a quiet street behind Oxford Street. No-nonsense flat whites and long blacks in a pared-back industrial space. A welcome escape from the Mayfair grandeur when you just want a great coffee.

Shopping Highlights

  • Bond Street — The epicentre of London luxury. Old Bond Street and New Bond Street are lined with Cartier, Chanel, Dior, Gucci, and every major fashion and jewellery house.
  • Savile Row — The world's most famous street for bespoke tailoring, home to legendary houses including Henry Poole, Gieves & Hawkes, and Huntsman.
  • Burlington Arcade — A covered Regency-era shopping arcade with uniformed beadles, housing jewellers, cashmere specialists, and perfumers. One of the most beautiful shopping experiences in London.
  • Mount Street — A quieter alternative to Bond Street with Marc Jacobs, Balenciaga, Christian Louboutin, and the excellent Mount Street Deli.
  • Cork Street galleries — London's traditional gallery street with a recently rebuilt row of exhibition spaces showing modern and contemporary art, all free to enter.

Getting There

Mayfair is surrounded by Tube stations on all sides:

  • Green Park (Jubilee, Piccadilly, Victoria lines) — the most central station for Mayfair, right at the southeast corner
  • Bond Street (Central, Jubilee, Elizabeth lines) — ideal for Bond Street shopping and the northern part of Mayfair
  • Oxford Circus (Victoria, Central, Bakerloo lines) — for Savile Row and the northeast corner
  • Hyde Park Corner (Piccadilly line) — for the western edge near Park Lane

Insider Tips

  • Mayfair's art galleries are almost all free to enter. Create your own gallery trail along Cork Street, through to Albemarle Street and down to the Royal Academy.
  • For Michelin-starred dining at a fraction of the dinner price, visit restaurants like Gymkhana and Tamarind at lunch when set menus are available.
  • Explore Mayfair's mews streets — the narrow cobbled lanes behind the grand terraces that originally housed horses and carriages. Now converted into some of London's most charming (and expensive) homes.
  • Grosvenor Square, Berkeley Square, and Mount Street Gardens are beautiful green spaces perfect for resting between gallery visits and shopping.
  • The annual Mayfair Art Weekend (usually June) offers free guided walks, open studios, and special gallery events across the neighbourhood.
