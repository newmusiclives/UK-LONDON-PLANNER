The Ultimate London Packing Checklist

Everything you need to pack for London — season by season. No more, no less. Print this page or save it to your phone.

Spring & Summer (March–August)

Avg temp: 11–23 °C Daylight: 12–16 hours Rain: frequent showers

Clothing Essentials

  • Lightweight waterproof jacketNot a heavy coat — a packable rain shell is ideal. London rain is light but frequent.
  • Compact umbrellaA small, sturdy folding umbrella. Do not bother with a full-size one; the wind will destroy it.
  • Layers: T-shirts + a light jumper or cardiganMornings can be cool (12 °C) even when afternoons reach 22 °C. Layering is essential.
  • Comfortable walking shoesYou will walk 10–15 km per day. Trainers or broken-in walking shoes are non-negotiable.
  • One pair of smart-casual shoesFor restaurants, theatre, and evening outings. Loafers or clean boots work well.
  • 3–5 days of underwear and socksWash at your hotel or use a launderette for longer trips.
  • Jeans or trousers (2 pairs)Dark jeans work for both sightseeing and evening dining.
  • Shorts or a light dress (summer only)June–August can hit 25–30 °C. Most buildings lack air conditioning.
  • Sunglasses and sun creamThe British sun can surprise you. SPF 30+ is wise on clear summer days.

Autumn & Winter (September–February)

Avg temp: 3–15 °C Daylight: 8–12 hours Rain: frequent, some fog

Clothing Essentials

  • Warm waterproof coatA mid-length coat that blocks wind and rain. Wool blends with water-resistant coating are ideal.
  • Warm scarf, hat, and glovesDecember–February temperatures regularly drop below 5 °C, especially in the evenings.
  • Thermal base layer (winter)A thin merino or synthetic thermal top makes a huge difference on freezing days.
  • Jumpers and warm layers (2–3)Buildings are well-heated, so you need layers you can remove indoors.
  • Waterproof boots or shoesPavements get wet and slippery. Leather boots with a rubber sole are perfect.
  • Compact umbrellaSame as summer — essential year-round in London.
  • Jeans or warm trousers (2 pairs)Lined trousers or thicker denim for November–February.
  • One smart outfitFor theatre, Christmas dinners, or New Year's celebrations.
  • Pyjamas and a warm sleeping layerSome older hotels and Airbnbs can be draughty at night.

Tech Essentials (All Seasons)

  • UK power adapter (Type G, 3-pin)UK plugs are different from US, EU, and most Asian countries. Buy before you travel — airport ones are overpriced.
  • Portable phone charger (10,000 mAh+)You will use your phone for maps, Oyster balance, restaurant bookings, and photos. Battery drains fast.
  • Charging cables (phone + earbuds)Bring a spare cable. London is not the place to run out of phone battery at 3 PM.
  • Smartphone with offline maps downloadedDownload the Google Maps or Citymapper offline map for London before you leave. Works underground.
  • Earbuds or headphonesFor the Tube, museum audio guides, and downtime. Noise-cancelling is a luxury that pays off on flights.
  • Camera or phone with good cameraLondon is incredibly photogenic. Ensure you have enough storage space cleared.
  • E-reader (optional)Lighter than books for long flights and Tube journeys.

Documents & Money

  • Valid passport (6+ months validity recommended)Some countries require 6 months validity. Check UK entry requirements for your nationality.
  • Visa or ETA (if required)US, Canadian, Australian, and EU citizens do not need a visa for stays under 6 months, but an ETA may be required from 2025 onwards. Check gov.uk.
  • Travel insurance documentsPrint and digital copies. Include your policy number and emergency contact number.
  • Hotel/accommodation confirmationDigital is fine, but a printed backup can help at immigration.
  • Return flight detailsImmigration may ask for proof of onward travel.
  • Contactless bank card (Visa/Mastercard)London is largely cashless. Your bank card is also your Tube pass (contactless payment).
  • Small amount of GBP cash (£50–100)For markets, small shops, and the occasional cash-only vendor. Withdraw from ATMs (avoid bureau de change).
  • Photocopies of key documentsPassport, insurance, and cards. Email copies to yourself as backup.

What NOT to Bring

  • Bulky guidebooksUse your phone. Guidebooks are heavy, outdated by print time, and you have this website.
  • Too many outfitsLondoners dress casually. You do not need a different outfit for every day — nobody notices or cares.
  • Full-size toiletriesBuy travel sizes or purchase on arrival at Boots or Superdrug (cheaper than airport shops).
  • Expensive jewelleryUnnecessary attention and risk. London is safe, but pickpockets target busy tourist areas.
  • Voltage converter (usually)Modern devices (phone chargers, laptops, cameras) are dual-voltage (100–240V). You only need a plug adapter, not a converter.
  • A large suitcase for a short tripTube escalators and stairs are brutal with large luggage. A carry-on or medium case is ideal for up to a week.
Final tip: London has excellent shopping if you forget anything. Primark (for cheap basics), Boots (pharmacy and toiletries), and Muji (travel accessories) are everywhere. Do not stress about packing perfectly — focus on shoes, rain protection, and layers. Everything else can be solved on arrival.
