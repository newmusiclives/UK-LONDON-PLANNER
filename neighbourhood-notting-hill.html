Notting Hill Neighbourhood Guide

Pastel-coloured houses, the world-famous Portobello Road Market, antique treasures, and a village feel in the heart of west London

Overview

Notting Hill is one of London's most photogenic neighbourhoods, a beguiling mix of grand Victorian terraces painted in ice-cream pastels, world-class antique dealers, independent bookshops, and a thriving food scene. Made globally famous by the 1999 film starring Hugh Grant and Julia Roberts, the area has long been a magnet for creatives, celebrities, and anyone drawn to its unique village-within-a-city atmosphere.

The vibe: Relaxed, affluent, and effortlessly stylish. Saturdays bring a carnival atmosphere as thousands flock to Portobello Road Market, while weekdays reveal a quieter, leafy neighbourhood of garden squares and boutique-lined streets. The area's Caribbean heritage — celebrated each August at the Notting Hill Carnival — adds a vibrant cultural dimension.

Best for: Antique hunters, photographers, market lovers, bookworms, film fans, and anyone who appreciates beautiful streets and artisan food. Notting Hill is perfect for a leisurely Saturday exploring.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Portobello Road Market

    Portobello Road, W11 1LJ

    The world's largest antiques market stretches over a mile along Portobello Road every Saturday. The northern end features antiques and vintage, the middle section has fruit, veg, and street food, and the area under the Westway flyover hosts emerging designers and vintage fashion. Arrive before 9am for the best antique finds.

  • 2. The Colourful Houses of Lancaster Road & Westbourne Grove

    Lancaster Road & Westbourne Grove, W11

    The iconic pastel-painted Victorian terraces that have become one of London's most Instagrammed spots. The best stretches are along Lancaster Road, Portobello Road near the junction with Westbourne Grove, and the side streets around Elgin Crescent.

  • 3. The Travel Bookshop (now The Notting Hill Bookshop)

    13 Blenheim Crescent, W11 2EE

    The bookshop that inspired the one in the film Notting Hill. Now rebranded as The Notting Hill Bookshop, it is a beautifully curated independent shop specialising in travel writing, fiction, and coffee-table books. A must-visit for bibliophiles.

  • 4. Museum of Brands

    111-117 Lancaster Road, W11 1QT

    A fascinating museum tracing the history of consumer culture through packaging, advertising, and design from the Victorian era to today. The Time Tunnel walk-through display of over 12,000 original items is mesmerising.

  • 5. Electric Cinema

    191 Portobello Road, W11 2ED

    One of the oldest working cinemas in Britain, opened in 1910. Watch films from luxurious leather armchairs, sofas, or even double beds at the front. A truly unique cinema experience with a licensed bar.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. The Ledbury

    127 Ledbury Road, W11 2AQ • Modern European • $$$$

    Brett Graham's two-Michelin-starred restaurant is one of London's finest dining experiences. The tasting menu showcases British ingredients with extraordinary technique. Book well in advance.

  • 2. Granger & Co.

    175 Westbourne Grove, W11 2SB • Australian • $$

    Bill Granger's all-day Australian cafe is famous for its ricotta hotcakes and sweetcorn fritters. Light-filled and relaxed, it is the perfect Notting Hill brunch spot. Expect queues at weekends.

  • 3. Cocotte

    95 Westbourne Grove, W2 4UW • French rotisserie • $$

    A compact, stylish French rotisserie serving exceptional free-range chicken with sides like truffle mac and cheese and roasted cauliflower. Casual and excellent value for the area.

  • 4. E&O

    14 Blenheim Crescent, W11 1NN • Pan-Asian • $$$

    A long-standing Notting Hill favourite for stylish pan-Asian dining. The dim sum is excellent, the black cod miso is a signature, and the buzzy atmosphere makes it a great spot for a special dinner.

  • 5. Flat Iron

    48 Westbourne Grove, W2 5SH • Steak • $

    A single-menu steak restaurant serving a perfectly cooked flat iron steak with a side salad for a remarkably affordable price. Free creamy ice cream for dessert. No reservations, but the queue moves quickly.

Best Pubs & Bars

  • 1. The Churchill Arms

    119 Kensington Church Street, W8 7LN

    One of London's most photographed pubs, famous for its spectacular flower-covered exterior and Christmas decorations. Inside, it is covered floor to ceiling in memorabilia. They also serve excellent Thai food from the conservatory at the back.

  • 2. The Elgin

    96 Ladbroke Grove, W11 1PY

    A grand Victorian pub on Ladbroke Grove with high ceilings, ornate plasterwork, and a relaxed atmosphere. Great cask ales, a solid pub menu, and pavement tables for warm days.

  • 3. The Distillery

    171 Portobello Road, W11 2DY

    Home to the Portobello Road Gin distillery, this multi-floor venue includes a gin museum, cocktail bar, and the Resting Room lounge. Take a distillery tour or simply enjoy a perfectly mixed G&T.

  • 4. The Cow

    89 Westbourne Park Road, W2 5QH

    Tom Conran's beloved gastropub with an Irish-inspired ground floor bar serving oysters and Guinness, plus a more formal dining room upstairs. A Notting Hill institution with real character.

  • 5. Trailer Happiness

    177 Portobello Road, W11 2DY

    A retro tiki-themed basement bar serving tropical cocktails in a fun, kitsch setting. Rum is the speciality, and the coladas and daiquiris are seriously good. Open Thursday to Sunday evenings.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Farm Girl

    59A Portobello Road, W11 3DB

    An Australian-inspired cafe with a gorgeous pink-hued interior. Famous for their rose lattes and acai bowls. The avocado toast and coconut BLT are also excellent. Very popular at weekends.

  • 2. Coffee Plant

    180 Portobello Road, W11 2EB

    A no-frills specialty coffee roaster that has been on Portobello Road since 1986. They roast their own beans on-site and serve some of the best filter coffee in west London. A proper coffee shop for purists.

  • 3. Books for Cooks

    4 Blenheim Crescent, W11 1NN

    A cookbook shop with a tiny test kitchen at the back where they serve a daily changing lunch menu cooked from the books on sale. The set lunch is a Notting Hill secret and sells out fast. Come early.

Shopping Highlights

  • Portobello Road Antiques — Hundreds of dealers line the southern end of Portobello Road on Saturdays, selling everything from Georgian silverware to vintage maps and Art Deco jewellery.
  • Alice's Antiques — A wonderfully cluttered shop at 86 Portobello Road stuffed with vintage curiosities, furniture, and decorative objects across multiple floors.
  • Westbourne Grove boutiques — Designer and independent fashion shops including Matches, Aesop, and numerous one-off boutiques line this elegant street.
  • Rough Trade West — The original Rough Trade record shop at 130 Talbot Road, a pilgrimage site for indie music fans since 1976.
  • Portobello Green Designers Market — Under the Westway flyover, this Friday and Saturday market showcases emerging fashion designers and vintage clothing dealers.

Getting There

Notting Hill is well served by several Tube stations depending on which part you want to explore:

  • Notting Hill Gate (Central, Circle, District lines) — the main station, at the southern end of Portobello Road
  • Ladbroke Grove (Hammersmith & City, Circle lines) — for the northern end of Portobello Road and the vintage market
  • Westbourne Park (Hammersmith & City, Circle lines) — useful for Westbourne Grove and the western side of the neighbourhood

Insider Tips

  • Saturday is market day and by far the busiest. Arrive before 9am for the best antiques; by 11am the streets are packed. Fridays offer a quieter market experience with many of the same stalls.
  • Walk the full length of Portobello Road from Notting Hill Gate to Ladbroke Grove. The character changes dramatically from antiques to food to fashion to vintage as you head north.
  • For the best photo of the colourful houses, head to the junction of Lancaster Road and Portobello Road early in the morning before the crowds arrive.
  • Notting Hill Carnival (August bank holiday weekend) is Europe's largest street festival. It is exhilarating but extremely crowded. Sunday is family day; Monday is the main adult parade.
  • Explore the side streets off Portobello Road — Blenheim Crescent, Elgin Crescent, and Westbourne Grove have some of the best independent shops and cafes away from the main drag.
