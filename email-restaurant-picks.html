Our Top 5 Restaurant Picks This Season

From a £9 bowl of pasta that rivals Rome to a pink dining room that belongs in a dream — here are five restaurants you should not miss.

1. Padella

££ £ £
Fresh Pasta £7–14 per dish Borough Market, SE1

Padella is the restaurant Londoners queue for without complaint. Hand-rolled pasta made fresh every hour, served at a counter with a view of the open kitchen. The quality rivals any trattoria in Italy, and the prices are genuinely astonishing for central London. It is tiny, always busy, and completely worth the wait.

What to Order

Pici cacio e pepe — the signature dish. Thick, hand-rolled pasta in a perfect emulsion of pecorino and black pepper. Simple, devastating, £9. Also try the pappardelle with 8-hour beef shin ragu (£11.50) and the lemon and mascarpone-filled doughnuts for dessert (£5).

Booking tip: Padella does not take reservations. Join the queue on Southwark Street at least 20 minutes before they open (12 PM weekdays, 11 AM weekends). The queue moves fast. Alternatively, their Shoreditch branch takes bookings online.

2. Dishoom

£ ££ £
Bombay Cafe £12–22 per dish King's Cross, Covent Garden, Shoreditch + more

Inspired by the Irani cafes of 1960s Bombay, Dishoom has become one of London's most beloved restaurant groups. The interiors are gorgeous — distressed wood, ceiling fans, vintage photos — and the food is vibrant, generous, and addictive. They serve breakfast, lunch, and dinner, and every meal is excellent.

What to Order

Black daal — simmered for 24 hours, it is rich, smoky, and buttery. The bacon naan roll at breakfast is legendary (and there is a vegan version with mushrooms). For dinner, add the lamb biryani (£18.90), chicken ruby (£14.50), and a mango lassi to wash it down.

Booking tip: Book online at least 1–2 weeks ahead for dinner. Walk-ins are available for breakfast and lunch, but arrive before 9 AM for breakfast to avoid a 30-minute wait. The King's Cross branch in a converted Victorian transit shed is the most atmospheric.

3. Bao

£ ££ £
Taiwanese £5–16 per dish Soho, Fitzrovia, Borough

Bao started as a market stall and quickly became one of London's most exciting restaurants. The fluffy steamed bao buns are the headline act, but the small plates, fried chicken, and Taiwanese-inspired desserts are just as compelling. The restaurants are minimal and moody — dark wood, dim lighting, and an open kitchen sending out clouds of steam.

What to Order

Classic bao — braised pork in a pillowy steamed bun with peanut powder and coriander (£6). Add the confit pork bao, trotter nuggets (trust us), 40-day aged beef brisket noodles, and the iconic Horlicks ice cream bao for dessert. Order 3–4 dishes per person and share everything.

Booking tip: The original Soho location is walk-in only — queue from 5:30 PM for a 6 PM seat. Bao Fitzrovia and Bao Borough take bookings online and are easier to get into. Lunch is quieter than dinner.

4. The Wolseley

£ £ ££
European Grand Cafe £20–40 per dish Piccadilly, W1

The Wolseley is a Viennese-style grand cafe in a stunning 1920s building on Piccadilly, opposite the Ritz. It feels like stepping into old-world European glamour — black and white marble floors, towering columns, and impeccable service. It is where London's establishment comes for breakfast, and it remains one of the city's most elegant dining rooms.

What to Order

The Wolseley breakfast is the main event: eggs Benedict (£18), Viennoiserie pastries, kedgeree, and excellent coffee. For lunch, the Wiener schnitzel (£28) is the signature dish. Afternoon tea (£37.50) is refined and far less touristy than many alternatives. For a special occasion, the three-course prix fixe lunch at £32.50 is outstanding value for this setting.

Booking tip: Book 1–2 weeks ahead for weekend breakfast and afternoon tea. Weekday lunch is easier. Request a table in the main room (not the side alcoves) for the full architectural experience.

5. Sketch

£ £ £ £
Modern European / Art Destination £45–80 per person Mayfair, W1

Sketch is not just a restaurant — it is a multi-room art installation that happens to serve food. The Gallery (pink room) is covered in David Shrigley illustrations and has become one of London's most Instagram-famous interiors. The Lecture Room upstairs holds two Michelin stars. Even the toilets are an experience — individual egg-shaped pods with mood lighting.

What to Order

Afternoon tea in the Gallery (£59) is the most accessible and memorable option: delicate pastries, finger sandwiches, and scones served on Shrigley-designed plates in that iconic pink room. For a serious dinner, the Lecture Room tasting menu (£145) is outstanding modern French cuisine by Pierre Gagnaire. The Parlour serves more casual all-day dining with excellent patisserie.

Booking tip: Book afternoon tea 2–3 weeks ahead, especially for weekends. Ask for a corner table in the Gallery for the best photos. The Parlour is walk-in only and a great option for pastries and coffee if the Gallery is fully booked.

General Dining Tips for London

Book ahead for dinner. London's popular restaurants fill up fast, especially Thursday through Saturday. Book at least a week in advance for mid-range spots and 2–3 weeks for high-end venues. Lunch is usually easier to walk into.

Lunch is where the value is. Many restaurants offer set lunch menus at 30–50% less than their dinner prices. You get the same kitchen, the same ingredients, just at a friendlier price point. Make lunch your main meal and eat lighter in the evening.

Explore beyond Zone 1. Some of London's best food is in Zone 2 neighbourhoods: Peckham for global street food, Brixton for Caribbean and African cuisine, Dalston for Turkish and Vietnamese, and Tooting for South Indian and Sri Lankan. These areas offer bigger portions at lower prices than central London.

Check for service charges. Most London restaurants add a 12.5% discretionary service charge to the bill. You are not obliged to pay it, but it is standard practice unless the service was genuinely poor. There is no need to tip on top of this charge.

Use OpenTable or Resy. Both apps show real-time availability and often feature exclusive offers or last-minute cancellations at fully-booked restaurants. The Resy app is particularly useful for trending new openings.
