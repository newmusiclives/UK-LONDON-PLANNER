Bermondsey Neighbourhood Guide

The Bermondsey Beer Mile, Maltby Street Market, world-class galleries, and one of London's most exciting food and drink neighbourhoods

Overview

Bermondsey has been transformed from a gritty industrial district into one of London's most exciting neighbourhoods for food, drink, and art. The Victorian railway arches that once housed leather tanneries and warehouses now contain craft breweries, taprooms, artisan bakeries, and gallery spaces. The Saturday scene around Maltby Street Market and the Beer Mile has become one of London's essential weekend experiences.

The vibe: Industrial-chic and effortlessly cool. Bermondsey retains a raw, unpolished edge — crumbling brick arches, cobblestone streets, and glimpses of its working-class past — but overlaid with world-class food, drink, and art. It feels like a neighbourhood that grew organically rather than being gentrified by committee.

Best for: Craft beer enthusiasts, market lovers, art gallery hoppers, food adventurers, and anyone who enjoys exploring converted industrial spaces. Bermondsey is best experienced on a Saturday when the market and taprooms are in full swing.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Bermondsey Beer Mile

    Druid Street to Enid Street, SE16/SE1

    A stretch of railway arches housing some of London's best craft breweries and taprooms, all within walking distance of each other. Key stops include Southwark Brewing Company, Fourpure, Anspach & Hobday, Brew By Numbers, and The Kernel Brewery. Most open Saturday afternoons — create your own pub crawl along the arches.

  • 2. Maltby Street Market

    Maltby Street, SE1 3PA

    A Saturday and Sunday food market in and around the railway arches of Ropewalk, originally an overflow from Borough Market. Much smaller and less touristy than its famous neighbour, with outstanding traders including St. John Bakery, African Volcano, and Wazen Lebanese. Arrive before noon for the best experience.

  • 3. White Cube Bermondsey

    144-152 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3TQ

    One of the world's most influential commercial galleries, showing blue-chip contemporary artists including Damien Hirst, Tracey Emin, and Antony Gormley. The huge industrial space allows for ambitious installations. Free entry to all exhibitions.

  • 4. Fashion and Textile Museum

    83 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3XF

    Founded by designer Zandra Rhodes, this bright orange and pink building houses exhibitions exploring fashion, textiles, and jewellery from the 1950s to today. The exhibitions are always well-curated and visually stunning. Small admission charge.

  • 5. Bermondsey Antiques Market

    Bermondsey Square, SE1 3UN

    A Friday morning antiques and collectibles market in Bermondsey Square, one of London's oldest. Serious dealers arrive at dawn with torches. By 6-7am there is a good selection of silverware, jewellery, vintage prints, and curiosities. Wraps up by early afternoon.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Jose

    104 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3UB • Spanish tapas • $$

    A tiny, standing-room-only sherry and tapas bar from Jose Pizarro. Exceptional Spanish ham carved to order, superb tortilla, and an outstanding sherry selection. No reservations — squeeze in at the counter and graze. His sit-down restaurant, Pizarro, is next door.

  • 2. Padella

    Southwark Street, SE1 1TL • Fresh pasta • $

    Fresh handmade pasta at astonishingly low prices. The pici cacio e pepe and the beef shin pappardelle are legendary. No reservations — join the queue, which is always worth the wait. One of the best value meals in London, near Borough Market.

  • 3. Casse-Croute

    109 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3XB • French bistro • $$

    An intimate French bistro serving classic dishes like duck confit, steak tartare, and tarte tatin with conviction and care. The short, weekly-changing menu is chalked on a blackboard. It feels like a Parisian neighbourhood restaurant transplanted to Bermondsey.

  • 4. Café Murano Bermondsey

    36 St Thomas Street, SE1 3QU • Italian • $$

    Angela Hartnett's accessible Italian restaurant serving refined pasta and regional Italian dishes. The set lunch is excellent value, and the cooking is polished without being pretentious. A great option near London Bridge station.

  • 5. Bala Baya

    Arch 25, Old Union Yard Arches, 229 Union Street, SE1 0LR • Middle Eastern • $$

    A vibrant Middle Eastern restaurant in a converted railway arch with colourful interiors and a lively atmosphere. The shakshuka brunch, charcoal-grilled meats, and creative mezze are all outstanding. Weekend brunch is particularly buzzing.

Best Pubs & Bars

  • 1. The Kernel Brewery

    Arch 11, Dockley Road Industrial Estate, SE16 3SF

    One of London's most respected craft breweries, open Saturday only for taproom sales. Eoin Walsh's beers — especially the pale ales, IPAs, and export stouts — are exceptional. Buy bottles to take home or drink fresh pints in the industrial arch setting.

  • 2. The Woolpack

    98 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3UB

    A friendly neighbourhood pub on Bermondsey Street with a good selection of craft beer, natural wine, and a relaxed atmosphere. The beer garden is a suntrap, and the pub quiz and live music nights add to the community feel.

  • 3. Anspach & Hobday Taproom

    118 Druid Street, SE1 2HH

    A railway arch taproom on the Beer Mile serving their excellent range of craft beers, including a superb porter, smoked brown, and seasonal IPAs. The industrial setting with wooden benches is quintessential Beer Mile drinking.

  • 4. The George Inn

    75-77 Borough High Street, SE1 1NH

    London's last surviving galleried coaching inn, owned by the National Trust. The timber-framed building dates to 1677 and Shakespeare is said to have performed in the courtyard. A remarkable historic pub just a short walk from Bermondsey Street.

  • 5. Brew By Numbers

    79 Enid Street, SE16 3RA

    A brewery and taproom at the eastern end of the Beer Mile with an excellent rotating selection of their own beers. The numbering system (each beer has a style and recipe number) makes it fun to explore their range. Great outdoor space on sunny days.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Watch House

    199 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3UW

    A specialty coffee shop in a converted 19th-century watch house (once used to guard against body snatchers in the adjacent churchyard). Outstanding coffee, excellent pastries, and one of the most characterful cafe spaces in south London.

  • 2. Fuckoffee (now Bermondsey Street Coffee)

    163 Bermondsey Street, SE1 3UW

    A tiny specialty coffee shop with a cult following for its excellent espresso and no-nonsense attitude. The space is minimal — a few stools and a bench — but the coffee quality is outstanding. A Bermondsey institution.

  • 3. St. John Bakery

    72 Druid Street, SE1 2EZ

    The bakery arm of the legendary St. John restaurant, selling sourdough bread, doughnuts (the custard and the salted caramel are legendary), pastries, and excellent coffee from a railway arch. The Saturday morning queue is a Bermondsey ritual.

Shopping Highlights

  • Maltby Street Market — Beyond the food stalls, the market and surrounding arches house artisan producers, vintage dealers, and independent makers.
  • Bermondsey Antiques Market — Friday mornings in Bermondsey Square. Serious collectors arrive at dawn, but there are finds to be had throughout the morning.
  • Bermondsey Street boutiques — Independent fashion, homewares, and design shops line this increasingly smart street, including Bermondsey 167 and various pop-ups.
  • Lassco — A vast architectural salvage warehouse at 41 Maltby Street selling reclaimed flooring, antique doors, industrial lighting, and vintage furniture.
  • Neal's Yard Dairy (Borough Market) — Just a short walk away, London's best cheese shop with an incredible selection of British and Irish artisan cheeses, many aged in their own Bermondsey arches.

Getting There

Bermondsey is well connected to central London:

  • Bermondsey (Jubilee line) — the main station, right on Bermondsey Street for the galleries, restaurants, and the start of the Beer Mile
  • London Bridge (Jubilee, Northern lines, National Rail) — for the western end of Bermondsey, Maltby Street Market, and Borough Market
  • South Bermondsey (National Rail, Southern) — for the eastern end of the Beer Mile, though less frequent service

Insider Tips

  • The Beer Mile is best on Saturdays when all the taprooms are open (typically noon to 6pm). Start at The Kernel or Southwark Brewing at the western end and work east. Pace yourself — there are a lot of stops.
  • Maltby Street Market is much smaller and more local than Borough Market. Saturday is the main day, with Sunday being quieter. Arrive between 10am and noon for the best experience.
  • Bermondsey's leather heritage is visible in the street names — Tanner Street, Morocco Street, Leathermarket Street — and in the Leathermarket area near Bermondsey Street.
  • Combine Bermondsey with a walk along the Thames Path from London Bridge, passing through the Shard Quarter and along Shad Thames (the atmospheric converted warehouse street) before reaching Bermondsey Street.
  • The Bermondsey Antiques Market on Friday mornings operates on a centuries-old law that supposedly allows goods to be sold without proof of provenance before dawn. While the law has changed, the early-morning tradition continues.
