South Bank Neighbourhood Guide

London's cultural mile — world-class art, Thames-side walks, and street performers along the river

Overview

The South Bank stretches along the southern shore of the Thames from Westminster Bridge to Tower Bridge, forming what is arguably the greatest concentration of cultural venues in Europe. This is where Londoners and visitors come for world-class art at Tate Modern, performances at the Southbank Centre and National Theatre, food at Borough Market, and one of the most iconic riverside walks anywhere.

The vibe: Cultural and alive. By day, the riverside promenade buzzes with joggers, families, skateboarders at the Undercroft, and buskers. Street performers, pop-up markets, and second-hand book stalls line the walkway. By evening, the illuminated bridges and buildings create one of London's most magical settings for a stroll.

Best for: Art lovers, families, photographers, theatre-goers, and anyone who wants to walk. The Thames Path here is completely flat and car-free, connecting a string of attractions that could fill an entire week. It is also ideal for a first-time visitor wanting to see many of London's highlights in a single walk.

Top Attractions

  • 1. Tate Modern

    Bankside, SE1 9TG

    One of the world's greatest modern and contemporary art museums, housed in the former Bankside Power Station. The permanent collection is free and spans works by Picasso, Warhol, Rothko, and Bourgeois. The Turbine Hall hosts massive commissioned installations. The tenth-floor viewing gallery offers spectacular free views of the London skyline.

  • 2. Southbank Centre

    Belvedere Road, SE1 8XX

    Europe's largest centre for the arts, comprising the Royal Festival Hall, Queen Elizabeth Hall, and the Hayward Gallery. Hosts classical music, jazz, literature festivals, and contemporary art exhibitions year-round. The brutalist architecture is iconic, and there is always something free happening in the foyers.

  • 3. Shakespeare's Globe

    21 New Globe Walk, SE1 9DT

    A faithful reconstruction of the Elizabethan playhouse where Shakespeare's plays were originally performed. Groundling tickets (standing in the open-air yard) cost just five pounds and are one of London's greatest cultural bargains. The exhibition and guided tours run year-round; performances April to October.

  • 4. Borough Market

    8 Southwark Street, SE1 1TL

    London's oldest food market, trading since 1756 (with roots going back to the 13th century). Over 100 stalls selling artisan cheese, fresh bread, charcuterie, oysters, cakes, and ready-to-eat food from around the world. Open Monday to Saturday, but the full market runs Wednesday to Saturday.

  • 5. London Eye

    Riverside Building, County Hall, SE1 7PB

    The iconic 135-metre observation wheel on the South Bank offers unrivalled 360-degree views across London. A full rotation takes roughly 30 minutes, and on a clear day you can see up to 40 kilometres. Book online in advance for cheaper tickets and shorter queues. Sunset and evening rides are particularly spectacular when the city lights up.

Best Restaurants

  • 1. Padella

    6 Southwark Street, SE1 1TQ • Fresh pasta • $

    Hand-rolled fresh pasta at astonishingly fair prices, right next to Borough Market. The pappardelle with eight-hour beef shin ragu and the cacio e pepe are extraordinary. No reservations, so join the queue (it moves fast). Most dishes under ten pounds.

  • 2. Anchor & Hope

    36 The Cut, SE1 8LP • British gastropub • $$

    A pioneering gastropub near the Old Vic theatre serving hearty, seasonal British cooking. The daily-changing menu might feature slow-roast lamb shoulder, devilled kidneys, or whole roast chicken for two. No reservations except for Sunday lunch. Get there when they open.

  • 3. Baltic

    74 Blackfriars Road, SE1 8HA • Eastern European • $$

    An elegant restaurant in a converted coach house serving Polish, Russian, and Eastern European cooking. Outstanding pierogi, borscht, and a vodka menu with over 70 varieties. The light-filled dining room with exposed timber beams is beautiful. Live jazz on weekends.

  • 4. El Pastor

    7A Stoney Street, SE1 9AA • Mexican • $$

    Proper Mexican tacos al pastor cooked on a trompo spit, alongside outstanding ceviche, tostadas, and mezcal cocktails. Underneath the Borough Market railway arches, the setting is atmospheric. Walk-ins only at the original location; arrive early for dinner.

  • 5. Arabica Bar & Kitchen

    3 Rochester Walk, SE1 9AF • Middle Eastern • $$

    A Borough Market gem serving Levantine meze, grills, and sharing plates. The hummus is exceptional, the lamb kofta smoky and tender, and the baklava a perfect finish. A bright, welcoming space tucked under the market's glass roof.

Best Bars & Pubs

  • 1. The Anchor Bankside

    34 Park Street, SE1 9EF

    A historic pub dating back to the 18th century with a riverside terrace offering views of St Paul's Cathedral and the Millennium Bridge. Samuel Johnson was a regular. Multiple rooms spread across several floors, each with its own character.

  • 2. The Old Thameside Inn

    Pickfords Wharf, Clink Street, SE1 9DG

    Set in a converted spice warehouse with a large outdoor terrace directly on the Thames Path. Excellent position between Tate Modern and Borough Market. A prime spot for a pint with a view of the river and the City skyline.

  • 3. Nine Lives

    8 Holyrood Street, SE1 2EL

    A sustainable cocktail bar near London Bridge that turns food waste into creative ingredients. The drinks are inventive and delicious, the space is intimate and candlelit, and the eco-conscious approach adds an extra layer of satisfaction. Excellent small plates menu too.

  • 4. The George Inn

    75-77 Borough High Street, SE1 1NH

    London's last surviving galleried coaching inn, now owned by the National Trust. Dating from 1677, the timber-framed building has a cobbled courtyard, multiple bars, and bags of historic atmosphere. Dickens drank here and mentioned it in Little Dorrit.

  • 5. Skylon

    Royal Festival Hall, Belvedere Road, SE1 8XX

    A stylish bar and restaurant on the third floor of the Royal Festival Hall with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the Thames. The cocktails are well made, the views are stunning, and it is a perfect pre-show or interval drink spot.

Best Cafes

  • 1. Monmouth Coffee at Borough Market

    2 Park Street, SE1 9AB

    One of London's original specialty coffee roasters, operating from this Borough Market shop since 2001. The single-origin filter coffee and espresso are superb. Small space with communal tables and a loyal queue of regulars every morning.

  • 2. Tate Modern Cafe (Level 1)

    Bankside, SE1 9TG

    The ground-level cafe in Tate Modern serves excellent coffee and pastries with views of the Thames through the vast windows. A calm, spacious spot to refuel between galleries without leaving the building.

  • 3. Konditor at Borough Market

    22 Stoney Street, SE1 9AD

    A German-inspired bakery and cafe producing outstanding cakes, brownies, and pastries. The curly whirly cake (chocolate and caramel) is a Borough Market legend. Sit upstairs overlooking the market stalls below.

Shopping Highlights

  • Borough Market — Beyond the food stalls, the market hosts specialist shops selling artisan olive oils, rare spices, handmade chocolate, and British farmhouse cheeses.
  • Southbank Centre Book Market — Open daily under Waterloo Bridge, with tables of second-hand and vintage books. A wonderful place to browse for bargain novels, art books, and London histories.
  • Tate Modern Shop — One of London's best museum shops with art books, prints, design objects, and creative gifts you will not find elsewhere.
  • Gabriel's Wharf — A small collection of independent shops and galleries between the OXO Tower and the Southbank Centre selling handmade jewellery, ceramics, and art prints.
  • OXO Tower Design Studios — The upper floors of the OXO Tower house working studios for contemporary designers and makers, open to the public, selling jewellery, fashion, and homeware.

Getting There

The South Bank stretches between several stations, so choose based on which end you want to start from:

  • Waterloo (Northern, Bakerloo, Jubilee, Waterloo & City lines) — the main station for the Southbank Centre, London Eye, and National Theatre
  • Southwark (Jubilee line) — closest to Tate Modern
  • London Bridge (Northern, Jubilee lines) — closest to Borough Market and the eastern end of the walk
  • Blackfriars (Circle, District, Thameslink) — has an exit on the south side of the river, right by Tate Modern

Insider Tips

  • Walk the entire South Bank from Westminster Bridge to Tower Bridge. It takes about 45 minutes without stops and passes virtually every major attraction. This is the single best walk in London.
  • The Tate Modern viewing gallery on the tenth floor is free and often less crowded than the Shard or London Eye. Visit at sunset for golden light over St Paul's Cathedral.
  • Borough Market is quietest on Wednesday and Thursday mornings. Saturday mornings are wonderful but extremely crowded. Avoid the lunchtime rush (12-2pm) if you want to browse comfortably.
  • Shakespeare's Globe groundling tickets (five pounds) go on sale the morning of performances. Standing in the open-air yard is the authentic Elizabethan experience and far more fun than the seats.
  • For the best evening atmosphere, walk the South Bank after dark when the bridges and buildings are illuminated. The stretch between the National Theatre and Tate Modern is particularly magical.
